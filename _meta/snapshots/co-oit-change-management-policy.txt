1. Purpose
The Governor's Office of Information Technology (OIT) is charged with managing and
supporting information technology for the executive branch. This includes the IT
infrastructure as well as the systems and applications that state agencies rely upon to serve
Colorado's residents, businesses, and/or visitors. Effective IT change management is integral
to provide a stable environment and minimize disruptions to customers and IT services.

2. Policy
OIT shall manage changes in a planned, predictable, and well-communicated manner that
minimizes unplanned downtime and unforeseen issues. All changes made to production
systems and applications managed or maintained by OIT shall be made in accordance with the
processes and procedures established by OIT's technical change management team and
documented in the Standard Operating Procedure as posted on OIT Plaza.

3. Applicability
This policy applies to all classified and non-classified employees within OIT including full or
part-time permanent and temporary employees, independent contractors, volunteers, and
interns, regardless of physical work location.

5. Definitions
   ●   ServiceHub: The Enterprise Change Management tool of record used for the tracking
       and approval of all changes in the production environment.
   ●   Change: The addition, modification, or removal of approved, supported, or baselined
       hardware, network, software, application, environment, or systems.
   ●   IT Change Management (CM): Refers to the formal process for making controlled
       changes to IT services and systems in a production environment.
   ●   CM Users: All classified and non-classified employees within OIT including full or part-
       time permanent and temporary employees, independent contractors, volunteers, and
       interns who have access to the Change module in the CA tool.
   ●   ITIL: Information Technology Infrastructure Library.
   ●   Pending: An RFC is in the approval process, but is not yet Approved for
       implementation.
   ●   Priority 1 Incident: An incident ticket tracked within ServiceHub deemed to be the
       highest priority based upon the established Service Desk Priority Matrix.
   ●   Rejected: Request for Change has been denied and will not proceed.
   ●   Request for Change (RFC): A request that is submitted for desired changes to an
       Information Technology Service environment using ServiceHub, also known as a Change
       Request.
   ●   Risk/Impact: The assessed threat to Production availability of any service involved or
       impacted by a Change. Risk and impact are determined based on an assessment
       completed during the RFC submission process.
   ●   Rollback: Any action in which all or part of a change is reverted to the previous state
       as a result of unforeseen problems or circumstances.
   ●   Rollback Plan: Documented procedure for rolling back all or part of any change
       activity, including risks, dependencies, and required timeframe. This is appended to
       the RFC and may be a requirement for approval.
   ●   Test Plan/Result: Documented evidence of proper testing, planning, and due diligence
       performed prior to submission of an RFC. This is appended to the RFC and may be a
       requirement for approval.
   ●   Unauthorized Change: Any change that proceeds or is implemented without following
       the IT Change Management process; any Change implemented without all necessary
       levels of approval will be considered an Unauthorized Change.
   ●   Violation: Any deviation from established policy or procedure will result in a Change
       violation and may prevent the implementation of the proposed change.

6. Standards
This policy applies to changes to a service or system that is in production in the OIT or
customer agency environment. It does not replace the project management process, though it
may be one step in that process.
OIT's IT Change Management process is, and shall continue to be, based on ITIL best practices.

7. Responsibilities
Before implementing a change, all Users are required to follow the Enterprise IT Change
Management Standard Operating Procedure CCO-SOP-100.
Roles and responsibilities are further defined in the Enterprise IT Change Management
Standard Operating Procedure CCO-SOP-100.

8. Compliance
Failure to comply with this policy may result in corrective action or disciplinary action, up to
and including termination, as follows:
1st Violation
   ●   Loss of access to the Change module with the ITSM Tool. Access will not be reinstated
       until the user undergoes IT Change Management training again.
   ●   Email sent to the user's immediate supervisor and Director over the user's team.
2nd Violation
   ●   Loss of access to the Change module with the ITSM Tool. This access will not be
       reinstated until the user undergoes IT Change Management training again.
   ●   Referral to the immediate supervisor for progressive corrective or disciplinary actions.
   ●   Email sent to the user's immediate supervisor, Director, Executive staff member over
       the user's team, and Human Resources.
Additional Violations
   ●   May result in additional corrective or disciplinary action as determined by the user's
       supervisor and Human Resources, up to and including termination of employment.
