                                                                        NPV Portfolio Prioritization

Using Net Present Value to
Prioritize State Information
Technology Projects
A practical guide for comparing state IT investments when funding and resources are limited.

This guide answers two related questions: How should Net Present Value (NPV) be calculated for
state IT project analysis, and how should agencies compare projects consistently when only one
or a few can be funded?

Applicability. This guide does not determine whether a Schedule IV-B is required. Agencies must
follow the current annual Legislative Budget Request (LBR) instructions. For the current budget
cycle, a Schedule IV-B is generally required for an IT funding request with a total estimated
project cost of $1 million or more, subject to the exceptions listed in those instructions. Section
216.023(4)(a)10, Florida Statutes, imposes additional governance/policy information
requirements for IT projects exceeding $10 million.

Key principle: NPV is one decision input, not the sole approval test for a public-sector investment.

A Balanced Portfolio Selection Method

Use the same documented process for every project so the decision is consistent, transparent,
and auditable.

1.      Gate mandatory projects. Identify statutory, regulatory, security, legislative, and critical
        operational work first and treat the mandate as a gating factor. Confirm the legal
        deadline, minimum compliant scope, consequences of noncompliance, available
        funding, and feasible alternatives. A mandate does not eliminate affordability,
        procurement, risk, or feasibility analysis.

2.      Score discretionary projects. Use approved criteria and weights to compare strategic
        alignment, expected benefits and public value, risk reduction, customer impact, technical
        debt, delivery confidence, urgency, and organizational readiness.

3.      Evaluate financial measures separately. For Schedule IV-B projects, use the Appendix A
        outputs: Return on Investment (ROI), Payback Period, Breakeven Fiscal Year, Net Present
        Value (NPV), and Internal Rate of Return (IRR). Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) may be used as a
        supplemental measure, but it is not an Appendix A output. Consider financial results
        alongside - not inside - the non-financial weighted score to avoid double-counting
        financial value.

Weighted score = Σ [(rating ÷ 5) × weight]. Use the 1-5 evidence-based portfolio rating scale
described in this guide. When weights are entered as percentage points (for example, 25 rather
than 0.25), the maximum total score is 100. A rating of 4 on a 25% criterion contributes 20 points:
(4 ÷ 5) × 25 = 20.

Important: This illustrative model intentionally excludes financial measures, so they are not
counted twice. This scoring model is recommended portfolio-management guidance and is not
prescribed by Florida statute, Rule Chapter 60GG-1, or Schedule IV-B. Agencies should tailor the
non-financial criteria and weights to statutory responsibilities, strategic priorities, risk tolerance,
and governance requirements.

Applying the 1-5 Portfolio Rating Scale

The rating scale is intended to reduce guesswork and make scoring repeatable. Assign the rating
that best matches the documented evidence for each criterion; do not score based on
preference, advocacy, or an unsupported expectation of future performance.

Rating Guidance

    •    Use criterion-specific evidence. Strategic alignment may rely on approved plans or
         mandates; benefits and public value should rely on measurable outcomes. For Schedule
         IV-B projects, focus the benefits/public value score on benefits not already monetized in
         Appendix A, or document how overlap is prevented. Risk reduction should rely on
         identified risks and expected exposure reduction; customer impact should rely on service
         data or user research; urgency, readiness, and delivery confidence should rely on
         documented deadlines, dependencies, capacity, and implementation evidence.

    •    Rate the current proposal, not the hoped-for future state. If evidence is not yet available,
         score conservatively and document what would be needed to justify a higher rating.

    •    Use the same anchors for every project in the same decision cycle. Do not change the
         meaning of a 3 or 4 from one project to another.

Recommended approach: Identify mandatory work first and treat the mandate as a gating factor;
assess the legal deadline, minimum compliant scope, consequences of noncompliance,
available funding, and feasible alternatives. Rank discretionary projects with an approved non-
financial weighted model, then combine the weighted score with Schedule IV-B financial outputs
and feasibility constraints such as dependencies, affordability, and resource availability to
support the final governance decision.

How Do We Choose One Project When Funding Is Limited?

When multiple discretionary projects compete for limited funding, do not select a project based
solely on NPV, IRR, or another single financial measure. Apply the same documented evaluation
method to each alternative so the decision is consistent, transparent, repeatable, and auditable.

Recommended Decision Rule

   1. Identify mandatory projects first. Statutory, regulatory, security, legislative, or critical
       operational requirements should be addressed before discretionary projects.
   2. Calculate financial value consistently. For Schedule IV-B projects, use the current
       Appendix A CBA workbook to calculate ROI, Payback Period, Breakeven Fiscal Year, NPV,
       and IRR using the workbook's prescribed assumptions. BCR may be used as a
       supplemental measure but is not an Appendix A output.
   3. Score non-financial value. Evaluate strategic alignment, benefits and public value, risk
       reduction, customer/service impact, technical debt, delivery confidence, urgency, and
       organizational readiness using the approved scoring criteria and evidence.

    4. Apply an approved non-financial weighted scoring model. Use the 1-5 evidence-based
        portfolio rating scale and predetermined weights. Require a documented rationale for
        each rating and retain the scoring record.
    5. Use financial measures as separate decision inputs, not the sole decision rule. A project
        with the highest IRR may not create the greatest NPV, while a project with the highest NPV
        may not provide the greatest overall public value.
    6. Document the decision. Record assumptions, Appendix A financial results, scoring criteria
        and weights, evidence supporting ratings, feasibility and risk considerations, resource
        constraints, approvals, and the rationale for selecting the project.

Bottom line: The decision question is not simply “Which project has the highest financial return?”
It is “Which feasible project provides the greatest overall value to the organization and the public,
based on consistently applied financial and non-financial criteria?”

Minimum Decision Record

    •   Alternatives and baseline; cash-flow sources and classifications; current Appendix A CBA
        outputs (Schedule IV-B ROI, Payback Period, Breakeven Fiscal Year, NPV, and IRR); cost-of-
        capital assumptions; sensitivity results; and supplemental BCR if used.

    •   Scoring criteria, weights, 1-5 portfolio ratings, supporting evidence, reviewer/approval
        record, and any scoring calibration notes.

    •   Selection rationale, conditions, dependencies, resource constraints, and accountable
        benefit owner.

Three-Project Example: If You Can Fund Only One

Use the same analysis for all three projects. The highest IRR does not automatically win; compare
present-value financial results alongside the approved non-financial portfolio score and
feasibility.

Example: Project A has a $10M NPV and 100% IRR; Project B has a $3M NPV and 150% IRR; Project
C has a $10M NPV and 50% IRR. IRR alone would favor B, while NPV alone would show A and C as
financially stronger in present-value dollars. The weighted non-financial score and governance
