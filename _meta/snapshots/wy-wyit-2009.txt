Scope:
This policy establishes workspace-level document security requirements that operate within the broader
framework of WyIT 3013, Physical and Environmental Protection.

Specifically, this policy defines minimum requirements for agencies to maintain a "clean desk" – where
internal, confidential, and sensitive information, regardless of format, structure, or formal asset
designation, is stored away and out of sight when not in use or when the workspace is vacant.

This policy addresses the following key areas:

   ●​ Physical security controls for confidential and sensitive documents in individual workspaces;
   ●​ Proper storage, handling, and disposal procedures for non-electronic sensitive materials;
   ●​ Integration with electronic information security requirements under WyIT 3001, General Security
       - Acceptable Use;
   ●​ Special considerations for highly secured areas handling criminal justice or health data;
   ●​ Clear desk protocols to prevent unauthorized visual access to sensitive information;
   ●​ Coordination with facility-level physical security measures under WyIT 3013, Physical and
       Environmental Protection.

This policy serves as the state baseline. Agencies may implement more restrictive policies to meet
applicable state and federal regulations or agency-specific requirements, provided they do not deviate
substantially from this policy. In lieu of agency-specific policies, this policy shall serve as the governing
standard.

      3.1.​ Highly Secured Areas with stringent access controls, such as those inspected and

              [2] , [5], [6]

4.​   NON-ELECTRONIC CONFIDENTIAL AND SENSITIVE INFORMATION
       All non-electronic sensitive and confidential information must be handled as follows:​

      4.1.​   When working in a private office, if left unattended or at the end of the workday,
              non-electronic confidential and sensitive information shall be secured behind the locked
              door of the office.​
              [2], [3]​

      4.2.​   When working in an open office environment, if left unattended for short periods,
              non-electronic confidential and sensitive information must be stored out of sight (i.e., in a
              drawer or filing cabinet). Any sensitive documents unattended for extended periods or at
              the end of the workday must be securely locked away.
              [2]

      4.3.​   Non-electronic confidential and sensitive information should not be sent to unsecured
              printers or fax machines and must be promptly removed if inadvertently printed.
              [2]
      4.4.​   Non-electronic confidential and sensitive information shall be shredded in official
              shredder bins or placed in a locked disposal bin for proper discarding.
              [2]​

      4.5.​   Whiteboards containing sensitive information shall be erased or secured from view when
              not actively being used.
              [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [3]

       4.6.​   If stored in a filing cabinet, the cabinet must be secure and keys protected and never left
               unattended.
               [2]

       4.7.​   Mass storage devices (i.e. loose hard drives, CDROMs, DVDs, or USB drives) must be
               secured, when unoccupied and at the end of the workday.

Compliance & Exceptions:
Compliance
Employees who violate this policy may be subject to appropriate disciplinary action up to and including
discharge as well as both civil and criminal penalties. Non-employees, including, without limitation,
contractors, may be subject to termination of contractual agreements, denial of access to IT resources,
and other actions as well as both civil and criminal penalties.

Exceptions
Requests for exceptions to this policy shall be reviewed by the Chief Information Security Officer
(CISO) and the Chief Information Officer (CIO). Departments requesting exceptions shall provide such
requests to the CIO in accordance with WyIT.3002 Policy Exception Request.

                                                    4

                                                        ​

The request should specifically state the scope of the exception along with justification for granting the
exception, the potential impact or risk attendant upon granting the exception, risk mitigation measures
to be undertaken by the IT Department, initiatives, actions and a time-frame for achieving the minimum
compliance level with the policies set forth herein. The CIO shall review such requests; confer with the
requesting department.

Any exception to established policy shall be defined and tracked as a risk to the state and established
mitigating controls monitored by the agency.
