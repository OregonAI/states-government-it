1. Purpose
This procedure outlines the process, requirements and responsibilities for the delegation of
authority of certain information technology (IT) functions from the Governor's Office of
Information Technology (OIT) to another state agency.

2. Applicability
This procedure applies to state agencies as defined in C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(28) as “all of the
departments, divisions, commissions, boards, bureaus, and institutions in the executive
branch of the state government” — not including the legislative or judicial departments, the
department of education, the department of law, the department of state, the department of
the treasury, or state-supported institutions of higher education.

Requirements
For delegation of authority requests to be considered, OIT requires confirmation that the
agency can fulfill the following requirements:
   ●      The state agency has requested that the function be delegated and the Agency's
          Executive Director supports the request.

   ●   The state agency has the necessary resources to perform or control the function
       requesting to be delegated.
   ●   The IT function being requested for delegation is a unique or mission-critical function
       of the state agency requesting delegation of authority.
   ●   The delegation of authority results in net cost savings or improved service delivery to
       the state as a whole, or for the unique, mission-critical function of the state agency.
   ●   The IT function is not otherwise provided in OIT's oversight and standards governance
       developed pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-105(4).

3. Process
Submissions
   ●   The agency's Information Technology Director (ITD) needs to receive a written request
       for delegation of authority from the Agency Executive Director.
   ●   All requests must be submitted using the OIT Delegation of Authority Request Form.
       The form will be received and processed by OIT.
   ●   OIT's Chief Information Officer and Executive Director (CIO) will make a decision to
       deny, approve to proceed, or seek additional information regarding a delegation of
       authority request within 30 calendar days of receipt of the full submission (written
       request plus completed form), unless the request requires expedited approval in
       support of a declared emergency or matters related to public safety pursuant to C.R.S.
       24-33.5-704.

Potential Outcomes
After consideration of a request, the CIO will make one of the following decisions and notify
the state agency submitting the request:
   ●   Denied: OIT will summarize why the request is being denied and cite the specific
       instances where the request fails to meet the requirements set forth in this procedure
       and/or C.R.S. 24-37.5-105.4.
   ●   Approval to Proceed: OIT will indicate the request has approval to proceed and initiate
       negotiations and the execution of the delegation of authority interagency agreement.

Disputes
In the event a dispute arises between the state agency and OIT in relation to the request,
including a denial of the request:
   ●   The CIO and the Agency's Executive Director will attempt to resolve the dispute.
   ●   If the CIO and the Agency's Executive Director are unable to resolve the dispute within
       10 business days from the date the dispute arises, the dispute will be referred to the
       Governor for final resolution.

Execution of Delegation of Authority Interagency Agreement
The delegation of authority interagency agreement explicitly delegates authority of the
requested IT function to the agency and shifts full responsibility, including an
acknowledgment by the agency that it has assumed responsibility for ensuring ongoing
compliance with state and federal IT policies and standards pursuant to section 24-37.5-
105(4), and all liability associated with the delegated IT function. Pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-
105.4(3), upon an approval-to-proceed determination:

   ●   OIT and the requesting agency representatives will negotiate and execute a written
       delegation of authority interagency agreement that must contain: a precise definition
       of the IT function to be delegated; a clear description of the standards to be met in
       performing the delegated function; designation of the state agency responsible for
       ensuring operational security and validating compliance with state and federal security
       policies and standards; a provision for periodic administrative audits by OIT; the date
       on which the agreement terminates; and designation of the appointed authority
       responsible for the delegated services, including rates to be charged for staff, if any.
   ●   OIT will distribute the signed contract to the Agency Executive Director, ITD, and OIT
       Procurement.

Negotiation of Delegation of Authority Interagency Agreement
OIT maintains an interagency agreement framework for delegation of authority. Using that
framework, OIT and the requesting agency will negotiate necessary details and execute the
agreement. If OIT and the requesting agency are unable to come to an agreement, approval
to proceed will be revoked.

Renewal of Delegation of Authority Interagency Agreement
Six months prior to expiration of an existing agreement, the renewal process begins:
   ●   The ITD will alert the agency that the agreement will expire in six months and ask
       whether the agency intends to request renewal.
   ●   The ITD will meet with the agency to discuss any changes to the agreement and submit
       the renewal form.
   ●   OIT will review the renewal form following the same process as the initial request.

Annual Review and Administrative Audits
OIT will conduct an annual review and complete additional administrative audits as needed
with the agency to assess compliance with the delegation of authority interagency
agreement. If needed, OIT will terminate an agreement if an annual review or administrative
audit reveals a lack of compliance by the state agency, pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-105.4(4).

4. Definitions and Descriptions
The following definitions and descriptions are for the sole purpose of the delegation of
authority policy and procedure.

Titles and Positions
   ●   State Chief Information Officer (CIO): The State Chief Information Officer appointed
       pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-103.
   ●   Executive Director: The executive director of the executive branch agency requesting
       delegation of authority.
   ●   State Auditor: The Colorado State Auditor appointed pursuant to C.R.S. 2-3-102.
   ●   Information Technology Director (ITD): Senior technology staff member serving as a
       liaison between their assigned customer agency and OIT.

Unique or Mission Critical Functions of State Agencies
These functions cannot be interrupted for 12 hours or must be resumed within 30 days. They
are organization-level functions that must be continued throughout, or resumed rapidly after,
a disruption of normal activities, where any interruption or potential loss of data could be
                                                                                      Page 3 of 6

detrimental to an organization's operations or statutory responsibilities. They require real-
time or constant action to ensure an agency's operations or authorities, are typically
monitored and/or staffed 24/7, and rely on command centers or other central operations
facilities.

Necessary Resources to Perform Information Technology Functions
Documentation must be provided demonstrating that the requesting agency has all necessary
resources specified below:
   ●   Staff: The requesting agency must already have in place the necessary and/or
       appropriated FTE to perform and fully administer the function being requested,
       including contracts, procurement, finance, accounting, human resources, operations,
       and management staff. OIT employees whose work is not solely attributed to the
       function being requested cannot be used to determine if the agency has the necessary
       resources.
   ●   Financial: The requesting agency must already have direct authority or responsibility
       for funding designated by federal, state, or local level appropriations.
   ●   Audit Findings: The agency must not have unimplemented critical findings or
       deficiencies related to the function being delegated that resulted from performance,
       financial, and/or IT audits performed by the State Auditor.

Additional Definitions
   ●   Net Cost Savings: Documentation, using qualitative and quantitative metrics,
       demonstrating the current cost services model compared to a potential cost services
       model, with clear and convincing evidence that savings cannot be achieved under the
       current supervision or structure. Net cost savings will be verified by OIT's Chief
       Financial Officer.
   ●   Improved Service Delivery: Documentation, using qualitative and quantitative metrics,
       demonstrating the current service delivery model and environment compared to a
       potential model, with clear and convincing evidence of improved service delivery
       potential.
   ●   Delegation of Authority Request Form: The form provided by OIT to agencies that want
       to make a request.
   ●   Delegation of Authority Interagency Agreement: An agreement between two or more
       state agencies pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-105.4(3) (delegation of authority) and 1 CCR
       101-1-3-5 (interagency agreements), including any exhibits and other necessary
       details.

5. Responsibilities
State Agency Responsibilities
   ●   Agencies must not implement plans or make strategic decisions in isolation on any IT
       function until an executed delegation of authority interagency agreement is in place.
   ●   Agencies must not take action or make public communications that would otherwise
       impact OIT's ability to review and make a determination for delegation of authority
       requests.
   ●   Agencies must coordinate with OIT before submitting budget decision items, budget
       amendments, supplemental budget requests, and legislative proposals that derive
       from a delegation of authority request pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-105.4.

                                                                                     Page 4 of 6

   ●   Agencies with delegated authority accept the role and responsibility to report and
       respond to the Joint Technology Committee and other legislative committees, boards,
       or commissions.
   ●   Agencies with delegated authority must ensure agency compliance with all laws, OIT's
       Colorado Information Security Policies, the Statewide GenAI Policy and any successor
       or related AI policies, and the terms of the executed delegation of authority
       interagency agreement.
   ●   Agencies with delegated authority must designate a point of contact responsible for
       responding to OIT inquiries and providing OIT with access to any reports, data,
       contracts, agreements, or other documentation used to execute the delegated
       function.

OIT Responsibilities
   ●   Establish and maintain clear procedures for handling delegation of authority requests.
   ●   Provide a framework for the delegation of authority interagency agreement.
   ●   Under the authority of the State Chief Information Officer, evaluate delegation of
       authority requests and supporting documentation in line with statutory requirements
       to determine denial or approval to proceed.
   ●   Ensure clear communication at all times on issues regarding delegation of authority
       requests.
   ●   Review the OIT Delegation of Authority Policy and this procedure every 12 months.
   ●   Annually review existing delegation of authority interagency agreements with the
       agency.
   ●   Review and perform periodic administrative audits of delegated functions with
       agencies as necessary.
   ●   Facilitate the review of existing delegation of authority interagency agreements
       following the beginning of a Governor's new term in office.
   ●   Terminate a delegation of authority interagency agreement if an administrative audit
       reveals a lack of compliance by the state agency, pursuant to C.R.S. 24-37.5-105.4(4).
   ●   Report annually to the Joint Technology Committee and other appropriate entities of
       the Colorado General Assembly regarding IT functions that have delegated authority.

6. Compliance
Failure to follow this procedure and comply with the associated OIT Delegation of Authority
Policy will result in automatic denial of a request and may risk placing the requesting agency,
OIT and/or the State out of statutory and/or legal compliance.
