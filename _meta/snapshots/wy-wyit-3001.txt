           GENERAL SECURITY - ACCEPTABLE USE
                                          WyIT 3001

PURPOSE: The purpose of this policy is to outline the expectations of all Executive
Branch agencies, boards, and commissions regarding the acceptable use of IT resources
by authorized users and to establish the parameters for the use of IT resources.
Additionally, this policy ensures the acceptable use of information systems, electronic
and computing devices, and network resources to conduct State business, whether
owned or leased by the State, its agencies, or a third party (aka Affiliates). All employees,
contractors, vendors, and/or affiliates of the State of Wyoming are responsible for
exercising good judgment regarding the appropriate use of information systems,
electronic devices, and network resources. The State requires that these systems be
used responsibly and in compliance with all State legislation and policies.

 I.​ APPLICABILITY

     This policy applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions staff
     (collectively referred to as “agencies”). Additionally, this policy is also applicable to
     consultants, affiliates, and temporary employees.

II.​ DEFINITIONS

     All definitions for this policy are located in the Policy Dictionary.

III.​ EXCEPTIONS

     Any exceptions provided to the following policy must abide by the WyIT - 3002 -
     Security Exception Request policy.

IV.​   ACCEPTABLE USE OF ASSETS

       ●​ Assets include, but are not limited to, physical equipment, such as desktop
          computers, servers, printers, laptops, telephones, mobile devices, and
          removable media (such as USB flash drives), as well as systems and services,
          such as the organizational network, internet, and voicemail. Organizational
          data is also an asset. All devices and systems are property of the State, and all
          use must be in accordance with its policies, standards, and guidelines.

         ○​ The CIO allows limited use of the network, systems, and devices for
            personal reasons, but personal use must not be abused. Occasional and
            incidental personal use is permitted, provided that it is limited to the
            following considerations:
              ■​ Is otherwise consistent with all State policies.
              ■​ Is limited in amount and duration.
              ■​ Does not impede the ability of the individual or other users to fulfill the
                  State’s responsibilities and duties, including but not limited to
                  extensive bandwidth, resource, or storage utilization.
              ■​ Is not being utilized during travel that is deemed personal and not
                  work related.

         ○​ Exercising good judgment regarding occasional and incidental personal
            use is important. The CIO may revoke or limit this privilege at any time.
         ○​ Users shall not access and/or purchase technology, devices, applications,
            or services that are not formally authorized and approved by the CIO
            procurement review process.
         ○​ IT assets, such as laptops and mobile devices, are intended to be used only
            by the people to whom they have been issued. The person to whom the
            device was issued is ultimately responsible for any actions performed with
            the device.
         ○​ Users will protect all State assets at all times, keeping them physically and
            logically secured and under the control of the user, including but not limited
            to:

             ■​ Ensuring the device is locked (screen/keyboard) when unattended by
                the authorized user.

         ○​ Access to State systems and devices is controlled through individual

                                                                            WyIT - 3001        Page 2

            accounts and passwords based on roles and responsibilities assigned.
         ○​ All voicemail boxes will be protected with a PIN (personal identification
            number). PINs must be changed every 60-days to aid in mailbox security.
            Easy-to-guess or previously used PINs will be blocked by the system. PINs
            must not be shared with others.
         ○​ Removable media, such as USB flash drives, etc., may be used with the
            following requirements:

             ■​ Information shall only be stored on removable media when required in
                the performance of the user’s role (e.g., USB shared between two
                employees during a conference).
             ■​ Removable storage devices, including but not limited to USB drives,
                shall be encrypted.
             ■​ Personally owned mobile devices (e.g., smartphones and tablets) are
                not permitted to be used as removable media to store or transfer any
                state-owned data.
             ■​ Any unknown removable media that is found unattended must be
                turned in to the CIO Service Desk and NOT inserted into any State
                issued device.
             ■​ End users are to take reasonable measures to secure removable media
                (e.g., storing it in a secure/locked location when not in use, not sharing it
                with unauthorized users, etc.).
             ■​ Upon completion of the assigned duties, all data shall be deleted, in
                accordance with NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1. Guidelines for Media
                Sanitization, from the removable media.​

V.​   UNACCEPTABLE USES

      The following list is not exhaustive, but is an attempt to provide a framework for
      activities that constitute unacceptable use. Users, however, may be exempted
      from one or more of these restrictions during their authorized job responsibilities,
      after approval from the CIO.

      ●​ Unacceptable use includes, but is not limited to, the following:
         ○​ Sharing account passwords, Personal Identification Numbers (PINs),
            security questions/answers, security tokens (e.g., smartcard, key fob), or
            similar information or devices used for authentication and authorization to
            State information assets.
         ○​ Engaging in the subversion of existing security controls (e.g., network,

                                                                             WyIT - 3001        Page 3

              computer, or device hacking and scanning, password cracking,
              penetration testing, conducting red team exercises, etc.).
           ○​ Purposely introducing malicious programs into the network or server (e.g.,
              malware, viruses, worms, trojan horses, etc.).
           ○​ Distributing,     transmitting, posting, or storing any electronic
              communications, material, or correspondence that is threatening, obscene,
              harassing,      pornographic,       offensive,    defamatory,  discriminatory,
              inflammatory, illegal, or intentionally false or inaccurate.
           ○​ Adding or installing personal information technology resources (e.g.,
              wireless access points, software, mobile devices, etc.) to existing State
              information systems.
           ○​ Purposely using, acquiring, reproducing, transmitting, or distributing any
              information, software, or other electronic materials (e.g., movies, music) in
              violation of applicable copyright, trademark, intellectual property laws, and
              contractual agreements.
           ○​ Installing, downloading, or running software that has not been approved
              by the CIO.
           ○​ Using an organization’s IT resources to circulate unauthorized solicitations
              or advertisements for non-organizational purposes, including religious,
              political, or not-for-profit organizations.
           ○​ Providing unauthorized affiliates access to the State’s technology
              resources that have not been designated as “guest.”
           ○​ Using State technology, information, or resources for commercial or
              personal purposes, in support of "for-profit" activities, or in support of
              other outside employment or business activity (e.g., consulting for pay,
              business transactions).
           ○​ Propagating chain letters, fraudulent mass mailings, spam, or other types
              of undesirable and unwanted email content using organizational IT
              resources.

VI.​ ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATION AND INTERNET USE​
     ​
     The use of the State’s communication and internet systems and services (including
     but not limited to email, instant messaging, voicemail, forums, and social media) is
     provided in order to perform regular job duties. The use is a privilege and must be
     used with respect, common sense, and in accordance with the following
     requirements:

                                                                            WyIT - 3001        Page 4

●​ The email systems and other messaging services used at the State are owned
   by the State and are, therefore, its property. This gives the State the right to
   monitor any and all email traffic passing through its email system. This
   monitoring may include, viewing of emails during the course of investigating
   cybersecurity incidents related to email abuse, phishing or hacking, inadvertent
   reading by IT staff during the normal course of managing the email system,
   review by human resources and legal team during the email discovery phase of
   litigation, and observation by management in cases of suspected abuse or
   employee inefficiency.
●​ The State often delivers official communications via email. Employees of the
   State with email accounts are expected to check their email consistently to be
   aware of important State announcements and updates, and for fulfilling
   business and role-oriented tasks.
●​ Electronic communication and the internet must not be used for illegal or
   unlawful purposes, including, but not limited to, copyright infringement,
   obscenity, libel, slander, fraud, defamation, plagiarism, harassment (including
   offensive and/or insulting content), discrimination, intimidation, forgery,
   impersonation, illegal gambling, soliciting for illegal pyramid schemes, and
   computer tampering (e.g. spreading computer viruses).
●​ State communication platforms and the internet are not to be used for purposes
   that could reasonably be expected to strain storage or bandwidth (e.g., emailing
   large attachments instead of pointing to a location on a shared drive). Individual
   use of resources will not interfere with others’ use of State email systems and
   services.
●​ Users are prohibited from using accounts that do not belong to them and are
   prohibited from using platforms to impersonate others.
●​ Users are not to give the impression that they are representing or providing
   opinions on behalf of the State unless otherwise authorized.
●​ Users shall not open message attachments or click on hyperlinks sent from
   unknown or unsigned sources through any platform (email, instant message,
   social media, etc.). Attachments/links are the primary source of malware and
   social engineering and shall be treated with utmost caution.
●​ Any allegations of misuse shall be promptly reported to the CIO Service Desk. If
   you receive an offensive or suspicious email, do not forward, delete, or reply to
   the message. Instead, report it directly to the CIO Service Desk.
●​ Email users are responsible for mailbox management, including organization and
   cleaning. If a user subscribes to a mailing list, they must be aware of how to
   unsubscribe from the list and are responsible for doing so in the event that their

                                                                      WyIT - 3001       Page 5

           current email address changes.
        ●​ Archival and backup copies of email messages exist, despite end-user deletion,
           in compliance with the State of Wyoming Records Retention Policy.
        ●​ Email access will be terminated when the employee or affiliate terminates their
           association with the State. The State is under no obligation to store or forward
           the contents of an individual’s email inbox/outbox after the term of their
           employment has ceased.
        ●​ Users shall not send sensitive information that is not appropriately protected
           (encrypted). Appropriate means of protection include but are not limited to
           Google Drive or sending encrypted email standards.
        ●​ Users shall take extra precautions when transmitting State, stakeholder, and/or
           other regulated information via electronic communications. Sensitive material
           shall be marked and encrypted appropriately. Keep in mind that all email
           messages sent outside of the State become the property of the receiver.
        ●​ Users are not permitted to automatically forward emails received by their State
           account to a non-state-owned email address or other messaging system.
        ●​ Email users are expected to remember that emails sent from the State’s email
           accounts reflect on the State. Please comply with normal standards of
           professional and personal courtesy and conduct.
        ●​ Users are permitted to remotely access the state network while offsite. All users
           who remotely access State IT resources shall ensure compliance with the
           Remote Access policy (WyIT-3017 - Remote Access).​

VII.​    SOCIAL MEDIA​
         ​
         State social media accounts are permitted to be used for business purposes only.
         These purposes include building a positive brand image, providing stakeholder
         support, monitoring public opinion, and professional networking. The following
         requirements are imposed for the appropriate use of social media:

          ●​ Access to social media will be open to staff who have received approval from
             their agency director. Approval will be provided given a legitimate business
             function.
          ●​ All actions and communications through social media must adhere to all
             previously defined acceptable uses of electronic communications.
          ●​ The use of personal social media accounts and user IDs for State use is
             prohibited.
          ●​ The use of State social media user IDs for personal use is prohibited.​

VIII.​   DATA SECURITY​
         ​
         Maintaining the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of organizational data is
         paramount to the security and success of the organization. The following
         requirements are defined to keep data secure and handled appropriately:​

         ●​ All organizational data is owned by the State, and all users are responsible for
            appropriately respecting and protecting all data.
         ●​ Users must keep all data secure by taking sensible precautions and following
            the requirements defined in the WyIT - 2001 Information Classification policy
            and the data-handling requirements defined in the WyIT - S - 2001 Information
            Classification Standard. This standard outlines the requirements for creating,
            using, storing, transmitting, archiving, and destroying data.
         ●​ Data must be classified based on sensitivity, as defined in the WyIT - 2001
            Information Classification policy. Data must be classified as Public, Internal, or
            Confidential. Data at each classification level must be safeguarded and
            handled appropriately in accordance with the WyIT - S - 2001 Information
            Classification Standard.
         ●​ Users may not view, copy, alter, or destroy data, software, documentation, or
            data communications belonging to the State or another individual without
            authorized permission.
         ●​ Users will only access data provided to them for duties in connection with
            their employment or engagement and in accordance with their terms and
            conditions of employment or equivalent.
         ●​ Extraction, manipulation, and reporting of State data must be done for
            business purposes only.
         ●​ Personal use of organizational data, including derived data, in any format and
            at any location, is prohibited.
         ●​ Users will follow all State sanctioned data removal procedures to permanently
            erase data from devices once its use is no longer required, as defined in WyIT
            - S - 2001 Information Classification Standard. Data must be retained for the
            length of time defined in accordance with the State of Wyoming Archivist
            Retention and Disposition requirements.

IX.​   MOBILE DEVICE USE​

       ●​ Personal mobile devices are not authorized to be accessed or used for state
          business.
       ●​ It is the responsibility of any employee who uses state-issued mobile device(s)
          to ensure that all security protocols normally used in the management of data
          on conventional storage infrastructure are also applied. It is imperative that any
          mobile device that is used to conduct State business be used appropriately,
          responsibly, and ethically.
       ●​ The CIO reserves the right to refuse, by physical and non-physical means, the
          ability to connect mobile devices to State and state-connected infrastructure.
          The CIO will engage in such action if such equipment is being used in a way
          that puts the State’s systems, data, users, and clients at risk.
       ●​ All authorized mobile devices used for access to State systems and/or data
          (such as email) must be protected by strong access control (e.g., complex
          passwords and multi-factor authentication). Employees must never disclose
          their passwords to anyone, even to family members, if work is conducted from
          a mobile device.
       ●​ All users of mobile devices must employ reasonable physical security
          measures. All users are expected to secure all such devices whether or not
          they are actually in use and/or being carried.
       ●​ No non-state computers are authorized to synchronize or back up data of
          state-issued mobile devices.
       ●​ Sensitive data and passwords must not be stored, without using the State
          authorized encryption solution, on the state-owned mobile devices.
       ●​ In the event of a lost or stolen State mobile device, it is incumbent on the user
          to report the incident to the CIO Service Desk immediately.
       ●​ Mobile devices may only access the State network and data through the
          internet using the authorized secure VPN connection. The VPN portal web
          address will be provided to users as required. Smart mobile devices such as
          smartphones, tablets, and laptops will access the State network and data using
          mobile VPN software installed on the device by the CIO.​

X.​    PASSWORD STANDARDS​

       ●​ Access to State systems and devices is controlled through individual accounts
          and passwords. The following requirements are in place to protect those
          passwords and access to sensitive data and systems:

                                                                            WyIT - 3001        Page 8

          ○​ Users will not share account or password information with another person.
          ○​ Accounts are to be used only by the assigned user of the account and only
              for authorized purposes.
          ○​ Attempting to obtain another user’s account password is strictly prohibited.
       ●​ A user must contact the CIO Service Desk to obtain a password reset if they
          have reason to believe any unauthorized person has learned their password.
          Users must take all necessary precautions to prevent unauthorized access to
          State resources, services, and data.
       ●​ Users with passwords that have been identified as compromised will be
          automatically reset.
       ●​ Users must not use State system passwords for non-state accounts (i.e.
          personal banking, social media, etc). In the event that non-state account(s) are
          compromised, it could leave State system accounts compromised as well.
       ●​ Password complexity will be enforced by IT through system-enforced policies
          to ensure strong passwords and proper password hygiene:
          ○​ Passwords will expire every 60 days, and users will be forced to change
              them. Users are encouraged to reset their passwords prior to the expiration
              date to minimize any interruption to network access.
          ○​ A minimum lifespan of 1-day is enforced to prevent too frequent password
              changes.
          ○​ The previous 24 passwords cannot be reused.
          ○​ Password complexity requirements enforce the use of a minimum of
              uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and special characters. It needs to be 14
              characters long.
          ○​ Upon 3 failed login attempts, accounts will be locked. Accounts can be
              unlocked by waiting for the 60 minutes to elapse or contacting the Service
              Desk.​

XI.​   INCIDENT RESPONSE AND REPORTING

       The State has an incident response program for efficient remediation of
       information security incidents. Employees are expected to comply with the
       following requirements in order to ensure effective and efficient incident
       remediation:

       ●​ Users must report any suspected security incident to the CIO service desk,
          including but not limited to lost/stolen equipment, suspected malware
          infection, compromised credentials, and any other possible compromises of
          State systems and/or data.

                                                                           WyIT - 3001       Page 9

        ●​ Users must cooperate with incident response processes, such as forfeiting
           their equipment to the CIO for investigation if it is potentially compromised.​

XII.​   SECURITY AWARENESS AND TRAINING​
        ​
        Human error and negligence are common sources of security issues. The State
        takes a proactive approach by requiring security awareness and training:​

        ●​ During onboarding, all users will be required to undergo information security
           awareness and training. Upon completion, users will be required to sign a
           declaration that they have completed training, understand the requirements
           and specific procedures taught, and intend to abide by the policies and
           procedures provided.
        ●​ Users must complete ongoing security awareness and training as assigned.
           Employees will be kept up to date on new improvements and emerging
           threats.
