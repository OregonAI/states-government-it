       1.   PURPOSE

            1.1.   This policy establishes the basic principles for the responsible and ethical
                   deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools across state government
                   operations. This policy aligns with preliminary findings of the West Virginia
                   Artificial Intelligence Task Force. The Task Force is mandated by W. Va. Code
                   §5A-6-9 to develop comprehensive policies that address data privacy, algorithmic
                   transparency, and the legal and ethical use of AI.

       2.   SCOPE

            2.1.   This policy applies to all departments (including agencies, boards, authorities,
                   and commissions) within the Executive Branch of the West Virginia government
                   that have access to or use state-provided resources.

            2.2.   An agency may impose additional restrictions on an employee's use of AI in the
                   workplace. However, the agency cannot allow exceptions to this policy unless it
                   receives permission from WVOT.

            2.3.   This policy does not supersede any federal, state, local, or agency rules, law,
                   policy, or ordinance that are more restrictive and applicable to state offices, or
                   state networks.

       3.   UTILIZATION OF AI IN STATE GOVERNMENT

            3.1.   AI plays a pivotal role in advancing the mission of the West Virginia state
                   government by optimizing the delivery of public services. By harnessing AI
                   capabilities, government agencies can operate with greater efficiency and
                   effectiveness, ensuring a seamless experience for citizens.

            3.2.   At all times, AI applications must comply with state and federal laws; must
                   maintain transparency, fairness, and accountability in government operations;
                   and must protect confidential information from disclosure. Responsible AI usage

 4.   PRINCIPLES FOR THE CREATION AND IMPLEMENTATION OF AI SYSTEMS

      4.1.     By integrating AI effectively, the State can empower employees, enhance service
               delivery, and drive innovation, all while ensuring compliance with regulatory
               frameworks.

      4.2.     All AI systems operating on the state network and state devices must adhere to
               fundamental principles that ensure responsible and ethical use. These systems
               must be fair, transparent, accountable, and designed with the understanding that
               they function within broader human systems.

      4.3.     Considerations for the WVOT network and connected devices:

               Every phase of AI creation and implementation must incorporate the following
               ethical safeguards and basic AI Principles of use (reference US EO 13960
               Principles of use in Government). Additionally, these minimal requirements apply
               to any application, device, or software connected to the WVOT network.

             4.3.1.     Non-Discrimination: To prevent discrimination, AI platforms must be
                        developed without biases related to race, religion, sex, or political
                        affiliations.

             4.3.2.     Public Benefit and Human-Centered: AI should be used to enhance and
                        improve the lives of the people it serves, not replace human judgment.
                        The primary goal is to empower the workforce and provide more efficient
                        and effective public services.

             4.3.3.     Privacy and Consent: User data, sensitive information, and citizen rights
                        must be protected through stringent security measures.

             4.3.4.     Unintended Consequences: AI outcomes should align with intended goals
                        and avoid negative impacts on users or affected communities.

             4.3.5.     Lawful and Respectful of Values: AI being used on the WVOT network or
                        WVOT connected devices must comply with the U.S. Constitution and all
                        applicable federal and state laws, rules, and policies.

             4.3.6.     Accurate, Reliable, and Effective: AI applications must be consistent with
                        the use cases for which they were trained and must be accurate, reliable,
                        and effective in their function.

             4.3.7.     Safe, Secure, and Resilient: AI systems must be secure against
                        vulnerabilities, adversarial manipulation, and other malicious exploitation
                        to ensure their safety and resilience.

             4.3.8.     Users must ensure proper data entry into AI systems with accurate data
                        classifications.

                  4.3.8.1.     Data Security and Input: Employees must never enter
                               confidential, sensitive, or protected state data, such as Personally
                               Identifiable Information (PII) or Protected Health Information (PHI),
                               into public-facing or otherwise unapproved generative AI systems
                               unless explicitly authorized under WVOT-PO1035. It must be
                               cautioned that even when employees are authorized to use
                               confidential information in generative AI systems, it is imperative
                               that employees take all reasonable and necessary measures to
                               ensure that the confidential information is secured and is not
                               improperly incorporated in public-facing documents.

             4.3.9.     Understandable: The operations and outcomes of AI applications should
                        be sufficiently understandable to subject matter experts, users, and other
                        relevant parties. This principle is key to building trust and enabling
                        effective oversight.

            4.3.10.     Responsible and Traceable: Accountability for AI requires a shared
                        responsibility model. While WVOT is responsible for approving AI tools
                        and ensuring baseline technical audit capabilities, individual state
                        agencies are responsible for defining the human roles and oversight
                        processes for their specific use cases. Retention of AI inputs and outputs
                        is governed by WVOT-PO1035, Section 7.

     4.4.      Continuous Monitoring and Improvement.

               The oversight of deployed AI systems is a shared responsibility. WVOT is
               responsible for continuously monitoring approved enterprise AI platforms for
               security, compliance, and network integrity. However, individual state agencies
               are strictly responsible for actively monitoring the outputs, accuracy, and ethical
               alignment of the final public products of the AI tools they deploy. Regular
               agency-level review ensures these systems operate as expected, do not develop
               biases, and continue to serve the public effectively.

             4.5.1.     Purposeful and Performance-Driven: Agencies should pursue AI
                        applications where the benefits significantly outweigh the risks, and those
                        risks can be properly assessed and managed.

             4.5.2.     Regularly Monitored: Agencies must regularly test their AI applications
                        against these principles. Mechanisms should be in place to supersede,
                        disengage, or deactivate systems that do not perform as intended.

             4.5.3.     Transparent: Where practicable and in accordance with applicable laws,
                        agencies must be transparent and should disclose relevant information
                        about the AI systems they deploy and AI-assisted consequential
                        decisions to appropriate stakeholders, including the WV Legislature and
                        its Committees, the Governor, the WVOT, and the public.

             4.5.4.     Accountable: Agencies are responsible and accountable for implementing
                        and enforcing proper safeguards for the use and function of their AI
                        applications. This includes but is not limited to, monitoring, auditing,
                        proper data classification use, and documenting compliance with these
                        safeguards, including monitoring for accuracy as defined in the Risk Tier
                        Classification Framework (Section 7).

 5.   AI GOVERNANCE AND OVERSIGHT

      5.1.     The CIO must approve all new artificial intelligence (AI) initiatives and projects
               before adoption or deployment. See CIO Review Services for details. This review
               mechanism ensures that the project or initiative adheres to established ethical
               and acceptable use policies.

             5.1.1.     Agencies must use the CIO Review process for the authorization of pilot
                        projects. For all Tier 2 and Tier 3 initiatives, agencies must submit a
                        completed Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) as a prerequisite for CIO
                        Review approval.

             5.1.2.     Through the CIO Review process, WVOT will maintain an inventory of all
                        AI use cases and verify that all projects satisfy stipulated security and
                        data privacy standards.

            7.       DEFINITIONS / RISK TIER CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK

                     Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA): A formal evaluation process used to identify and
                     mitigate the potential risks of an automated system. (Click here to access the form).

                     Consequential Decision: A decision made or substantially influenced by an AI system
                     that meaningfully affects an individual’s rights, access to public services, opportunities,
                     safety, or economic wellbeing. Consequential decisions also include AI‑assisted actions
                     that create significant legal, financial, equity, safety, or security risks for the public or the
                     state.

                     High-Risk AI System: An AI system that significantly affects individuals’ rights,
                     opportunities, safety, or economic well-being. This includes any system whose errors or
                     misuse could cause meaningful harm, inequitable outcomes, legal violations, or major
                     risks to business continuity and core operations.

          8.       IMPLEMENTATION GUIDELINES

                   Compliance: All AI utilization must also adhere to the Generative AI Acceptable Use
                   Policy (WVOT-PO1035) regarding data classification, security protocols, and
                   procurement risk management.

                   The "External-Facing" Rule: All public-facing AI (e.g., website chatbots) are automatically
                   classified as Tier 3 due to public trust and legal liability risks.

                   Human-in-the-Loop: Tier 2 and Tier 3 systems require a documented human override
                   process.

                   Procurement: For Tier 3 systems, the AIA must be completed during the
                   RFP/Purchasing phase.

                   References: NIST AI Risk Management Framework, Utah SB 149, West Virginia
                   PO1034 & PO1035.
