                          PATCH MANAGEMENT
                                       WyIT - 3021

 PURPOSE: This policy provides ongoing and consistent systems and applications,
 which are critical for a secure operational environment, by enforcing regular security
 updates and patches.

  I.   APPLICABILITY

       This policy applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions staff
       (collectively referred to as “agencies”). This policy is also applicable to consultants,
       affiliates, and temporary employees.

 II.   DEFINITIONS

       All definitions for this policy are located in the Policy Dictionary.

III.   EXCEPTIONS

       Any exceptions provided to the following policy must abide by the WyIT - 3002 -
       Security Exception Request policy.

IV.    REQUIREMENTS

       Regular application of properly licensed, affiliate-issued critical security updates
       and patches is necessary to protect data and systems from malicious attacks,
       compromise, and known vulnerabilities. All electronic devices connected to the
       state network, including servers, workstations, firewalls, network switches and
       routers, tablets, mobile devices, cellular devices, operating systems, office
       productivity software, database software, and affiliate applications, routinely require
       patching for functional and secure operations. Patches will be applied regularly
       within the timeframes outlined in Table 4 of WyIT 3020, Vulnerability Scanning.

  V.    AUTOMATED PATCHING PROCEDURES

        Many affiliates have automated patching procedures for individual applications and
        affiliate tools in coordination with the CIO; only approved management software to
        support this process across the enterprise is authorized. Regular application of
        critical/high-security patches is reviewed as part of normal change management
        and audit procedures.

 VI.    PATCHING EXCEPTIONS

        Production system patches may require complex testing and installation
        procedures. In certain cases, a security exception request may be required. The
        reason for departing this policy and the alternative protection measures to be taken
        shall be documented in writing, approved through a security exception request, and
        approved by the CIO. Deviations from normal patch schedules require the CIO’s
        approval.

VII.    SECURITY PATCHING PROCEDURES

        CIO established policies and procedures must be adhered to for vulnerability and
        patch management, ensuring application, system, and network device
        vulnerabilities are:

        ● Evaluated regularly and responded to in a timely fashion.
        ● Documented and well understood by support staff.
        ● Automated and regularly monitored.
        ● Executed in a manner applicable to affiliate-supplied tools on a regularly
          communicated schedule.
        ● Applied in a timely and orderly manner based on the criticality and applicability
          of patches and enhancements.

VIII.   SYSTEM REBOOT CYCLE

        State-authorized and owned systems will be required to reboot at the discretion of
        the patching job. These required reboots will be administered via patch
        management software, the specific patching job configurations, and the operating
        system. The required reboots shall include notifications that proactively inform end
        users of the reboot, provide a window of time and limited deferrals to save work
        and restart their system. If the window of time is exceeded or the deferrals are

      depleted, the forced reboot will occur. The operating system will force such reboots
      to ensure each system meets the minimum requirements. The patching job will
      define all reboots. Any deviation from this policy will result in a security exception
      request to be processed.

IX.   AUDIT CONTROLS AND MANAGEMENT

      On-demand documented procedures and evidence of practice shall be in place for
      this operational policy as part of the CIO’s internal systems change management
      and update procedures. Examples of adequate controls include:

      ● Documented change management meetings and conversations between key
        state stakeholders.
      ● System updates and patch logs for all major system and utility categories.
      ● Outlined in Wyoming Statute § 9-2-2904(a)(i), this policy applies to all executive
        branch agencies, boards and commissions, and state personnel members,
        including, but not limited to, full-time employees, part-time employees, trainees,
        volunteers, contractors, and temporary workers. Additionally, some affiliates,
        such as contractors or vendors, may be required to adhere, as indicated in
        contractual requirements.
      ● Logs shall include system ID, date patched, patch status, exception, and reason
        for exception.
      ● Demonstrated infrastructure supporting enterprise patch management across
        systems, applications, and devices.
