Introduction:
This guide supports Wyoming state agencies in properly classifying, protecting, and handling state
information. This guide incorporates data domain concepts as a foundational element of effective
information governance.

This standard applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions staff (collectively
referred to as "agencies"). This standard is also applicable to consultants, affiliates, third party vendors
and temporary employees that use or store data. ​

Authority
This guideline is promulgated under the authority of Wyoming State Statute 9-2-2906 and its
subsections, the Department of Enterprise Technology Services is required to adopt and enforce
information technology standards, and promote efficient use of information technology throughout state
government.

Definitions
Key Definitions, Acronyms, and Terms

Data Classification Levels:

   ●​ Public (Low Sensitivity)

       ○​ Information that poses no risk to the State if made generally available.

       ○​ This includes information intended for public release or already publicly available.

       ○​ Example data types: press releases, public meeting minutes, published reports, agency
          contact information, public-facing website content.

   ●​ Internal (Moderate Sensitivity):
      ○​ Information whose loss, corruption, or unauthorized disclosure is of importance only inside
          the State and, therefore, would not result in a tangible business, financial, or legal loss.

       ○​ This information is not routinely shared outside the State government but is generally
          accessible to State employees.

   ●​ Confidential (High Sensitivity):
      ○​ Information whose loss, corruption, or unauthorized disclosure would seriously harm an
         individual's, business's, or the State's reputation or business position, resulting in severe
         financial and legal loss.

       ○​ Confidential information is subject to controlled access and special handling requirements.

           ○​ Confidential-Standard: Sensitive business or operational information

           ○​ Confidential-Regulated: Information subject to specific regulatory requirements

Other Key Concepts
   ●​ Classification Inheritance

           ○​ The process by which compiled or derivative information takes on the highest
              classification level of any component information.
           ○​ When information consists of elements with different classification levels, the entire
              information asset shall be classified at the highest level of any component element.

                                                      2

                                                    ​
   ●​ Aggregated Data

           ○​ Collections of data elements from multiple sources that may require higher classification
              than individual elements due to revealing patterns or sensitive insights when combined.
           ○​ Agencies must consider whether aggregated data requires higher classification than its
              component parts due to mosaic effects or inference risks.

Detailed Classification System:
 1.​   PUBLIC (LOW SENSITIVITY) INFORMATION
       1.1.​ Public (low sensitivity) information is information that poses no risk to the State, if made
             generally available. This type of information is typically either Unrestricted or Available
             Upon Request.​

       1.2.​   Unrestricted:​

               1.2.1.​   Information that is public knowledge or readily available to the public.​

               1.2.2.​   May be disclosed in the normal course of conversation or other methods of
                         communication without regard to whom the communication occurs.

       1.3.​   Available Upon Request
                1.3.1.​ Information readily available to the public but requires a formal request either
                         in written form or in person.​

               1.3.2.​   Personnel should verify that the request is reasonable based on the Wyoming
                         Public Records Act (WPRA).​

               1.3.3.​   Example types: employee names and direct telephone numbers, general or
                         generic information about the organization, public meeting minutes.​

       1.4.​   Typically, the handling requirements for this type of information:​

               1.4.1.​   May be freely distributed without restrictions, in accordance with W.S.
                         §16-4-202.​

               1.4.2.​   May be published on internet websites available to all internet users.​

               1.4.3.​   Have no special security measures required beyond normal good practices.​

               1.4.4.​   Should still be reviewed for accuracy before public release.​

 2.​   INTERNAL (MODERATE SENSITIVITY) INFORMATION
       2.1.​ Examples may include, but are not limited to:​

               2.1.1.​   Internal Memos​

                                                     3

                                                    ​
              2.1.2.​    Project Plans​

              2.1.3.​    Non-sensitive Internal Communications​

              2.1.4.​    Draft Documents​

              2.1.5.​    General Operating Procedures​

              2.1.6.​    General Employee Directories​

              2.1.7.​    Non-sensitive Meeting Notes​

              2.1.8.​    Routine Administrative Information​

      2.2.​   Typically, the handling requirements for this type of information:​

              2.2.1.​    Should be kept within state organization​

              2.2.2.​    May be shared between agencies but not with external parties without
                         approval​

              2.2.3.​    Requires basic access controls to ensure only authorized state personnel can
                         access.​

              2.2.4.​    May be included in agency intranets and areas of internet websites that
                         require users to log in.​

              2.2.5.​    Should be labeled as “internal” where practical​

              2.2.6.​    Should be stored in systems with appropriate security controls.

3.​   CONFIDENTIAL (HIGH SENSITIVITY)
      3.1.​ Confidential (high sensitivity) is information whose loss, corruption, or unauthorized
            disclosure would seriously harm an individual's, business's, or the State's reputation or
            business position, resulting in severe financial and legal loss.​

      3.2.​   Confidential-Standard information is sensitive business or operational information.

              3.2.1.​    Example data types: security plans, sensitive strategic plans, contract
                         negotiations, legal opinions, processing volumes, service pricing, equipment
                         configurations, vendor information, application software configurations, and
                         other similar business and technical information.​

              3.2.2.​    Confidential-Regulated information is information subject to specific regulatory
                         requirements and requires strict control due to legal and regulatory mandates.​

                 3.2.2.1.​    Example data types​

                        3.2.2.1.1.​   Personally Identifiable Information (PII)​

                                                     4

                                                    ​
                        3.2.2.1.2.​   Protected Health Information (PHI)​

                        3.2.2.1.3.​   Federal Tax Information (FTI)​

                        3.2.2.1.4.​   Criminal Justice Information (CJI)​

                        3.2.2.1.5.​   Payment Card Data​

                        3.2.2.1.6.​   Employee private information (home address, telephone number,
                                      years of service, etc.); and​

                        3.2.2.1.7.​   Customer information (names, contacts, addresses, etc.)​

              3.2.3.​    The handling requirements for this type of information includes:​

                 3.2.3.1.​    Strict control requirement;.​

                 3.2.3.2.​    Access on a need-to-know basis only;

                 3.2.3.3.​    Encryption for storage and transmission;

                 3.2.3.4.​    Written approval is needed for any external disclosure, in compliance with
                              W.S. §16-4-203 and applicable federal laws;

                 3.2.3.5.​    Never published on any Internet Website;

                 3.2.3.6.​    Clear labeling as "Confidential" required;

                 3.2.3.7.​    Should be stored in secured systems with robust access controls; and

                 3.2.3.8.​    Transfer and sharing subject to additional restrictions

4.​   DATA DOMAINS
      4.1.​ Data domains are logical groupings of related data that share common characteristics,
            serve similar business functions, or support specific program areas within an
            organization. They represent distinct categories of information that an agency manages
            and uses to fulfill its mission.
5.​   ALIGN DATA DOMAINS WITH ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE AND FUNCTIONS​

      5.1.​   Identify domains based on major program areas within your agency.​

      5.2.​   Ensure domains reflect the primary functions and services your agency provides.​

              5.2.1.​    Example: A health department might have domains for vital records, disease
                         surveillance, provider licensing, and public health education.

                                                     5

                                                      ​
 6.​    ACCESS DATA CLASSIFICATION AND SENSITIVITY
        6.1.​ Use Wyoming's three-tier classification system to group data with similar sensitivity
              levels.​

        6.2.​   Group data with similar protection requirements to streamline security controls.​

                 6.2.1.​   Example: Group all data containing personally identifiable information (PII)
                           that requires similar protection.

 7.​    CONSIDER REGULATORY AND COMPLIANCE FACTORS
        7.1.​ Identify data subject to specific regulations or statutes.​

        7.2.​   Group data that shares compliance requirements.​

                 7.2.1.​   Example: Create a domain for all data subject to HIPAA regulations in a
                           healthcare agency.​

 8.​    ANALYZE DATA RELATIONSHIPS AND DEPENDENCIES
        8.1.​ Map how data flows between systems and stakeholders.​

        8.2.​   Identify relationships between different types of data​

                 8.2.1.​   Example: Customer contact information used across multiple programs should
                           be managed as a single domain.​

 9.​    EVALUATE DATA LIFECYCLE CONSIDERATIONS
        9.1.​ Group data with similar retention schedules.​

        9.2.​   Consider how data is created, updated, archived, and destroyed​

                 9.2.1.​   Example: Group financial records that must be retained for 7 years​

10.​    ASSESS OPERATIONAL MANAGEMENT FACTORS
       10.1.​ Identify natural groupings based on who manages the data.​

       10.2.​   Consider who needs access to the data and how it's used​

                10.2.1.​   Example: Create a domain for all GIS data that requires specialized
                           knowledge and tools​

11.​    DATA DOMAINS AND CLASSIFICATION SUPPORT
       11.1.​ Establish clear lines of responsibility for data classification.​

       11.2.​   Enable designation of specific data stewards responsible for classification.​

                                                      6

                                                     ​
       11.3.​   Create clear accountability for data quality, security, and compliance.​

12.​    ENHANCED DATA QUALITY AND CONSISTENCY
       12.1.​ Allow for consistent classification across similar data types.​

       12.2.​   Facilitate implementation of appropriate quality measures for each domain.​

       12.3.​   Reduce redundancy and conflicting classification decisions.​

13.​    OPTIMIZED SECURITY CONTROLS
       13.1.​ Enable right-sized security controls based on domain sensitivity.​

       13.2.​   Facilitate better risk assessment and mitigation strategies.​

       13.3.​   Streamline compliance with relevant regulations.​

14.​    BETTER DATA SHARING AND INTEGRATION
       14.1.​ Enable appropriate sharing within and across agencies.​

       14.2.​   Define clear boundaries for what data can be shared, with whom, and under what
                conditions.​

       14.3.​   Facilitate data integration between systems and agencies
