Guidelines for Use of Generative AI
Agency of Digital Services — Division of Artificial Intelligence
Last Updated: July 1, 2026

Purpose and Scope
Pursuant to 3 V.S.A. § 5022, the Division of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has reviewed the
capabilities of currently-available generative AI tools, including large language models (LLMs)
such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and LLaMA, as well as other AI-powered modalities such as
meeting assistants, transcription tools, and AI features embedded within third-party applications.
The Division is issuing the following guidance for State employees who wish to use these tools
in the performance of their official duties.

Use of generative AI tools for official duties is acceptable subject to the limitations described
below. ADS will update these guidelines as technologies evolve, and Departments or Agencies
may issue more restrictive guidelines at their discretion.

Key Definitions and Tool Categories
For the purposes of these guidelines, the following categories of tools are distinguished:

Public AI Tools: Externally hosted, publicly available AI tools and services, including
consumer-facing LLM chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini), AI meeting assistants hosted by third-
party vendors (e.g., Fireflies.ai), and AI features embedded in third-party applications where
data is processed on external servers. Non-public information may not be entered into or
processed by public AI tools.

Internal AI Tools: Secure AI tools hosted on the State’s internal network and limited to access
by SoV employees only, such as ChatVT. These tools are pre-approved for general employee
use and may be used with most types of sensitive or confidential State data, except for Federal
Tax Information (FTI), MARS-E regulated data, and Criminal Justice Information Services
(CJIS) data.

Capabilities
Generative AI tools offer assistance across a broad range of modalities, including:

   •   Text generation and editing: drafting correspondence, policy documents, summaries,
       and code
   •   Visual content generation: creating images and graphics
   •   Meeting assistance: real-time transcription, summarization, and action item extraction
       during calls and meetings
   •   Embedded AI features: AI-powered tools built into productivity applications (e.g.,
       grammar and drafting assistants, AI search, automated workflows)

These tools can be powerful in a document and correspondence-heavy organization.
Generative AI shifts the content generation process to focus on developing ideas and refining
outputs rather than the mechanics of writing or transcription. This can result in better quality
content with lower overall effort.

Usage Guidelines
The following guidelines apply to all generative AI tools used for official State business.
Additional considerations specific to certain tool categories are addressed in subsequent
sections.

   1. Compliance with Policy: Employees must use generative AI tools in accordance with
      State Personnel Policy 11.7, the AI Code of Ethics, and these guidelines.

   2. Supervisory Approval: Employees must obtain supervisory approval before using any
      AI tool for official State business. Supervisors may consult with the Director of AI or their
      ADS IT Lead when evaluating whether a particular use is acceptable.

   3. Restrictions on Non-Public Information in Public AI Tools: Employees may not input
      or transmit non-public information into or through public AI tools. All information
      submitted to a public AI tool should be treated as potentially public. This restriction
      applies regardless of the modality — whether a chatbot, a meeting assistant, or an
      embedded application feature. The following is a non-exhaustive list of information that
      may not be used with public AI tools:
           ◦ Confidential or privileged information or communications
           ◦ Personally identifying information (PII)
           ◦ Protected health information (PHI)
           ◦ Code containing passwords or secrets
           ◦ Information that has the potential to erode trust in Vermont’s institutions

Note on ChatVT: As an internal tool, ChatVT is pre-approved for use with most sensitive and
confidential State data. The above data restrictions do not apply to ChatVT, except that FTI,
MARS-E regulated data, and CJIS data may not be processed in ChatVT.

   4. Free Tools Only (Public AI): With the above restrictions, this policy enables employees
      to use publicly available AI tools offered free of charge. Employees may not individually
      pay for AI services or sign up for services requiring payment using State resources. Any
      procurement of AI products must go through the mandated State procurement
      processes. Specific public generative AI tools may be conditionally approved for use by
      the Director of Artificial Intelligence if those tools are purchased under an ADS-held
      contract that meets required data and security criteria.

   5. Accuracy and Verification: Employees must ensure the accuracy of all content
      produced with AI assistance. Generative AI may produce content that is incorrect,
      outdated, or fictitious, and such errors may not be readily apparent. All AI-generated
      content must be reviewed for accuracy, veracity, and completeness, as with any other
      source of information.

   6. Employee Responsibility: Employees using AI tools remain responsible for their work
      product regardless of what portion was produced by AI. AI tools have demonstrated
      biases which can negatively impact groups or individuals. All content must be reviewed
      for accuracy, completeness, and alignment with Vermont’s values.

   7. Use of State Accounts: When using a public AI tool that requires account registration,
      employees must register and log in using their State email address only. Official

       business may not be conducted using an account established with a personal email
       address.

   8. Reputational Risk: Employees must not use AI tools in any way that could cause
      reputational harm to the State if it were known.

   9. Supplemental, Not Relied Upon: While it is acceptable to use AI tools to assist with
      official job duties, employees should not develop reliance on them — particularly free
      public tools, which may have unforeseen downtime, pricing changes, or discontinuation.
      Treat AI assistance as an optional enhancement. If there is an opportunity to make
      generative AI a part of a standard work process, ADS and the AI Director will assist with
      procuring and implementing a robust, institutionally supported solution.

   10. Intellectual Property: Employees may not use AI tools in any way that infringes on the
       intellectual property rights of others.

AI Meeting Assistants and Transcription Tools
Third-party AI meeting assistants — including tools that transcribe, summarize, or extract action
items from calls and meetings — present privacy and security considerations, especially when
external participants are present.

Employee Authority
State employees are authorized to prohibit or require the removal of third-party AI meeting tools
during any call or meeting that may involve sensitive or non-public information. Employees may
exercise this authority:
    • Before a meeting begins, as a condition of participation
    • During a meeting, at any time, without requiring additional approval
    • Regardless of whether the meeting is internal or includes external parties

Responsibilities
Employees and meeting organizers using or enabling AI meeting tools must ensure that:
  • Any third-party meeting assistant is appropriate for the sensitivity of content to be
      discussed, consistent with Section 3 above
  • External participants are informed of the State’s policy on AI tool usage before or at the
      start of the meeting
  • Existing procedures for maintaining official records of meetings are followed where
      applicable (e.g., standard protocols for recording and publishing public meetings)
  • Transcripts, summaries, or other AI-generated meeting records are treated as official
      records subject to applicable retention and disclosure requirements

Key Risks
Employees should be aware of the following risks when considering whether to permit AI
meeting tools:

   •   Data Exposure: Audio and text data may be transmitted to external servers for
       processing, creating potential exposure of sensitive information outside SoV-controlled
       environments.
   •   Unverified Control: Employees often lack direct administrative control or visibility into
       whether these tools have stopped recording or processing data.

   •   Retention and Secondary Use: Vendors may retain transcripts or audio data for
       extended periods and use them for purposes such as model training, increasing risk of
       unauthorized disclosure.
   •   Compliance Risks: Use of external AI tools may conflict with state privacy laws,
       contractual obligations, or public records requirements.
Note on Accessibility Use Cases: AI tools may be used for transcription, translation, and
captioning in support of accessibility needs (e.g., providing captions for public meetings,
translating materials for limited-English-proficient audiences). These uses are generally
acceptable, but require human review and citation prior to distribution. Where resources allow,
human-generated transcription, translation, or captioning is preferable.

AI Features Embedded in Third-Party Applications
Many productivity and business applications now include embedded AI features, such as AI-
assisted drafting, smart summarization, automated classification, or AI-powered search. The
same overarching principle applies: employees are responsible for ensuring that any AI feature
they enable or use processes non-public data only through approved, secure channels.

Before enabling an embedded AI feature in a third-party application, employees should confirm:

   •   Whether the feature transmits data to external servers
   •   Whether the data to be processed is appropriate for that tool under Section 3 of these
       guidelines
   •   That supervisory approval has been obtained

Employees uncertain about the data handling practices of an embedded AI feature should
consult with their ADS IT Lead or the Director of Artificial Intelligence.

Acceptable Use and Citation Requirements
To maintain public trust and meet the transparency requirements of the AI Code of Ethics,
employees must appropriately disclose the use of AI-generated content where indicated below.
These requirements apply regardless of the AI modality used (chatbot, meeting assistant,
embedded tool, etc.).

If there are questions about where a particular use case falls, supervisors and employees may
consult with their IT Lead or the Director of AI.

Standard Citation Format
   This content was [drafted / edited / translated / summarized] with the assistance of a generative
   artificial intelligence tool [name tool]. The content has been reviewed and verified to be accurate and
   complete, and represents the intent of [office / department / the State / person’s name].

Emergency Translation Citation Format
   This content was translated with the assistance of a generative artificial intelligence [Google
   Translate / Azure AI]. The content has NOT YET been reviewed and verified but will be as soon as
   possible. This notice will be updated once the review is complete. For any questions about this
   content or to report confusing or conflicting text, please contact [office-email@vermont.gov].
