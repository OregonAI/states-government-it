I.      POLICY

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This policy sets forth the permissible uses for video surveillance, including mandatory
technical specifications for all video surveillance deployed by and within the State.

PURPOSE
The increased availability of surveillance devices and cameras has raised questions concerning
their appropriate use, particularly inside the workplace. Video surveillance systems procured
by the State must maintain minimum security specifications to ensure the safety of State
employees, facilities, and data. This policy regulates all uses of surveillance cameras and
surveillance monitoring and recording in order to achieve these purposes while also protecting
the legal and privacy interests of the State, its residents, visitors, and employees. Excluding
law enforcement, only State authorized surveillance equipment is allowed to be used for
surveillance. The use of personally owned surveillance equipment is thereby prohibited from
being used for surveillance of any nature.

POLICY
  1. State organizations that choose to implement a video surveillance solution must
     adhere to the enterprise requirements outlined in this policy.
  2. Video surveillance systems recording access to State facilities should store and
     maintain at least 90 days' worth of recorded activity.

   II.       DEFINITIONS
     1. Surveillance Equipment/Security Cameras — Any item, system, camera,
        technology device, communications device, or process, used alone or in conjunction
        with a network, for gathering, monitoring, recording, or storing an image(s) of State
        property and/or people on State property. Video surveillance is also referred to as
        CCTV.
     2. Facility – Any State-owned or managed physical structure, building, or place that
        provides more than one open and active connection to the State’s computing
        network.
     3. Law Enforcement – Law enforcement refers to the organized system and agencies
        responsible for ensuring compliance with laws, maintaining public order, and
        preventing, detecting, and investigating crimes. Law enforcement agencies operate
        at various levels, including local, state, and federal, and they work together to
        protect communities and uphold the rule of law.
     4. Network Video Recorder (NVR) – A computer system that records video footage
        and stores it on a hard disk, a mass storage device, or cloud storage. It is paired
        with digital internet protocol (IP) cameras to create video surveillance systems.

Appendix A
The following are minimum requirements of the IP Video Surveillance Systems in
State of DE facilities:

Open Network Video Interface Forum (ONVIF) Compliance
ONVIF Compliance ensures that regardless of manufacturer, an IP Camera will be
compatible with devices from other manufacturers.
ONVIF Certified products can be identified using the ONVIF conformity tool, located at:
https://www.onvif.org/conformant-products/
• Must be compliant with ONVIF profile/s S and/or T.
• For Devices that support onboard recording to a Secure Digital (SD) card ONVIF Profile
    G is recommended.
• All other ONVIF profiles optional.
        o S – Supports compatibility between Network Video Recorders and Video
            Management Systems. Also includes ONVIF specifications for Pan Tilt Zoom (PTZ)
            control, audio, multicasting, and relay outputs for conformant devices and clients
            that support such features.

Network Requirements
• Devices must be able to function without connectivity to the internet.
• Devices must reside within a designated security network with isolation from internet
   access and the internal user network. All inbound and outbound access must be
   implemented through a State firewall and approved by the DTI Chief Security Office.
• Camera systems outside the State defined requirements must be approved by the Chief
   Security Office within DTI, before purchase via the State’s waiver process.

Environment Specific Requirements
• Cameras exposed to weather elements must be rated for outdoor use.
• PTZ Cameras that are exposed to freezing temperatures must be rated for
   arctic/freezing temperatures.
• Where cameras may be exposed to high levels of humidity, defogging lenses are
   recommended.
• Cameras that can be easily reached by inmates must be rated as Vandal Resistant or
   provide Active Tampering notification.
• Cameras used in low light applications should have built-in Infrared (IR) capabilities.
