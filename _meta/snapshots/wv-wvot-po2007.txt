 ​1.​   ​PURPOSE​

        ​1.1.​   ​ he​​purpose​​of​​this​​Data​​Sharing​​Policy​​is​​to​​establish​​a​​clear,​​consistent,​
                 T
                 ​and​ ​secure​ ​framework​ ​for​ ​the​ ​responsible​ ​sharing​ ​of​ ​data​ ​across​ ​state​
                  ​government​ ​agencies​ ​and​ ​with​ ​external​ ​partners.​ ​This​ ​policy​ ​aims​ ​to​
                   ​promote​ ​transparency,​ ​improve​ ​public​ ​services,​ ​support​ ​evidence-based​
                    ​decision-making,​ ​and​ ​foster​ ​innovation,​ ​while​ ​ensuring​ ​the​ ​protection​ ​of​
                     ​personal​ ​privacy,​ ​data​ ​security,​ ​and​ ​compliance​ ​with​ ​applicable​​laws​​and​
                      ​regulations.​ ​One​ ​of​ ​the​ ​biggest​ ​disruptors​ ​to​ ​day-to-day​ ​business​ ​is​
                       ​meeting​ ​and​ ​addressing​ ​new​ ​regulatory​ ​standards​ ​and​ ​data​ ​security​ ​set​
                        ​forth​ ​by​ ​Federal​ ​and​ ​State​ ​regulation.​ ​The​ ​Goal​ ​is​ ​to​ ​remove​ ​barriers​ ​in​
                         ​taking​​in​​the​​State​​Agency’s​​IT​​landscape​​and​​facilitate​​smarter​​IT​​choices​
                          ​while aligning with the States' strategic goals.​

 ​2.​   ​SCOPE​

        ​2.1.​   ​ his​​Data​​Sharing​​Policy​​applies​​to​​all​​departments,​​agencies,​​boards​​and​
                 T
                 ​commissions​​operating​​under​​the​​jurisdiction​​of​​the​​state​​government,​​that​
                  ​is​ ​currently​ ​supported​ ​by​ ​WV​ ​Office​ ​of​ ​technology​ ​and​ ​that​ ​collect,​
                   ​generate,​ ​store,​​manage,​​or​​use​​data.​​It​​governs​​the​​sharing​​of​​data​​both​
                    ​internally​ ​among​ ​government​ ​bodies​ ​and​ ​externally​ ​with​ ​approved​ ​third​
                     ​parties,​ ​including​ ​local​ ​governments,​ ​federal​ ​agencies,​ ​academic​
                      ​institutions, non-profit organizations, and private sector partners.​

                  ​exchange data with each other. APIs enable systems to interact in a​
                   ​standardized way, allowing developers to access specific functions or data​
                    ​needing to understand the internal workings of the other system.​

        ​4.2.​   ​ oard of Risk and Insurance Management (BRIM) - The WV Board of Risk​

        ​4.3.​   ​ hief Information Officer (CIO) - the senior executive for overseeing an​
                 C

                  ​In the context of state government, the CIO ensures alignment of IT​
                   ​initiatives with agency goals, promotes cybersecurity and data​
                    ​governance, and provides leadership in the planning, development, and​
                     ​implementation of technology solutions.​

        ​4.4.​   ​ ata - recognized as a strategic asset that must be managed responsibly​

        ​4.5.​   ​ ata Retention - the policies and practices that govern how long data is​

                     ​purposes.​

     ​4.7.​   ​ ata Sharing Agreement (DSA) - a formal, written agreement between two​
              D
              ​or more parties that outlines the terms, conditions, and responsibilities​
               ​related to the access, exchange, use, and protection of shared data. It​
                ​defines the purpose of the data sharing, the types of data involved,​
                 ​security and privacy requirements, compliance obligations (such as​
                  ​FERPA, HIPAA, or IRS 1075), and procedures for data handling, retention,​
                   ​and breach notification.​

     ​4.8.​   ​ igital Record - information or documentation that is created, received,​
              D
              ​stored, or managed in electronic format. It serves as an official record of​
               ​actions, decisions, transactions, or communications and must be​
                ​maintained in accordance with applicable legal, regulatory, and records​
                 ​management requirements.​

     ​4.9.​   ​ amily Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) is a federal law that​
              F
              ​protects the privacy of student education records. It grants parents—and​
               ​eligible students once they reach 18 years of age or attend a​
                ​postsecondary institution—the right to access, review, and request​
                 ​corrections to their education records, and it restricts the disclosure of​
                  ​those records without prior written consent, except under certain permitted​
                   ​conditions.​

    ​4.10.​   ​ ederal Tax Information (FTI) - any data, document, or record provided by​
              F
              ​the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) or obtained through an authorized​
               ​source that contains information about a taxpayer, including return or​
                ​return information. FTI is protected under the Internal Revenue Code​
                 ​(IRC) §6103 and is subject to strict handling, security, and disclosure​
                  ​requirements as outlined in IRS Publication 1075.​

    ​4.11.​   ​ ealth Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) - federal law​
              H
              ​enacted in 1996 that establishes national standards for protecting the​
               ​privacy and security of individuals’ health information. HIPAA applies to​
                ​healthcare providers, health plans, and healthcare clearinghouses, as well​
                 ​as their business associates, and governs the use, disclosure, and​
                  ​safeguarding of Protected Health Information (PHI) in both physical and​
                   ​electronic formats.​

    ​4.12.​   I​nternal Revenue Code (IRC) §6103 - federal statute that governs the​
               ​confidentiality and disclosure of Federal Tax Information (FTI). It strictly​
                ​limits how tax return data and related information can be accessed, used,​
                 ​and shared by the IRS, federal agencies, and authorized state or local​
                  ​entities. The provision is designed to protect taxpayer privacy by​
                   ​prohibiting unauthorized disclosure of tax information, with civil and​
                    ​criminal penalties for violations.​

    ​4.13.​   I​RS Publication 1075 - guidelines and requirements for ensuring the​
               ​confidentiality, integrity, and security of Federal Tax Information (FTI). It​
                ​establishes the standards that federal, state, and local agencies must​
                 ​follow when accessing, storing, processing, or transmitting FTI. The​
                  ​publication outlines physical, administrative, and technical safeguards to​
                   ​prevent unauthorized access or disclosure and ensure compliance with​
                    ​the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) §6103.​

    ​4.14.​   I​ntergovernmental Relationship Manager (IRM) - a WVOT liaison​
               ​responsible for facilitating collaboration, communication, and coordination​
                ​between government entities at various levels (local, state, and federal).​
                 ​This role ensures alignment on shared initiatives, and supports​
                  ​interagency partnerships to achieve common goals.​

    ​4.15.​   I​nformation Security Officer (ISO) is the individual responsible for​
               ​establishing, implementing, and maintaining an agency’s information​
                ​security program. The ISO ensures compliance with applicable security​
                 ​policies, standards, and regulations, manages risk, and oversees the​
                  ​protection of information systems and data from unauthorized access,​
                   ​disclosure, or disruption.​

    ​4.16.​   I​nformation Technology Investment Portfolio System (ITPS) - the West​
               ​Virginia Office of Technology’s centralized platform for managing, tracking,​
                ​and reporting information technology investments across state agencies. It​
                 ​also serves as the official system of record for WVOT to document Data​
                  ​Sharing Agreements.​

    ​4.18.​   ​ IST Special Publication 800-37 (SP 800-37) - titled "Risk Management​
              N
              ​Framework for Information Systems and Organizations," is a publication​
               ​by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) that provides​
                ​guidelines for applying the Risk Management Framework (RMF) to federal​
                 ​information systems. It outlines a structured, lifecycle-based process for​
                  ​managing security and privacy risks through steps such as system​
                   ​categorization, control selection and implementation, assessment,​
                    ​authorization, and continuous monitoring. SP 800-37 is widely used​
                     ​across federal and state agencies to ensure compliance, strengthen​
                      ​cybersecurity, and support informed risk-based decision-making.​

    ​4.19.​   ​ rivacy Framework - structured approach that organizations use to​
              P
              ​identify, manage, and mitigate privacy risks related to the collection, use,​
               ​sharing, and storage of personal data. It provides a set of principles,​
                ​policies, and controls designed to ensure compliance with privacy laws​
                 ​and regulations, protect individual rights, and promote responsible data​
                  ​governance. Notable examples include the NIST Privacy Framework and​
                   ​the OECD Privacy Principles.​

    ​4.20.​   ​ rotected Health Information (PHI) - individually identifiable health​
              P
              ​information that is created, received, maintained, or transmitted by a​
               ​covered entity or its business associate, as defined by HIPAA. PHI​
                ​includes data related to an individual’s past, present, or future physical or​
                 ​mental health condition, the provision of healthcare, or payment for​
                  ​healthcare services, and can exist in any form—electronic, paper, or oral.​

    ​4.21.​   ​ rganization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)​
              O
              ​Privacy Principles - a set of internationally recognized guidelines​
               ​developed by the OECD to promote the protection of personal data and​
                ​privacy across member countries. Originally established in 1980 and​
                 ​updated in 2013, these principles include key concepts such as collection​
                  ​limitation, data quality, purpose specification, use limitation, security​
                   ​safeguards, openness, individual participation, and accountability. They​
                    ​serve as a foundational framework for many national and international​
                     ​privacy laws and policies.​
