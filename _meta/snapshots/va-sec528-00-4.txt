1.    PROHIBITED TECHNOLOGIES POLICY

1.1     Background

        The Commonwealth of Virginia (COV) utilizes numerous information technology services,
        hardware and software to support the missions of its agencies.

        The Commonwealth and the federal government have identified some services, hardware and
        software that are prohibited for Commonwealth usage. The CIO is charged to promote awareness
        of prohibited hardware, software, or services, per Virginia Code § 2.2-2009(H): The CIO shall
        promptly notify all public bodies as defined in § 2.2-5514 of hardware, software, or services that
        have been prohibited pursuant to Chapter 55.3 (§ 2.2-5514).

        Virginia public bodies may not use (directly or indirectly) hardware, software, or services that are
        prohibited, as listed in this policy. Virginia public bodies also should ensure that their contractors
        do not use such hardware, software, or services.

1.2     Guidance

        Some hardware, software, or services are prohibited by federal authorities and, as a result, by
        Virginia law. See Va. Code § 2.2-5514. This policy provides a central list.

        The Commonwealth also may prohibit information technology that is not prohibited by the federal
        government.

        Various federal entities and sources provide identification of certain prohibited services,
        hardware, and software, including:

                 -   Section 889 of the of the John S. McCain National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA)
                     for Fiscal Year (FY) 2019 (Pub. L. 115-232) and implementing Federal Acquisition
                     Regulation (FAR) rules and guidance from the Department of Defense (DoD), General
                     Services Administration (GSA), and National Aeronautics and Space Agency (NASA).
                     More information about federal contracting requirements and rules may be found
                     from the above entities and/or by visiting https://www.acquisition.gov/Section-889-
                     Policies.
                 -   Division R of the December 2022 omnibus spending bill (the Consolidated
                     Appropriations Act, 2023), and implementing standards and guidelines led by the
                     Office of Management and Budget (OMB).
                 -   The Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC), an interagency federal body
                     focused on supply chain risk management.
                 -   The Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), a component of the
                     Department of Homeland Security.

        Federal government prohibitions bar obtaining (through a contract or other instrument) certain
        telecommunications equipment (including video surveillance equipment) or services produced by
        the covered entities and their subsidiaries and affiliates.

        Note that TikTok is prohibited both by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 and by
        Executive Order 24 (EO24), issued by Governor Youngkin on December 16, 2022, and available
        from the Governor’s Office’s website.

1.3       Statement of Policy

          It is the policy of the COV (see Virginia Code §2.2-603(F) that each Agency Head is responsible
          for securing the electronic data that is held by the agency and shall comply with the requirements
          of Virginia Code § 2.2-2009. Information security policies, standards, and guidelines promulgated
          pursuant to § 2.2009 shall apply to the Commonwealth's executive, legislative, and judicial
          branches and independent agencies. Per §2.2-2009 (H), the CIO shall promptly notify all public
          bodies as defined in § 2.2-5514 of hardware, software, or services that have been prohibited
          pursuant to Chapter 55.3 (§ 2.2-5514).

          This policy notifies public bodies that they may not use (directly or indirectly) prohibited
          hardware, software, or services and informs public bodies of current prohibitions. Public bodies
          also should not enter into, extend, or renew a contract with an entity that uses covered
          telecommunications equipment or services as a substantial or essential component of any
          system, or as critical technology as part of any system.

3. Compliance

Compliance with applicable prohibitions on use and contracting is mandatory for all public bodies, as
defined in Virginia Code § 2.2-5514(A).

4. Process for Requesting Exceptions

        A state public body may request an exception through the following process. If the head of an
        agency determines that compliance with the provisions of this policy or any related information
        security standards would adversely impact a business process, the agency head may request
        approval to deviate from a specific requirement by submitting an exception request to the CISO.
        For each exception, the requesting agency shall fully document:

             •   The business need;
             •   The scope and extent;
             •   Mitigating safeguards;
             •   Residual risks;
             •   The specific duration; and
             •   Agency head approval.

        Each request shall be approved by the agency head, indicating acceptance of the defined residual
        risks, prior to submission to the CISO. Requests for exception shall be evaluated and decided
        upon by the CISO, and the requesting party informed of the action taken. An exception cannot be
        processed unless all residual risks have been identified and the agency head has approved,
        indicating acceptance of these risks. Denied exception requests may be appealed to the CIO of
        the Commonwealth.
