1.    PURPOSE
      This Policy defines the information security management authorities, roles and
      responsibilities of the New Jersey Executive Branch officers, departments, and agencies.

2.    AUTHORITY
      N.J.S.A C.52:18A-224, et. seq. (The Office of Information Technology Reorganization Act);
      New Jersey Executive Order #5 (Corzine, 3/6/2006).
      New Jersey Executive Order #178 (Christie, 5/20/2015).
      New Jersey Executive Order #44 (Murphy, 11/1/18).
      New Jersey Executive Order #404 (Murphy 11/18/26).
      The Critical Infrastructure Information Act of 2002, 6 U.S.C. §133, et. seq.

3.    SCOPE
      All Executive Branch departments and State agencies are directed to cooperate fully with
      the provisions of this Policy so as to ensure effective use of IT within the Executive Branch
      of State Government.

4.    POLICY
      The Chief Technology Officer (CTO) of the New Jersey Office of Information Technology
      (NJOIT) and the Director of the New Jersey Office of Homeland Security and Preparedness
      (NJOHSP) jointly establish the Information Security Governance Committee and a
      management structure for information security across the Executive Branch of New Jersey
      State government.

5.      ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
        NJOHSP
        NJOHSP shall be responsible for administering, coordinating, and leading, New Jersey's
        counterterrorism, counterintelligence, cybersecurity, and related preparedness efforts,
        with the goal of unifying prevention, mitigation, response, and recovery activities across
        federal, state, and local governments, other jurisdictions, non-profit organizations, and
        the private sector to safeguard the people of New Jersey. In addition, the Director shall
        be responsible for overseeing the strategic development, execution, and management of
        an effective and efficient whole-of-state cybersecurity program. This includes:
               a)      Overseeing the response to information security incidents;
               b)      Staying abreast of cybersecurity threats; and
               c)      Advising the Domestic Security Preparedness Task Force (DSPTF) through
                       regular briefings and reports.

        NJOIT
        NJOIT is responsible for providing and maintaining the IT infrastructure of the Executive
        Branch, including all ancillary departments and agencies. The CTO is responsible for
        coordinating and conducting all Executive Branch IT operations. The CTO directs the
        planning, implementation, and governance of enterprise IT systems in support of the
        Executive Branch’s business objectives and operations to improve cost-effectiveness,
        service quality, and mission development.

        At the CTO's direction, NJOIT fulfills the following responsibilities in support of the State's
        Information Security Program:
            a) Design, acquisition, and implementation of enterprise IT systems in compliance
               with the SISM;
            b) Operation and support of IT systems in compliance with approved security
               procedures, including, but not limited to:
               1. IT asset management;
               2. Malware protection;
               3. Patch management;
               4. Web proxying and Content filtering;
               5. Secure file exchange; and
               6. Data encryption;
            c) Management of third parties providing managed information services to NJOIT
               and other State Entities;
            d) Identity and access management;
            e) Disaster recovery planning and operations;

            f) Providing recommendations on policy and control enhancements to NJOHSP's
               Division of Cybersecurity;
            g) Monitoring NJOIT's IT environment to identify, contain, or eliminate unauthorized
               activity;
            h) Assisting in implementing the Information Security Incident Response Plan;
            i) Providing subject-matter expertise for technical issues regarding information
               security; and
            j) Executing day-to-day security management of enterprise information, systems,
               and solutions through the application of controls as defined within the SISM.

        NJ Innovation Authority (NJIA)
        The State Chief Innovation Officer (CIO) leads NJIA with the goal of government
        modernization via data, AI, and user-centered design. The CIO advises the Executive
        Branch on technological trends and collaborates with State agencies to design user-
        friendly digital tools for residents and businesses.

        The CIO carries out their duties through the following initiatives and programs of work:
           a) Leading both the resident experience initiative to simplify access to benefits and
              the business experience initiative to streamline starting/operating businesses;
           b) Utilizing data analytics, AI, and performance metrics to enhance decision-making
              and improve government transparency;
           c) Providing expert advice on technology and innovation to the Governor's office and
              state agencies;
           d) Developing services tailored to the needs of New Jerseyans, focusing on
              accessibility and ease of use; and
           e) Overseeing the strategic direction of the NJIA.

        With respect to information security, the CIO shall:
           a) Coordinate with the State CISO and NJCCIC on the cybersecurity implications of
               statewide AI initiatives and emerging technology deployments;
           b) Ensure that AI systems developed or procured through the NJIA are subject to the
               AI governance requirements; and
           c) Participate in the AI production use approval process established in AI-09 (SISM).

        State Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and Director of the New Jersey
        Cybersecurity and Communications Integration Cell (NJCCIC)
        The State CISO and Director of the NJCCIC reports to the Director of NJOHSP and serves
        as head of NJOHSP's Division of Cybersecurity. The State CISO/Director of the NJCCIC
        shall develop, execute, and manage a whole-of-state cybersecurity program The State
        CISO/Director of the NJCCIC has overall responsibility for the development,
        implementation, and performance of the information security program by:

            a) Developing the State Cybersecurity Strategic Plan and setting strategic information
               security direction across the Executive Branch of State Government;
            b) Publishing and maintaining the SISM; Establishing the standards for, and approving
               the selection of, enterprise cybersecurity tools, technologies, and services to ensure
               interoperability, consistent security assurance, and alignment with the State
               Information Security Manual (SISM) across the Executive Branch.
            c)
            d) Developing, managing, and executing the Statewide Information Security Incident
               Response Plan and providing cybersecurity response assistance and subject-matter
               expertise;
            e) Supporting State agencies in achieving their business goals and objectives by
               identifying security requirements that appropriately balance risk mitigation with
               operational needs;
            f) Providing information security subject matter expertise and recommending best
               practices on cybersecurity and resilience measures to public and private entities;
            g) Developing and implementing a Security Awareness and Training program for State
               agencies and providing cyber threat intelligence reports, briefings, alerts, and
               trainings to public and private organizations;
            h) Providing security metrics to track the performance of the information security
               program;
            i) Leading a cybersecurity information sharing and analysis organization to
               coordinate with CISA, other federal agencies, and public and private sector entities,
               and promoting shared situational awareness;
            j) Implementing and monitoring a centralized Security Information and Event
               Management (SIEM) system and identifying, containing, or eliminating
               unauthorized activity and other cyber threats;
            k) Developing and implementing a cybersecurity threat information exchange with
               appropriate sources including public utilities, critical infrastructure, and private
               industry;
            l) Developing working relationships with external organizations including law
               enforcement, the private sector, academia, Information Sharing and Analysis
               Organizations and Centers, and regulatory authorities;
            m) Providing threat, risk, and major incident reports to the New Jersey Domestic
               Security Preparedness Task Force (DSPTF);
            n) Maintaining fiscal accountability for the Division of Cybersecurity's budget and
               resources;
            o) Managing cybersecurity grant programs, including the State and Local
               Cybersecurity Grant Program (SLCGP), to support cybersecurity improvements
               across state and local government; and
            p) Developing an Information Security Governance, Risk, and Compliance program,
               including:

                   i. Coordinating and conducting compliance and risk assessments of agencies
                      and their information assets;
                 ii.  Conducting and managing vulnerability assessments and penetration tests
                      of agency networks, applications, databases, and systems; and
            q) Managing the New Jersey Civilian Cyber Resilience Corps, a DSPTF-authorized
               volunteer cybersecurity force that provides surge capacity and specialized expertise
               to support government entities, critical infrastructure providers, and community
               organizations across the State as part of the NJCCIC's whole-of-state cybersecurity
               mission.
            r) Conducting information security risk assessments of third parties with access to
               State of New Jersey information assets.

        New Jersey State Privacy Officer
        The New Jersey State Privacy Officer reports to the CTO and serves as the central point of
        leadership, coordination, and oversight of agency privacy programs. At the CTO’s
        direction, the State Privacy Officer coordinates the planning, implementation, and
        management of enterprise privacy initiatives. They are also responsible for the oversight
        of the State’s compliance with all applicable State and federal laws and regulations
        regarding the collection, use, sharing, and disposal of personally identifiable information.

        The State Privacy Officer:
           a) Ensures that the public has access to information about the State’s privacy activities
               and can communicate with its officials for privacy;
           b) Ensures that the State’s privacy practices and reports are publicly available; and
           c) Utilizes publicly-facing email addresses and/or phone lines to enable the public to
               provide feedback and/or direct questions to privacy offices.

        Heads of State Agencies (Including Secretaries, Directors, Commissioners,
        Chairpersons, or Equivalent Head of State Entity Within the Executive Branch of
        State Government)
        Heads of State Agencies (includes Secretaries, Directors, Commissioners, Chairpersons, or
        equivalent head of a state entity within the Executive Branch of State Government) are
        responsible for their respective agency's operations. Likewise, they are responsible for the
        overall protection and use of information assets owned, managed, or licensed by the
        agency. To these ends, they are charged with:
           a) Driving commitment and support for the information security program;
           b) Accepting risk on behalf of the agency;
           c) Assigning appropriate IT management responsibilities within their respective
               agency to a designee who has responsibility for the implementation and
               management of information technology systems in support of agency goals and

               objectives, and in accordance with the Executive Branch of State Government
               Statewide Information Security Policies and Standards;
            d) Assigning appropriate responsibilities within their agency to a designee who has
               the authority and responsibility for ensuring the implementation of, and the
               adherence to the Information Security Program;
            e) Promoting adherence to information security policies and cyber awareness
               programs.

        Agency Chief Information Officer
        The Agency Chief Information Officer shall be responsible for the direction, planning, and
        implementation of IT systems in support of agency business goals and objectives. In
        accordance with NJOIT standards and strategy, the agency Chief Information Officer
        directs the planning and implementation of the agency's IT systems.

        The officers have the following responsibilities in support of the State’s information
        security program:
           a) Design, acquisition, implementation, and operation of IT systems in compliance
               with approved policies and standards;
           b) Operation and support of IT systems in compliance with approved security
               procedures, including, but not limited to: IT asset management, malware
               protection, patch management, and data encryption;
           c) Management of third parties that provide managed information services to the
               agency;
           d) Identity and access management;
           e) Disaster recovery planning and operations in coordination with NJOIT;
           f) Providing policy and control enhancement recommendations to NJOHSP's
               Division of Cybersecurity;
           g) Monitoring the agency IT environment and, where appropriate, identifying,
               containing, and eliminating unauthorized activity;
           h) Assisting in the implementation of the Information Security Incident Response
               Plan;
           i) Executing the day-to-day security management of information, systems, and
               solutions through the application of controls as defined within the SISM;
           j) Providing subject-matter expertise for technical issues regarding information
               security.

        Agency Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
        The Agency CISO shall be responsible for protecting and maintaining the confidentiality,
        integrity, and availability of information assets under their purview.

        The Agency CISO fulfills the following responsibilities in support of the SISM:

            a. Identifying security requirements to limit cyber risks associated with the agency's
               business goals and objectives;
            b. Implementing and promoting information security awareness within their
               respective agency;
            c. Ensuring compliance with the SISM within their respective State agency,
               including, but not limited to:
                   1. Coordination of risk assessments and compliance audits with NJOHSP's
                      Division of Cybersecurity;
                   2. Coordination of vulnerability assessments of agency networks,
                      applications, databases, and systems; and
                   3. Coordination of risk assessments of third parties having access to agency
                      information assets;
            d. Assisting in the implementation of the Information Security Incident Response
               Plan; and
            e. Reporting all information on security incidents to the NJCCIC.

        Agency Privacy Officer
        The Agency Privacy Officer is responsible for the oversight of the agency’s compliance
        with all applicable State and federal laws and regulations regarding the collection, use,
        maintenance, sharing, and disposal of personally identifiable information.

        Agency Artificial Intelligence (AI) Governance Officer
        The Agency AI Governance Officer is responsible for overseeing the Agency's AI risk
        management program and ensuring AI systems are developed, deployed, and used in
        accordance with the SISM and all applicable requirements. Responsibilities include:
           a) Establishing and maintaining the agency's AI governance policies and procedures;
           b) Maintaining AI system inventory and ensuring AI systems are properly classified;
           c) Coordinating AI risk assessments and ensuring risks are appropriately managed;
           d) Providing guidance to agency personnel on acceptable AI use and trustworthy AI
              requirements;
           e) Ensuring AI systems meet transparency, accountability, and human oversight
              requirements;
           f) Coordinating with the Agency CISO, Privacy Officer, and legal counsel on AI-related
              matters;
           g) Reporting on AI governance activities to agency leadership; and
           h) Serving as the primary point of contact for AI governance matters with NJOIT and
              NJCCIC.
