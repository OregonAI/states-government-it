8-213. International travel.
    International travel increases cyber risks to the information technology infrastructure of the
State of Nebraska. These risks include the use of unsecured public Wi-Fi, device loss, data loss,
and cyber espionage. To mitigate these risks, anyone traveling outside the legal jurisdictional
boundary of the United States (outside of the United States, its territories, embassies, or military
installations) must comply with cybersecurity best practices. The following are requirements
when traveling internationally:
   (1) If traveling with a state-issued device, you must contact the Office of the CIO at least 72
hours prior to departure. In part, this is to avoid service disruptions and international data charges
on mobile devices;
    (2) All state-issued devices must be factory reset, or wiped, upon return. The agency may
contact the Office of the CIO to obtain temporary loaner equipment to be used while traveling
internationally;
    (3) Always maintain positive control over devices. Never leave a device unattended and do
not place in checked baggage;
     (4) Multi-factor authentication must be used to access state resources;
     (5) OCIO-approved VPN must be used to access state resources;
    (6) State-issued devices must have full disk encryption enable to prevent unauthorized access
to your data in case a device is lost or stolen;
   (7) Software and operating systems must be patched and up to date with the latest patches
and updates to mitigate known vulnerabilities and limit cyber risk exposure and attack surface;
    (8) User passwords should be changed before leaving to ensure they do not expire while
traveling. User passwords must be changed after returning to ensure that they are safe in the
event that any may have become compromised while traveling;
    (9) Only certain state personnel will be able to access OWA or other cloud-based resources
when traveling internationally. This means that email on mobile devices may not update
automatically; and
   (10) Based on the level of threat and risk from the region of travel, the state information
security officer may restrict access to certain state resources.
