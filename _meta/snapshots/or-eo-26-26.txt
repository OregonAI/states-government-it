EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. 26-26

ESTABLISHING RESPONSIBLE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
PROCUREMENT STANDARDS FOR STATE GOVERNMENT.

TO MY AUTHORITY AS THE GOVERNOR OF THE STATE OF OREGON, I
FIND THAT:

WHEREAS, artificial intelligence technologies are developing at an extraordinary
pace and are increasingly capable of performing tasks that affect individuals,
communities, businesses, workers, and governments; and

WHEREAS, the development and deployment of increasingly capable artificial
intelligence systems also present potential risks to privacy, cybersecurity, public
safety, civil rights, economic security, workforce demand, and the public interest;
and

WHEREAS, the rapid development of frontier artificial intelligence models is
outpacing the development of comprehensive oversight, evaluation, and
accountability structures necessary to ensure that these technologies are developed
and deployed responsibly; and

WHEREAS, the federal government has an important role to play in establishing
clear and effective national standards for the safety and security of frontier artificial
intelligence systems, including through independent evaluation, oversight, and
accountability; and

WHEREAS, President Donald Trump has put up no barriers to the rapid,
unregulated growth of artificial intelligence in the United States, and demonstrates
a lack of leadership or understanding of the impacts that this technology is having
across the country and in Oregon; and

WHEREAS, Congress should, within its authority, conduct hearings and oversight
concerning the safety and security of increasingly capable artificial intelligence
systems and consider appropriate federal standards and safeguards; and

WHEREAS, states have the responsibility to protect the safety, security, privacy,
rights, and economic interests of its residents and businesses while supporting
responsible innovation and the appropriate use of emerging technologies; and

WHEREAS, Oregon has previously taken action to establish responsible artificial
intelligence practices in state government, including through Executive Order 23-
26, which established the State Government Artificial Intelligence Advisory
Council and resulted in recommendations for the awareness, education, and use of
artificial intelligence in state government consistent with Oregon’s policies, goals,
and values; and

WHEREAS, independent review, testing, auditing, verification, and other forms of
third-party assessment are proven tools for promoting safety, reliability, and
accountability across complex and consequential industries; and

WHEREAS, independent evaluation can provide government and the public with
additional information about the capabilities, limitations, risks, and safety practices
associated with increasingly capable artificial intelligence systems; and

WHEREAS, the State of Oregon should use its purchasing and contracting
authority to encourage responsible and accountable development and deployment
of artificial intelligence technologies; and

WHEREAS, Oregon can benefit from the expertise of artificial intelligence
auditors, independent verification organizations, academic institutions, private-
sector developers and operators, other states, consumer protection and labour
organizations, civil society, and relevant federal, state, local, national, and
international organizations in developing such standards;

NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY DIRECTED AND ORDERED

e It is the policy of the State of Oregon that, in the interest of the safety and
security of the people of Oregon, while supporting innovation and the
responsible use of artificial intelligence, the State shall seek to purchase,
contract for, or otherwise utilize frontier artificial intelligence models that
have received independent, third-party review for artificial intelligence
safety, consistent with applicable state and federal law.

e The State Chief Information Officer shall, within 90 days of the date of this
Executive Order, develop and submit to the Governor a proposal for
implementing the policy established in this Executive Order. The proposal
shall address, at a minimum:

o A definition of “frontier artificial intelligence model” appropriate for
application to state government procurement and use;

o Development of standards or criteria for determining whether an
artificial intelligence model has received adequate independent, third-
party review for artificial intelligence safety;

o Evaluation of the viability of a “kill switch” requirement as one of the
standards for frontier artificial intelligence models;

o Development of appropriate methods for evaluating the independence,
qualifications, scope, methodology, and transparency of third-party
reviewers;

o Processes for incorporating artificial intelligence safety reviews into
state procurement, contracting, and technology-use practices;

o Appropriate exceptions or alternative requirements when an applicable
third-party review is unavailable, including circumstances involving
emerging technologies, specialized applications, or state or public safety
needs;

o Procedures for ongoing monitoring and reevaluation as artificial
intelligence models, capabilities, risks, and independent review
standards evolve;

| o Recommendations for transparency and accountability concerning the

| State’s procurement and use of frontier artificial intelligence models;
and

o Any statutory, administrative, budgetary, or policy changes necessary to

| implement the proposal.

| e Upon approval and direction by the Governor, the State Chief Information

| Officer shall coordinate with state agencies to implement the approved

| policy, consistent with applicable state and federal law. State agencies shall

cooperate with requests and provide information reasonably necessary to
respond to this order.

This Executive Order is effective immediately and shall remain in effect until
terminated by the Governor. The Governor shall reassess the necessity of this
Executive Order every three months and shall retain, modify, or supplement it
as circumstances warrant.

Done at Salem, Oregon, this 23" day of September, 2026.
