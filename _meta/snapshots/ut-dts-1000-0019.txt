Dissemination

Vendors providing the State of Utah Executive Branch Agencies any X as a Service goods must maintain a minimum service level or better.

1.0 Purpose

This policy defines the minimum Service Level Agreement for XaaS vendors.

1.1 Background

When a vendor is contracted with the State of Utah to provide XaaS products, they must be able to adhere to some minimum industry standards for system availability and response times.

1.2 Scope

This policy applies to all vendors providing the State of Utah Executive Branch Agencies any X as a Service goods.

1.3 Exceptions

None

2.0 Policy

2.1 SYSTEM AVAILABILITY

• The Contractor’s system shall be available 24 hours per Day, 365 Days per year, excluding agreed upon maintenance downtime (“System Availability”).
• If the monthly System Availability averages less than 99.5% (excluding agreed-upon maintenance downtime), the State of Utah shall be entitled to recover damages, apply credits, or use other contractual remedies, including those set forth in the Statement of Work.
• If the monthly System Availability averages less than 99.5% (excluding agreed-upon maintenance downtime) for 3 or more months in a rolling 12-month period, the State may terminate the Contract for material breach.
• Contractor shall provide at least ten business days’ advance written notice to the State of any major upgrades or changes that will affect the System Availability.

2.2 CONTRACTOR RESPONSE TIMES

• Contractor will respond to the State of Utah's requests for support services when service interruptions occur in accordance with the procedures identified below. In each case, the State of Utah may describe and submit the problem by telephone, email, or online helpdesk/service desk tool. The State of Utah will determine the severity level. The severity level will not be changed unless agreed to by the State of Utah and Contractor.

Incident Severity | Response Time | Resolution Time
High Impact System is unusable | 1 hour | Final resolution within 4 hours
Medium Impact System is useable with severely restricted functionality or performance | 4 hours | Final resolution within one business day/8 hours
Low Impact System is useable with minor impact on functionality or performance | 8 hours | Final resolution with one business week/40 hours

• Contractor's failure to meet the above defined response times or resolution times in any given month during the term and any renewal term shall be deemed a service level default (“Service Level Default”) and the State of Utah may obtain the non-exclusive remedies set forth below.

Response and Resolution Levels (calculated monthly on a per-incident basis) | Service Level Credits (percentage of monthly fees paid to Contractor)
1st failure to meet response or resolution time | 5%
2nd failure to meet response or resolution time | 10%
3rd failure to meet response or resolution time | 15%

• Credits shall be applied against the next invoice. In the event a service level default occurs after a party has given notice of termination, or the State of Utah has made final payment to Contractor and no further invoices shall issue as a result, Contractor shall refund to the State of Utah the amount of the appropriate service level credit due for the period of default.
• For any performance measurement not met during the monthly reporting period, the SLA credit for that individual measurement shall be applied to the monthly charges.
• Service credits will be cumulative for each missed service requirement. The State of Utah, at its option, for amounts due to the State as service credits, may deduct such from any money payable to the Contractor or may bill the Contractor as a separate item. In the event of a catastrophic failure affecting all services or the entire system, in addition to all other rights and remedies available to the State, all applicable service level credits shall be credited to the State.
• In addition, for each High Impact event, the affected parties will perform a root cause analysis and institute a process of problem management to prevent recurrence of the issue.
• The State and the Contractor agree that under no circumstances shall the imposition of service level credits be construed as the State’s sole or exclusive remedy for any failure to meet the service levels to which they relate, and do not preclude additional remedies by the State for damages related to such failures.

2.2 MEASURING AND REPORTING SERVICE LEVELS

• The Contractor will implement and utilize the necessary measurement and monitoring tools and procedures required to measure and report the system’s performance against the applicable service levels at no cost to the State. Such measurement and monitoring will permit reporting at a level of detail sufficient to verify compliance with the service levels and will be subject to audit by the State. The Contractor will provide the State with information and/or access to such tools and procedures upon request, for purposes of verification. In addition, the Contractor will make available to the State any data in the Contractor’s possession regarding measurements taken by the Contractor with respect to any service levels.
• For the avoidance of doubt, if Contractor breaches its performance obligations under this Contract, the fact that such breach did not cause Contractor to miss the applicable service level shall not relieve Contractor of responsibility for such breach or any resulting damages.
