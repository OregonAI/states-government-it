1.0.      Document Purpose
          The purpose of this document is to define the State of Maine procedures for
          implementing system and communications protection requirements for specific
          technologies that support the State’s information technology environment. These
          procedures outline how OIT applies required safeguards to ensure the
          confidentiality, integrity, and availability of State information assets when using
          these technologies. This document corresponds to the System and
          Communications Protection (SC) Control Family of National Institute of Standards
          and Technology (NIST) Special Publication 800-53 (Rev. 5).1
2.0.      Scope
2.1.      These procedures apply to State of Maine employees and contractors (collectively
          referred to as personnel in this document) with access to:
          2.1.1.      Executive Branch Agency information assets, irrespective of location;
                      and
          2.1.2.      Information assets from other State government branches that use
                      Executive Branch-managed services.
3.0.      Procedure Conflict
          If these procedures conflict with any law or union contract in effect, the terms of
          the existing law or contract prevail.

8.0.      Procedures
          The following standards apply to and represent the security controls established
          to meet an acceptable level of protection for the State of Maine's information
          assets supported by the specific technologies addressed in this document.
8.1.      SC Controls, Cross References: The following controls for the System and
          Communications Protection Policy and Procedures (SC) are published in separate
          policy and procedure documents:
          8.1.1.      System and Communications Protection Policy and Procedures (SC);4
          8.1.2.      System and Communications Protection Procedures for Defense in
                      Depth (SC-2, 4, 5, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28, 32, and 39);5 and
          8.1.3.      System and Communications Protection Procedures for Encryption
                      Mechanisms (SC-12, 13, and 17).6
          8.1.4.      System and Communications Protection controls applicable exclusively
                      to Federal Tax Information (FTI), including Voice over Internet Protocol
                      (SC-19), are addressed in IRS Publication 1075–scoped documentation
                      and are not implemented within the general System and
                      Communications Protection procedures.
8.2.      Network Disconnect (SC-10)
          8.2.1.      The OIT Network Services team ensures Virtual Private Network (VPN)
                      connections associated with a communications session are:
                      8.2.1.1.       Terminated at the end of a session when the user
                                     disconnects.
                      8.2.1.2.       Automatically deallocate inactive Dynamic Host
                                     Configuration Protocol (DHCP) (see Definition) leases after
                                     seven (7) days. An active Internet Protocol (IP) address may
                                     be reallocated as long as the device is in session.

8.3.      Collaborative Computing Devices (SC-15)
          8.3.1.      OIT Client Technologies prohibits remote activation of collaborative
                      computing devices (see Definitions), for example, networked
                      whiteboards, cameras, and microphones.
          8.3.2.      OIT Client Technologies provides explicit indication of use to users
                      physically present at the collaborative computing devices. Explicit
                      indication of use includes, for example, signals to users when
                      collaborative computing devices are activated.
          8.3.3.      Physical Disconnect (SC-15(1)): OIT Client Technologies ensures
                      collaborative computing devices are automatically disconnected when a
                      session ends to ensure participant disconnection.
          8.3.4.      OIT Client Technologies disables remote access to collaborative
                      computing devices from information systems or information system
                      components through VPN Deny and Active Directory to prevent, for
                      example, eavesdropping on conversations.
          8.3.5.      Explicitly Indicate Current Participants (SC-15(4)): Signaling local
                      users is done to show those who are physically present at their cameras
                      when collaborative computing devices are in use.
8.4.      Mobile Code (SC-18)
          8.4.1.      OIT Security, as well as all the Information Asset Owners, define and
                      establish usage, restrictions, and guidance for mobile code technologies
                      (see Definitions). They authorize, monitor, and control mobile code
                      technologies within the information system through OIT’s Change
                      Management Policy and Procedures.7 The acquisition, development, and
                      use of mobile code must comply with all regulatory requirements
                      applicable to the data handled by the user.

12.0.     Public Records Exceptions
          Under the Maine Freedom of Access Act (FOAA),13 certain public records
          exceptions may limit disclosure of agency records related to information
          technology infrastructure and systems, as well as security plans, procedures, or
          risk assessments. Information contained in these records may be disclosed to the
          Maine State Legislature or, in the case of a political or administrative subdivision,
          to municipal officials or board members under conditions that protect the
          information from further disclosure. Any aggrieved person seeking relief for an
          alleged violation of the FOAA may bring suit in any Superior Court in the State.
13.0.     Definitions
13.1.     Asymmetric Key Cryptography: A cryptographic system where users have a private
          key that is kept secret and used to generate a public key (which is freely provided
          to others). Users can digitally sign data with their private key, and the resulting
          signature can be verified by anyone using the corresponding public key. Also
          known as public-key cryptography. Source: NIST.14
13.2.     Certificate Authority: A trusted entity that issues and revokes public key
          certificates. Source: NIST.15
13.3.     External-facing Applications: Applications that are open to the internet and
          provide content to public users, internal employees, and business partners.
13.4.     Lookout: A data protection and cloud security platform.
13.5.     Media Player (MP): Software that "plays" audio, video, or animation files on a
          computer. Windows Media Player is the default player from Microsoft, but iTunes,
          RealPlayer, and other software are also widely used. iTunes and QuickTime Player
          are the default products for Mac devices.
13.6.     Microsoft Intune: A cloud-based service that focuses on mobile device
          management and mobile application management for mobile phones, tablets, and
          laptops (part of Microsoft Endpoint Manager).
13.7.     Principle of Least Privilege: A security principle where users are assigned the
          minimal access necessary to perform their job responsibilities. Access is granted
          for the shortest duration possible.

13.8.     Private Key: A cryptographic key, used with a public key cryptographic algorithm,
          that is uniquely associated with an entity and is not made public. Source: NIST.16
13.9.     Public Key Infrastructure (PKI): The architecture, organization, techniques,
          practices, and procedures that collectively support the implementation and
          operation of a certificate-based public key cryptographic system. Framework
          established to issue, maintain, and revoke public key certificates. Source: NIST.17
13.10.    Traffic Light Protocol (TLP): A standardized information-sharing protocol used to
          indicate the sensitivity of information and the extent to which it may be shared.
          TLP markings are used to guide the handling, dissemination, and protection of
          information and may be applied to a wide range of information types, including
          but not limited to sensitive or restricted data. Within the State of Maine, the
          Federal Homeland Security Traffic Light Protocol is used by OIT to support the
          classification of Personally Identifiable Information (PII) impact level. See the Data
          Classification Policy.18
13.11.    Trust Anchor: An authoritative public key or certificate authority that is explicitly
          trusted to establish and validate trust relationships within a public key
          infrastructure. Trust anchors are typically root certificate authority certificates
          and are used to validate certificate chains within trust stores.
13.12.    Trust Store: A list of root, intermediate, and user certificates that are trusted by the
          operating system or application.
13.13.    Unencrypted Transmission: A communication method that does not meet
          encryption requirements as outlined in the Transmission of Sensitive Information
          Standard. For example, confidential or sensitive data may be intercepted by a
          malicious user if plaintext data is transmitted across an unencrypted network,
          allowing them to gain unauthorized access. This method can jeopardize the
          confidentiality of the sensitive data.
