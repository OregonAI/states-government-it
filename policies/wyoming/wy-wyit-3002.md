# Security Exception Request (WyIT 3002)

## Full text

             SECURITY EXCEPTION REQUEST
                                    WyIT 3002​

PURPOSE: To provide a method of documenting a security exception request that
deviates from state enterprise best practice methodologies.

 I.​ APPLICABILITY

     This policy applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions
     staff (collectively referred to as “agencies”). This policy is also applicable to
     consultants, affiliates, and temporary employees.​

II.​ DEFINITIONS

    All definitions for this policy are located in the Policy Dictionary.

III.​ EXCEPTIONS

    Any exceptions provided to the following policy must abide by the WyIT - 3002 -
    Security Exception Request policy.

IV.​ REQUIREMENTS

     An exception to a published IT/information security policy, standard, or practice
     may be granted by the CIO in any of the following situations:

     ●​ A temporary exception where immediate compliance would disrupt critical
        operations.
     ●​ A legacy system is retiring, and compliance is not possible (risk must be
        managed).
     ●​ A long-term exception where compliance would adversely impact the State of

        Wyoming business.
      ●​ Where compliance would cause a major adverse financial impact that would
         not be offset by the reduced risk occasioned by compliance (i.e., the cost to
         comply offsets the risk of non-compliance).​

V.​ TERM OF REQUESTS​

     ●​ Exceptions are reviewed on a case-by-case basis and approval is not
        automatic.
     ●​ Granted exceptions will not exceed one year.
     ●​ Extensions may be requested upon expiration. ​

     A determination regarding any requested exception request will be provided by
     the CIO as soon as reasonably possible. More complex requests may receive a
     determination that further investigation is necessary. The CIO will evaluate
     exception requests on a case-by-case basis, accounting for the level of risk,
     potential threats and vulnerabilities, and operational and technical limitations or
     constraints. Additional stakeholders and subject matter experts may be included
     during the evaluation process, but the CIO will have final responsibility and
     accountability for approving or denying a request for an exception. The CIO may
     grant an exception while working with the requestor to establish a timeline for full
     compliance. Exceptions are valid for the agreed-upon timeline but will not exceed
     one year. If there is a continuing need for an exception, the requestor must
     request an extension to the exception at least 30 days prior to its expiration. If
     there is a pattern of requested exceptions for a particular solution, the CIO and/or
     CIO may recommend alternative solutions.

VI.​ REQUIRED INFORMATION

     Exception requests must be submitted on a completed Exception Request
     Form and include:
     ●​ The name and contact information of the agency Director making the request.
        (all requests must be submitted by an agency Director or designee).
     ●​ A description of the technology, process, and standard the exception is being
        requested for.
     ●​ A detailed description of the specific device, application, or service the
        exception is being requested for.

       ●​ The alternative security solution proposed as part of the request.
       ●​ The type of data affected, directly or indirectly, and any statute or compliance
          related to the request.
       ●​ The business impact if this request were denied.
       ●​ Acknowledgment that the agency Director considered and fully understood
          the associated internal and external risks required to support the request.
       ●​ Acknowledgment that the agency Director accepts the risks associated with
          the request.​

VII.​ REVOKING REQUESTS

       The exception request process is intended to be a generic method applying to all
       IT information security policies and standards. Exception requests may be revoked
       in the event of a security incident or policy violation using established incident
       response procedures.
