Scope:
This policy establishes requirements for assessing potential exposure of sensitive information,
activating appropriate response procedures, ensuring Governor's Office notification, and coordinating
decisions regarding individual notification. This policy serves as a triage and coordination framework
that triggers existing incident response and security threat response procedures established in WyIT
3010, Incident Response and WyIT 3009, Computer Security Threat Response.

This policy addresses the privacy-specific assessment requirements for determining sensitive
information exposure and establishes coordination protocols with the Governor's Office and legal
counsel for notification decisions. This policy supplements existing incident response procedures and
does not replace technical response or security coordination established in other WyIT policies.

This policy does not change Agency responsibilities with respect to regulations that govern data
exchanges, data privacy and data security requirements, or Agency responsibilities under Wyoming
State Statute 16-4-202. No part of this policy is intended to supersede or supplant relevant federal and
state controlling laws, rules, or regulations.

Audience
This policy applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions staff (collectively
referred to as "agencies"). This policy is also applicable to consultants, affiliates, third party vendors
and temporary employees that use or store data.

Key Definitions, Acronyms, and Terms

Authority
This policy is promulgated under the authority of Wyoming State Statute 9-2-2906 and its subsections, the
Department of Enterprise Technology Services is required to adopt and enforce information technology
standards, and promote efficient use of information technology throughout state
government.
​
This policy is further promulgated under the authority of Wyoming State Statute 9-21-101, which
authorizes the State CIO to adopt and enforce data security policies, establish standards for enterprise
data analytics, and require every agency to adopt, enforce and maintain data governance.

It also requires every Agency to adopt, enforce and maintain data collection, access, and security
policies that adhere to the standards established by the Office of the Chief Information Officer (OCIO).

Responsibilities

Enterprise Technology Services (ETS) / Information Technology (IT) Responsibilities:

   ●​ Provide technical assistance for breach assessment when requested by agencies.
   ●​ Maintain coordination capabilities through incidents escalated.

                                                 4

   ●​ Support multi-agency incident coordination through established CCERT procedures when
      activated.

Agency Responsibilities:

   ●​ Designate and maintain an Agency Official for Privacy (AOP).
   ●​ Ensure immediate reporting of suspected sensitive data or information, regardless of format,
      structure, or formal asset designation exposure to the AOP.
   ●​ Coordinate with the Governor's Office for confirmed breach notification within required
      timeframes.
   ●​ Ensure AOP coordination with agency head or delegate and others as appropriate for
      notification determinations.
   ●​ Integrate breach assessment procedures with existing incident response plans.

User Responsibilities:

   ●​ Report suspected breaches or potential exposure of sensitive data or information, regardless of
      format, structure, or formal asset designation immediately to designated agency contacts.
   ●​ Cooperate with breach assessment activities as directed by the AOP.
   ●​ Follow agency procedures for handling and protecting sensitive data or information, regardless
      of format, structure, or formal asset designation to prevent exposure.

Compliance & Exceptions:
Compliance
Employees who violate this policy may be subject to appropriate disciplinary action up to and including
discharge as well as both civil and criminal penalties. Non-employees, including, without limitation,
contractors, may be subject to termination of contractual agreements, denial of access to IT resources,
and other actions as well as both civil and criminal penalties.

Exceptions
Exceptions may be requested for transfers and storage of Wyoming government information outside
the United States and its territories as defined in 8 U.S.C. § 1101 through the Data and Privacy
Exception Request process WyIT 3002, Exception Request. Exceptions will only be permitted if
recipient countries ensure protection in accordance with the level of confidentiality of the information to
be transferred as determined through the exception process.

No exceptions shall be given for the unencrypted transfer of sensitive information. All exceptions for
alternative encryption methods should be requested in accordance with WyIT 3002, Security Exception
Request. Any request made to the Chief Information Officer (CIO) that does not meet the equivalent
security standards, may not be approved.

The request should specifically state the scope of the exception, along with justification for granting the
exception, the potential impact or risk if the exception is not granted, risk mitigation measures to be
undertaken by the agency, initiatives, actions and a time-frame for achieving the minimum compliance
level with the policies set forth herein.

Any exception to established policy shall be defined and tracked as a risk to the state and established
mitigating controls monitored by the agency.

Responsible Department / Measurements

Compliance Measurements:

   1.​ Breach assessment completion within required timeframes (95% target).
   2.​ Governor's Office notification compliance for confirmed breaches (100% target).
   3.​ AOP designation across all agencies (100% target)
   4.​ Integration of breach assessment procedures in agency incident response plans (100% target)
