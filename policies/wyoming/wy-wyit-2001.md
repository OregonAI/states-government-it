# Data Classification (WyIT 2001)

## At a glance

WyIT 2001, Data Classification: a statewide policy in the Enterprise Technology Services policy library. Per the Applicability section of the file it applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards and commissions staff and to consultants, affiliates and temporary employees.

Scope:
This policy governs proper information classification, protection, and handling of State of Wyoming
information, regardless of format (e.g., written and/or electronic), using a domain-based approach. It
establishes the foundation for protecting information based on sensitivity, ensuring regulatory
compliance, and implementing appropriate controls throughout the information lifecycle.

This policy serves as the state baseline. Agencies may implement more restrictive policies to meet
applicable state and federal regulations or agency-specific requirements, provided they do not deviate
substantially from this policy. In lieu of agency-specific policies, this policy shall serve as the governing
standard.

Authority
This policy is promulgated under the authority of Wyoming State Statute 9-2-2906 and its subsections,
the Department of Enterprise Technology Services is required to adopt and enforce information
technology standards, and promote efficient use of information technology throughout state
government.

Specifically, this policy is further promulgated under the authority of the below listed Wyoming State
Statute subsections.

   ●​ Implements and maintains an information technology governance program for state agencies
      (W.S 9-2-2906,(b), (ii));
   ●​ Establishes and enforces data security and privacy policies and standards for the state data
      infrastructure Establishes review criteria for the agencies information technology projects,
      procurements and purchases. (W.S 9-2-2906,(b), (iii)); and

                                                         ​
Adopts information technology policies and standards and ensures agency compliance with those
policies and standards (W.S. 9-2-2906,(b), (vi)).

Audience
This policy applies to all Executive Branch agencies, boards, and commissions staff (collectively
referred to as "agencies"). This policy is also applicable to consultants, affiliates, third party vendors
and temporary employees that use or store state data.

Definitions
Key Definitions, Acronyms, and Terms

Data Classification Levels:

   ●​ Public (Low Sensitivity)

       ○​ Information that poses no risk to the State if made generally available.

       ○​ This includes information intended for public release or already publicly available.

       ○​ Example data types: press releases, public meeting minutes, published reports, agency contact
           information, public-facing website content.

   ●​ Internal (Moderate Sensitivity):
      ○​ Information whose loss, corruption, or unauthorized disclosure is of importance only inside
          the State and, therefore, would not result in a tangible business, financial, or legal loss.

       ○​ This information is not routinely shared outside the State government but is generally
          accessible to State employees.

   ●​ Confidential (High Sensitivity):
      ○​ Information whose loss, corruption, or unauthorized disclosure would seriously harm an
         individual's, business's, or the State's reputation or business position, resulting in severe
         financial and legal loss.

       ○​ Confidential information is subject to controlled access and special handling requirements.

           ○​ Confidential-Standard: Sensitive business or operational information

           ○​ Confidential-Regulated: Information subject to specific regulatory requirements

Policy Statements:
 1.​   Agencies must classify all State of Wyoming data and information, regardless of format,
       structure, or formal asset designation, according to one of the three classification levels defined
       in this policy:

       1.1.​     Public (Low Sensitivity);​

       1.2.​     Internal (Moderate Sensitivity); ​

       1.3.​     Confidential (High Sensitivity);​

       1.4.​     Classification must occur upon data creation, acquisition, or when existing data
                 undergoes significant modification.
       [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]

 2.​   Any agency that collects, uses, or stores information must identify and train a data owner with
       responsibility over that data and the related controls. ​
       [1], [2], [3], [4], [6], [7], [6], [8]

       2.1. Data Owners are responsible for classifying information assets according to their
       sensitivity and applicable regulatory requirements.​
       [1], [3], [4], [5], [8]

 3.​   All data and information classified as Confidential-Regulated must be protected in accordance
       with applicable federal and state regulations. Data owners are responsible for understanding
       applicable regulations, applying and auditing controls, and partnering with Enterprise
       Technology Services (ETS) on technical control implementation.​
       [9], [1], [10], [8], [3], [4], [12], [13]

       3.1. Confidential-Regulated information must not be stored on portable or removable storage
       media, except when specifically authorized in writing by the relevant Agency Head or Program
       Manager. When such storage is approved: ​

                 3.1.1      Each storage device must have a designated and identifiable owner;​

                 3.1.2. The media must be encrypted using FIPS 140-2 validated encryption;​

                 3.1.3. The media must be physically secured at all times; and​

                 3.1.4. A documented inventory of all such media must be maintained.
       [9], [10], [14], [21], [13], [11]

 4.​   Information handling procedures established in related Wyoming policies must be applied
       according to the classification level assigned under this policy, with higher classification levels
       requiring more stringent controls.​
       [1], [22], [23], [24], [25], [21], [26], [3], [4], [5]

 5.​   All employees and affiliates must be trained on their agency's information classification
       procedures and responsibilities for protecting agency information. Data owners must ensure
       that all employees and affiliates with access to data and systems under their authority receive
       training on proper data handling responsibilities.​
       [1], [3], [11], [5]

 6.​   Agencies shall maintain comprehensive data inventories as required by W.S. 9-21-101(a)(i) and
       organize their data and information into logical data domains that group related data types to
       ensure consistent classification decisions and effective governance.​
       [1], [3], [5]

 7.​   When information consists of elements with different classification levels, the entire information
       asset shall be classified at the highest level of any component element (classification
       inheritance).​
       [1], [5]

Compliance & Exceptions:
Compliance
Employees who violate this policy may be subject to appropriate disciplinary action up to and including
discharge as well as both civil and criminal penalties. Non-employees, including, without limitation,
contractors, may be subject to termination of contractual agreements, denial of access to IT resources,
and other actions as well as both civil and criminal penalties.

Exceptions
Requests for exceptions to this policy shall be reviewed by the Chief Information Security Officer
(CISO) and the Chief Information Officer (CIO). Departments requesting exceptions shall provide such
requests to the CIO in accordance with WyIT.3002 Policy Exception Request.

The request should specifically state the scope of the exception along with justification for granting the
exception, the potential impact or risk attendant upon granting the exception, risk mitigation measures
to be undertaken by the IT Department, initiatives, actions and a time-frame for achieving the minimum
compliance level with the policies set forth herein. The CIO shall review such requests; confer with the
requesting department.

Any exception to established policy shall be defined and tracked as a risk to the state and established
mitigating controls monitored by the agency.
