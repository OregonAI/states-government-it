   1.0 Purpose and Benefits
   The purpose of this policy is to outline the process for obtaining an exception
   regarding compliance with a published information security policy or standard
   issued by the New York State (NYS) Office of Information Technology Services
   (ITS).

   2.0 Authority
   Section 103(10) of the State Technology Law provides ITS with the authority to
   establish statewide technology policies, including technology and security
   standards. Section 2 of Executive Order No. 1171, established January 2002,
   provides the State Chief Information Officer with the authority to oversee, direct
   and coordinate the establishment of information technology policies, protocols,
   and standards for State government, including hardware, software, security, and
   business re-engineering. Details regarding this authority can be found in NYS ITS
   Policy, NYS-P08-002 Authority to Establish Enterprise Information Technology
   Policies, Standards, and Guidelines.

   3.0 Scope
   This policy applies to all “State Entities” (SE), defined as “State Government”
   entities in Executive Order 117 1 or “State Agencies” in Section 101 of the State
   Technology Law. This includes employees of the SE and ITS and all third parties
   (such as local governments, consultants, vendors, and contractors) that use or
   access any IT resource for which the SE or ITS has administrative responsibility,
   including systems managed or hosted by third parties on behalf of the SE. While
   an SE may adopt a different policy, it must include the requirements set forth in
   this one. Where a conflict exists between this policy and an SE’s policy, the more
   restrictive requirement will take precedence.

   4.0 Information Statement
   An exception may be granted by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) of
   ITS, or their designee, when there is no legitimate method to accomplish a
   business objective in a manner compliant with NYS security policies and
   standards. Justification for approval of policy or standard exceptions include, but
   are not limited to:

        •     Implementation of a solution with equivalent or superior protection to the
              requirements in the NYS policy or standard issued by the ITS CISO.

        •     Implementation of a solution with equivalent protection to the requirements
              in the CISO policy or standard.

        •     Impending retirement of a system.

        •     Inability to implement the requirements of a policy or standard due to some
              limitation (e.g., technical constraint, business limitation or statutory
              requirement).

   Exceptions are reviewed and considered on a case-by-case basis and approval
   should not be assumed. Exceptions may be denied based upon the risks posed to
   State information technology environments. If the non-compliance with the
   information security policy or standard is due to a superior solution, an exception
   is still required.

   Exceptions that are granted will be for a limited period of time. Upon expiration of
   the exception, a one-time extension may be requested, if it is still required.

   The exception request must be submitted on a completed Exception Request Form
   and must include:

       •      Description of the non-compliance (should include the specific policies or
              standards that are not be able to be met);

       •      Assessment of risks, if any, associated with non-compliance;

       •      Expected remediation date;

       •      Existing compensating controls for managing the risks associated with non-
              compliance, including, but not limited to: isolated network, access controls,
              multi-factor authentications (MFA). Unacceptable controls include, but are
              not limited to: standard data center inclusion, Endpoint Detection and
              Response (EDR), potential controls not yet implemented;

       •      Any ITSM numbers, demand numbers, or project numbers;

       •      A list of all devices in scope of the exception and a network diagram detailing
              the environment and data flow; and

       •      Detailed corrective action plan or mitigation plan – should include
              milestones and progress review dates.

   The Exception Request Form must be signed by the following:

       •      Information/Business Owner;

       •      Cyber Risk Coordinator (CRC) or Information Security Officer/ Designated
              Security Representative (ISO);

       •      Deputy Commissioner for Technology (DCT); and, where applicable;

       •      Commissioner/Executive Deputy Commissioner (or authorized designee).

   Upon submission of the Exception Request Form, the CISO, or their designee, will
   contact the requester to confirm receipt and request additional information, if
   needed. Once all required information has been received, the CISO or designee
   will either grant or deny the request.

   Upon approval, the CISO, or their designee, will send the approved Exception
   Request Form to the requestor. If the request is denied, the Exception Request
   Form will be returned with a brief explanation of why the request was denied.

   In the event that the request is denied, the Commissioner/Executive Deputy
   Commissioner and the CIO/DCT who signed the Exception Request Form may
   request a meeting with the ITS CIO and the CISO, the Director of Security and

   5.0 Compliance
   This policy shall take effect upon publication. Compliance is required with all
   enterprise policies and standards. ITS may amend its policies and standards at
   any time; compliance with amended policies and standards is required.
