# Acceptable Use of State Data & Information Technology Resources (CISP-001)

## At a glance

1. Purpose
This policy supports the State of Colorado Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) in
achieving the requirements of the Colorado Information Security Act (C.R.S. 24-37.5-401, et
seq.). It governs the acceptable use of the State of Colorado's data and information
technology (IT) resources by public agencies and users.

2. Applicability
This policy applies to all public agencies as defined by C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(26) and contracted
parties. This policy is not intended to govern the use of state IT resources made available for
public use (e.g., public Wi-Fi in state facilities by members of the public).

3. Defined Terms
All CISP-specific terms are defined in the Colorado Information Security Policy - CISP Glossary
unless otherwise defined in this policy.

4. Policy
All state IT resources and state data are the sole property of the State of Colorado, governed
by applicable statutes, policies and technical standards. This policy governs all use of state IT
resources and state data by any user.

5. Unacceptable and Prohibited Use of State Data and State IT
Resources
As a user of state data or state IT resources provided by the State of Colorado, you must not:
   ●   Use state data or state IT resources for commercial or personal financial gain.
   ●   Use state data or state IT resources to participate in political activities.
   ●   Forward state data to personal email accounts, except for emails or personal data
       related to the employee (e.g., state benefits, PERA, or course-completion
       certificates).
   ●   Post state data to personal social media accounts.
   ●   Access any social media account using state IT resources except as specifically
       authorized in this policy.
   ●   Disclose or forward state data to unauthorized parties.
   ●   Use state IT resources in a way that negatively impacts the confidentiality, integrity or
       availability of the IT resources.
   ●   Use state IT resources to send, intentionally receive or access defamatory,
       discriminatory, harassing or pornographic content or communication.
   ●   Use IT resources, including public-facing state IT resources, to engage in conduct
       violating any local, state or federal law or regulation, the State of Colorado Universal
       Policies, agency-specific policies, or the Colorado Information Security Policies.
   ●   Use personally owned technology to access state data or state IT resources without
       obtaining explicit approval as described in the Use of Personally Owned Equipment
       section of this policy.
   ●   Download, install, or access software or applications on state IT resources without
       authorization from your appointing authority.
   ●   Take unauthorized, deliberate action that damages or disrupts a state IT resource;
       alters or degrades its normal performance; reduces established security controls; or
       causes it to malfunction.
   ●   Access state data or state IT resources without authorization.
   ●   Allow unauthorized individuals to access state systems or IT resources.

6. Acceptable Use
Internet, Network, System, Calendar and Email Accounts
State data, IT resources, the state network, internet, systems, and email accounts must only
be used to conduct official state business. Examples of acceptable use include:
   ●   Communication with business associates.
   ●   Conducting research relevant to the user's work or agency needs.
   ●   Obtaining relevant news/information.
   ●   Job-specific training provided through a social media platform.
   ●   Listening to applicable legislative hearings or committee meetings to fulfill the user's
       agency or work group's mission.
    ● Adding personal appointments to a state work calendar only to block time and notify a
       team of necessary absences.
All public agencies have the right to monitor and/or investigate user activity while using or
accessing any state data or state IT resource, including the state network, internet, systems,
calendar, or email account. Users have no expectation of privacy while using state IT
resources; all electronic communications sent to and from state-assigned accounts are the

property of the State of Colorado and are subject to the Colorado Open Records Act (CORA,
C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1, et seq.).
Social Media
Users may use state IT resources to access social media to obtain information related to their
work or state employment. When expressly authorized by their appointing authority and in
line with their duties, users may post to social media as a representative of their agency or
the state. Public agencies may have their own more restrictive social media policies, which
users must follow.
License Agreements / Copyright Compliance
State IT resources must be used in accordance with the terms of software license agreements
or other copyright restrictions. Users may not access or store restricted copyrighted material
using any state IT resource.
Remote Access, Authentication and Devices
Connecting to internal state data and state networks must be done through approved remote
access processes. Direct access to the state network without using a virtual private network
(VPN) and multi-factor authentication (MFA) is prohibited. Users may not remotely access
state data or a state IT resource via a personal device unless expressly approved by the user's
appointing authority (accessing email and calendar are exempted).
User Responsibilities for Account and Access Management
While OIT, technology providers, and agencies are primarily responsible for managing
accounts and access, users have the following responsibilities:
   ●   Account Credentials: Follow technical standards to create strong passwords; protect
       account credentials and prevent unauthorized use; avoid reusing accounts or
       identifiers; use unique passwords; never share credentials.
   ●   Access Control: Report when data is transmitted insecurely or to unauthorized
       recipients to the user's manager and the OIT Service Desk to generate an alert for the
       CISO.
Security Awareness Training and Education
Annual security awareness training and education must be provided to all users, who must
complete assigned training in a timely manner, follow the principles and practices covered,
and report suspected security incidents or policy violations.
Use of Personally Owned Equipment
Agencies with personnel who have a direct business need to use a personally owned device
must follow the exceptions process; the requesting agency is responsible for tracking
approvals. Approved personal devices must be free from malware, protected by strong
authentication, and kept current with operating system and software updates to state
standards.
Protecting Devices and State Data
Users must take reasonable precautions to prevent the loss or theft of state IT resources or
state data, and must immediately report any loss or theft of devices used for state business
(including personal devices) to their manager and the OIT Service Desk (for consolidated
agencies) or as specified by their non-consolidated agency's applicable policy.
International Access to State Data and IT Resources
User access to state data and state IT resources while visiting international locations must
comply with Department of Personnel and Administration universal policy requirements.
International user access to systems and data is prohibited by Criminal Justice Information
Sharing (CJIS) and Federal Tax Information (FTI) compliance requirements. Access from, or
transmission of state data to, high-risk countries on the State Department's International
Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) list is prohibited at all times.

7. Compliance
Annual Acceptance / Retention
This policy must be accepted by users at the start of employment and no less than annually
thereafter. Agencies must:
   ●   Provide a documented acknowledgment of this policy for users to read and sign before
       accessing state IT resources and data.
   ●   Maintain a record of signed acknowledgments for appropriate retention periods.
   ●   Require individuals to re-read and re-sign the policy annually, regardless of prior
       acknowledgments.
Impacts of Non-compliance
Failure of users to comply with this policy may result in, as applicable:
   ●   Agency-level sanctions regarding state IT resources: restriction, deactivation, or
       suspension of the user's account, project, or system, or termination of employment.
   ● Referral to law enforcement.
   ● Termination of contract or assignment for temporary workers, interns, volunteers,
       contractors, or vendors.
   ● Loss of federal grant funding, fines, and penalties.
Non-consolidated agencies must have appropriate policies and/or procedures to ensure
compliance with this policy and all other Colorado Information Security Policies.
User Privacy and Right to Monitor and Investigate
All state records stored on a state-issued or approved IT resource, with minimal exception,
are subject to the Colorado Open Records Act (CORA, C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1, et seq.).

8. Exceptions
Any exceptions to this policy must be documented, justified, and approved in advance of
implementation. Requestors must submit a Secure Configuration Exception Request.
