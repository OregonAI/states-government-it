Statewide GenAI Policy
  Document ID                     POL-AI-001
  Title                           Statewide GenAI Policy
  Executive Sponsor               David Edinger, Chief Information Officer & Executive Director
  Issuing Authority               Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT)
  Applies to                      All consolidated state agencies and individual users of state-
                                  governed IT resources, assets, and systems.
  Instrument Type                 Policy
  Version                         2.0
  Effective Date                  09/17/2024
  Last Updated                    04/01/2026

1. Purpose
This policy supports the appropriate development, deployment, and use of generative
artificial intelligence (GenAI) systems, products, services, tools, and content within
consolidated state agencies.
This policy should not be interpreted as superseding existing state law or Governor's Office of
Information Technology (OIT) policy. Rather, any use of GenAI should operate within existing
technology processes and procedures, in addition to the requirements outlined in this policy.
Consolidated agencies should consult their assigned IT Director when there is a perceived
conflict between this policy and existing state law or other OIT policy.

2. Policy
All consolidated state agency efforts that include the development or deployment of GenAI
systems, or other technologies with related GenAI components or considerations, must
undergo an intake and risk assessment by OIT. This also applies to third-party vendor use
cases and projects.
Individual users within state agencies that use GenAI products, services, tools or content must
follow all existing Colorado Information Security Policies (CISPs) and standards. Individual
users must adhere to these requirements while using state-governed IT resources, assets and
systems, when conducting business on behalf of the state and using the state's data.

3. Applicability
This policy applies to all consolidated state agencies and individual users of state-governed IT
resources, assets and systems.

This policy does not apply to artificial intelligence features premised on machine learning
without a generative component, such as fraud and spam filters or autocorrect software for
spelling. Additionally, this policy does not govern the personal use of GenAI on personal
devices.

4. References
   ●   National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) AI Risk Management Framework
   ●   Colorado Information Security Policies
   ●   Colorado Revised Statutes, Section 24-37.5-105.2
   ●   1 CCR 101-9 Procurement Rules
   ●   Enterprise Project Management Office (EPMO) Project Lifecycle Methodology
   ●   Glossary of AI Terms

5. Definitions
   ●   Consolidated State Agency (state agencies): Refers to all of the state agencies for
       which OIT is the provider of information technology services and support. As defined in
       C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(28), these are “all of the departments, divisions, commissions,
       boards, bureaus, and institutions in the executive branch of the state government.”
       State agency does not include the legislative or judicial departments, the department
       of education, law, state or treasury, or state-supported institutions of higher
       education.
   ●   GenAI: An artificial intelligence technology that creates synthetic digital content of
       images, videos, audio, text, and other digital content from large bodies of data in
       response to user prompts. Unlike other artificial intelligence systems that are designed
       to perform specific tasks based on predefined rules and patterns, GenAI is capable of
       producing novel and unique outputs.
   ●   Individual Users: All State of Colorado employees, temporary workers, contractors,
       interns, volunteers, third-party vendors, and all other persons or entities granted
       access to non-public State of Colorado information technology resources, assets, and
       systems.

6. Responsibilities
Consolidated State Agency Responsibilities
   ●   Proposals for Deployment of GenAI systems: State agencies must obtain OIT approval
       prior to deploying new GenAI systems, and must ensure their use of GenAI is in
       accordance with OIT policies, standards and procedures and all applicable state and
       federal laws and regulations.
   ●   Development of GenAI systems: State agency efforts to develop GenAI systems must be
       done in accordance with the project management processes defined by OIT's
       Enterprise Project Management Office (EPMO), and comply with applicable OIT policy
       and standards and Colorado state and federal law.
   ●   Human Review of Consequential Decisions: GenAI used by state agencies may assist a
       human reviewer but may not independently make determinations materially impacting
       individuals or entities. For all state programs and services that use GenAI to assist with
       determinations concerning whether individuals or entities receive a state benefit or
       funding, or whether an adverse action should be taken against an individual or entity
       (arrest, detention, etc.), a human must be the ultimate decision-maker as part of the
       workflow.

Individual User Responsibilities
   ●   Individual users are strictly required to ensure their use of GenAI is in accordance with
       OIT policies, standards, procedures and all applicable state and federal laws and
       regulations.

Office of Information Technology Responsibilities
   ●   OIT is required by state law in Colorado Revised Statutes Title 24 Article 37.5 to
       enforce technology and security standards and to conduct due diligence and audits of
       contractors.
   ●   Determine the risk of GenAI cases using the NIST Framework to document and
       maintain processes.
   ●   Refer all high-risk cases to OIT's Chief Information Officer, or designee, in consultation
       with the Governor's Office and the relevant state agency's executive leadership for
       review.

7. Compliance
Failure of state employees or other users to comply with this policy may result in, as
applicable:
   ●    Loss of or reduced access to state IT resources.
   ●    Corrective action and/or disciplinary action up to and including termination of
        employment.
    ● Referral to law enforcement.
A violation of this policy by temporary workers, interns, volunteers, contractors or vendors
may result in termination of their contract or assignment with the State of Colorado.
Failure of a consolidated state agency to comply with this policy may result in, as applicable:
   ●   Agency-level sanctions regarding state IT resources.
   ●   Restriction, deactivation or suspension of the project or system.

8. Expiration
This policy remains in effect until superseded or rescinded by the State Chief Information
Officer.
