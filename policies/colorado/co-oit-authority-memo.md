                                                        Operations Memorandum
                                                        OIT’s Authority & Governance

  Executive Summary
  Information technology (IT) is an essential component of state government, enabling the
  delivery of critical services to Colorado’s residents, businesses and visitors. Since 2008, the
  Colorado Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) has been the enterprise
  provider of IT support and services to nearly all executive branch agencies. This
  memorandum highlights the current statutory roles and responsibilities of OIT as well as
  those of state agencies served by OIT.

  Statutory Authority
  Colorado Revised Statutes Title 24 Article 37.5 empowers OIT to be the organization it is
  today. OIT has the statutory authority and responsibility to integrate, coordinate and
  oversee information technology for the state and state agencies.1 In July 2008, Senate Bill
  08-155 consolidated the delivery of state agency IT services and functions, systems,
  personnel and assets under OIT. This consolidation reduced the number of disparate and
  duplicative systems through the deployment of enterprise solutions. Senate Bill 22-191 and
  House Bill 21-1236 provided additional clarity and further solidified OIT’s authority and role
  in relation to state agency IT. Additionally, Senate Bill 24-224 empowered OIT to promulgate
  rules to establish a technology life-cycle plan for the state’s IT systems and assets. OIT’s
  statutory authority encompasses the following areas:

        IT Staff. OIT oversees the hiring, management, training and performance of all IT
        personnel in the state who are performing IT functions. This includes IT personnel and
        contractors who support state agencies to perform IT work. Each state agency is
        assigned an IT Director who serves as the bridge between the agency and OIT by
        supporting collaborative decision-making in the procurement, implementation and
        management of IT systems, services, processes and standards.

       IT Standards. OIT establishes, maintains, oversees and enforces the IT technical
       standards and information security policies to which state agencies are accountable.
       OIT also oversees statewide IT strategy, rates and services, broadband, cybersecurity,

       IT Projects and Project Management. OIT governs all state agency IT projects.

       standards, architecture and best practices. OIT ensures that projects are managed by
       an assigned project manager and that the state agency working on the capital IT
       project reports to OIT as required. State agencies undertaking major IT projects must
       work closely with OIT, prior to commencing the project, to develop the project plan and
       obtain approval from OIT.

       Internet-based Government. OIT is charged with developing and encouraging an
       internet-based state government and facilitating the dissemination of information onto
       the internet in a secure, accessible and equitably-available manner. OIT works with
       partners including the Statewide Internet Portal Authority (SIPA) and state agencies to
       enhance the citizen experience with state government. OIT is implementing a digital
       government strategic plan to advance statewide adoption of digital government services
       and increase offerings for these services.

       IT Infrastructure, Applications and Systems. OIT ensures cost-effective, efficient,
       and secure IT systems for state agencies to reduce redundancy, improve
       interoperability, and meet business needs. OIT also manages the state's IT asset
       inventory, refresh cycles, and cost projections. Additionally, OIT establishes application
       development methodology and oversees installation, maintenance, and retirement of IT
       assets. By Dec. 31, 2024, OIT will establish a technology life-cycle plan for the state.

  Chief Information Officer Authority2
  The Chief Information Officer (CIO) is appointed by the Governor to lead OIT as Executive
  Director and serve as a member of the Governor’s cabinet. The CIO actively monitors trends
  and advances in IT resources, directs and approves the annual comprehensive statewide IT
  planning process, and plans for the acquisition, management and use of IT. The CIO advises
  the Joint Technology Committee (JTC) and Joint Budget Committee (JBC) on new and
  ongoing IT projects in relation to project contracts, budgets, and timelines and assists the
  JTC in its oversight of OIT. The CIO has the authority to promulgate rules for the governance
  of IT, including the standards, policies, procedures and guidelines for the oversight of IT
  staff, strategy, security, resources, data, projects, systems, infrastructure and applications,
  as well as to establish IT accessibility standards for individuals with a disability.

  Chief Information Security Officer Authority3
  Statute provides that a Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) within OIT be appointed by
  the CIO to ensure that the information Coloradans have entrusted to public agencies4 is
  safe, secure and protected from unauthorized access, unauthorized use or destruction. The
  CISO is responsible for establishing and maintaining a robust IT security program for public
  agencies. This includes developing and updating security standards, policies, and guidelines,
  and ensuring that agencies incorporate these standards into their IT security plans. The
  CISO also plays a critical role in incident response, with the authority to temporarily
  suspend agency operations to resolve security incidents. Additionally, the CISO directs IT
  security audits, establishes a risk management process to identify vulnerabilities, and
  deploys mitigation strategies to address those risks.

  Government Data Advisory Board Authority5
  The CIO or the CIO’s delegate serves as a member and Chair of the Government Data
  Advisory Board (advisory board). In this capacity, the CIO or CIO’s delegate is responsible for
  extending invitations to state agency personnel who are either experts in data or
  responsible for diverse aspects of data management to serve on the advisory board. The CIO
  or CIO’s delegate is required to schedule meetings of the advisory board as necessary to
  complete the advisory board’s duties, as outlined in statute,6 and annually report to the JTC
  on the advisory board’s progress, accomplishments and recommendations for future work.

  Colorado Broadband Office7
  The Colorado Broadband Office (CBO) leads the statewide effort to expand broadband
  coverage and quality for all Coloradans. In 2021, House Bill 21-1289 codified the CBO in OIT
  and established the authority of the CBO and the office’s Executive Director.

  State Agency Responsibility
  “State agency” includes all of the departments, divisions, commissions, boards, bureaus,
  and institutions in the executive branch of Colorado state government; however, this does
  not include the legislative or judicial departments, the departments of education, law,
  state, and treasury, or state-supported institutions of higher education.8

  State agency adherence to the IT statutory requirements discussed in this memorandum
  serves as the state’s best defense in mitigating security threats, limiting agency and state
  contractual and legal liabilities, and ensuring that data is being used and accessed in
  accordance with state and federal laws. Alignment with statutory requirements also creates
  streamlined processes and economies of scale that maximize taxpayer resources and

  Public agencies are required to annually develop an information security plan using the
  security policies, standards and guidelines developed by the CISO and submit the plan to the
  CISO for approval by July 15 of each year.12 The information security plan must provide
  information security for the communication and information resources that support the
  operations and assets of the public agency and include the following components:
       ●​ Periodic assessments of the risk and magnitude of the harm that could result from a
          security incident.
       ●​ A process for providing adequate information security for the communication and
          information resources of the public agency.
       ●​ Regular security awareness training to inform the employees and users of the public
          agency’s communication and information resources about information security risks
          and the responsibility of employees and users to comply with agency policies,
          standards, and procedures designed to reduce those risks.
       ●​ Periodic testing and evaluation of the effectiveness of information security for the
          public agency, which shall be performed not less than annually.
       ●​ A process for detecting, reporting, and responding to security incidents consistent
          with the information security standards, policies, and guidelines issued by the CISO.
       ●​ Plans and procedures to ensure the continuity of operations for information resources
          that support the operations and assets of the public agency in the event of a security
          incident.13
  While not included in the definition of “public agency” both the general assembly14 and
  institutions of higher education15 are also required to implement information security plans
  in accordance with statutory requirements.
