# Real-time Billing Dispute Procedure (PROC-FIN-001)

## At a glance

OIT's mandatory procedure for agencies to dispute Real-time Billing charges: OIT and agency responsibilities and the submission steps (the purpose the PDF states).

## Full text

Real-time Billing Dispute Procedure
  Document ID                       PROC-FIN-001
  Title                             Real-time Billing Dispute Procedure
  Executive Sponsor                 David Edinger, Chief Information Officer & Executive Director
  Issuing Authority                 Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT)
  Applies to                        All Colorado state agencies that use OIT common policy services
                                    and participate in the Real-time Billing (RtB) process.
  Instrument Type                   Procedure
  Version                           1.0
  Effective Date                    05/05/2026
  Last Updated                      05/05/2026

1. Purpose
To establish a standardized process for agencies to dispute Real-time Billing (RtB) charges and
request changes during the billing process. This procedure outlines the Governor's Office of
Information Technology (OIT) and agency responsibilities, submission and processing
timelines, and the specific steps for disputing RtB charges and requesting changes to RtB
invoices.

2. Applicability
This procedure applies to all Colorado state agencies that use OIT common policy services and
participate in the RtB process.

3. Process
The RtB Dispute process consists of submission and resolution stages for both agency-initiated
and OIT-initiated disputes. For standard disputes (those not related to lack of detail, service
quality, multi-agency impacts, or division coding changes) reference the Real-time Billing
Dispute Process: Agency Steps guide.

Agency-initiated Dispute Submission Process
If an agency identifies the need for a correction that may result in a dispute, the agency must
take the following steps:
   ●      Notify the assigned agency IT Director of the needed correction to determine if a
          dispute should be submitted.

   ●   If a dispute should be submitted, the assigned agency Subject Matter Expert (SME)
       must click the “Dispute Charges” button on any of the Detailed Dashboards on the OIT
       RtB website to access and complete the official Dispute Form.
   ●   The agency SME must complete the request form detailing the service(s) needing
       changes and providing the rationale for the correction.
   ●   OIT Budget processes the request, notifies the appropriate service owner for
       review/awareness, and copies OIT_SMO@state.co.us.
   ●   Changes to charges for closed previous fiscal years will not be corrected due to fiscal
       controls.

OIT-initiated Dispute Submission Process
If OIT identifies missed billable hours, the following occurs:
   ●   OIT Financial Services and Service Owners confirm total hours and the amount that
       should have been billed.
   ●   The OIT Service Management Office and Customer Office validate the data.
   ●   The OIT Service Management Office and Customer Office review the data with the
       impacted Agency Customer.
   ●   The Agency Customer must agree to and approve the adjustment prior to being billed.
   ●   If approved, OIT Budget includes the approved adjustment in the next billing period.

Dispute Resolution Process
Dispute findings typically fall into seven categories: Resolved as requested; Resolved as
requested, agency action needed; Finding different from requested; Finding different from
requested, agency action needed; Finding different from requested, additional discussion
needed; Unresolved, pending OSC decision; and Resolved, OSC decision.
Agreement with dispute request: If OIT's finding agrees with the agency dispute request and
an adjustment is required, OIT will process the adjustment, reflected in the agency's
Adjustments Dashboard and included as an adjustment to the agency's invoice in the next
billing period.
Disagreement with dispute request: If OIT's finding differs from the agency dispute request,
OIT will email the agency specifying any agency actions needed and any OIT actions being
taken. The agency SME must respond to confirm the finding, resolve the dispute and close the
request, or request a meeting for additional information. If the agency SME does not respond
within 14 business days, the dispute will be closed and the finding will stand.
Additional discussion needed: If the agency requires additional discussion, the agency SME
must respond within the 14-business-day period to request a meeting. OIT will schedule a
meeting with the service owner and agency SME (the agency SME may invite additional staff).
Based on the outcome, the agency SME must schedule additional meetings, confirm the
finding and resolve the dispute, or disagree with the finding. If the agency SME disagrees, the
dispute is considered unresolved and follows the Unresolved Dispute Process; OIT will update
the dispute status to “unresolved” in the agency's Adjustments Dashboard.

Unresolved Dispute Process
Unresolved agency disputes follow the process required by Colorado Fiscal Rule 2-3, Payment
Terms, 3.4 Disputes (this procedure does not supersede the fiscal rule):
   ●   The agency disputing the charge and the agency providing the service shall attempt to
       resolve the dispute, utilizing agency Chief Executive Officers if necessary.

   ●    If the dispute is not resolved within 60 business days, the disputing agency shall
        petition the State Controller to resolve the dispute within 30 business days of
        determining it cannot be resolved.
    ● If petitioned, the State Controller shall render a final and binding decision for all
        parties within a reasonable time frame.
OIT responsibilities following State Controller decision: OIT will follow the binding decision
and, if applicable, reflect any adjustments in the next billing period; update the dispute
status to “Resolved - OSC decision” and close the request; and, as appropriate, incorporate
the decision into policies and procedures to prevent similar future disputes.
Agency responsibility following State Controller decision: Agencies will follow the binding
decision issued by the State Controller.

Unique Disputes
Lack of detail: Raised when the agency cannot confirm that RtB charges are accurate due to a
lack of detail concerning usage and benefit received from an OIT common policy service. The
agency SME must work with the assigned IT Director to obtain additional information from the
service owner. OIT will track all “lack of detail” disputes to prioritize service enhancements
and incorporate greater detail into the RtB website dashboards.
Quality of service did not meet agency expectations: Raised when expected outcomes or work
products did not meet agency expectations relative to time and effort billed. OIT will validate
the disputed utilization charges; the agency SME must work with the IT Director to address
issues, and the agency will pay charges due. If issues continue, the agency SME may
commence a “stop work order” and not authorize future services; any unauthorized hours
billed after service authorization removal must be disputed per this procedure.
Corrections with multi-agency impact: Where an agency-initiated dispute impacts OIT
common policy billing of other agencies, OIT will contact the IT Director, Budget Director and
Controller for the other agencies by email; create a separate dispute for each impacted
agency; not prevent a submitted dispute from being resolved due to impact on other
agencies; resolve the dispute with the original agency once validated and agreed; and offer
each impacted agency an opportunity to provide input before finalization.
Division coding change: An agency-initiated dispute intended solely to correct the division
assigned to a specific charge (assumes no other changes are needed). OIT will review the
dispute and confirm with the appropriate service owner whether the charged division needs
to change; if warranted, OIT will communicate needed actions to the agency SME. Financial
adjustments to coding beyond costs may require a journal entry by the agency. OIT will not
make adjustments for periods already billed; once completed, the adjustment is reflected in
the next billing period.

4. Definitions and Descriptions
The following definitions and descriptions are for the sole purpose of the Real-time Billing
Dispute policy and procedure:
   ●   Chief Financial Officer (CFO): Executive leader in the functional area of OIT that
       focuses on financial services.
   ●   Colorado Operations Resource Engine (CORE): The program used for interagency billing
       processes.
   ●   Internal Transaction Initiator (ITI): The CORE document sent by OIT to the agency
       showing the invoiced amount due to OIT from the agency.
   ●   Internal Transaction Agreement (ITA): Similar to the ITI document; the document the
       agency approves and references when paying the bill to OIT through CORE.
                                                                                     Page 3 of 6

   ●   Real-time Billing (RtB): The process OIT uses to charge back state agency customers on
       a monthly basis for actual service costs incurred.
   ●   Subject Matter Expert (SME): An assigned agency representative responsible for
       working with OIT to resolve RtB disputes.
   ●   Utilization: An agency's actual consumption of an OIT service.
   ●   90 Day Rule: Submission deadlines for disputed charges and requests for changes (see
       Section 5, Agency Deadlines table).

Responsibilities for Invoice Review and Processing
OIT Responsibilities:
   ●  OIT sends a monthly email to IT Directors, Budget Directors, and Controllers
      announcing the availability of the agency's monthly data and invoice on the RtB
      website.
   ● OIT requests payment from the agency in CORE using an Internal Transfer Initiation
      (ITI) document with the invoice attached as a PDF.
Agency Responsibilities:
   ●   Agencies process the invoice and assess the services utilized to confirm the charges.
   ●   Agencies properly code the payment in CORE as an Internal Transaction Agreement
       (ITA) within 30 days of receipt.

Responsibilities for Dispute Charges/Request Changes
OIT Responsibilities:
   ●  OIT maintains a dispute form on the RtB website and a status portal for agencies to
      submit OIT common policy billing and utilization corrections, track progress of
      disputes, and verify resolution.
Agency Responsibilities:
   ●   Agencies assign specific SMEs to assess the invoice details provided for each service to
       ensure billed items are being consumed and utilized by the agency.

   ●    If corrections are needed, agencies should submit those to OIT following the Agency-
        initiated Dispute Submission Process outlined in this document.
   ●    Agencies are responsible for monitoring usage and reviewing updates monthly. Dispute
        requests for changes to closed previous fiscal years will not be corrected due to fiscal
        controls.

Responsibilities for Tracking Submitted Dispute Status
OIT Responsibilities:
OIT provides the status of submitted disputes on the RtB Website. The website provides the
following information for each agency for all disputes:
   ● Unique dispute identification
   ● Number of days in queue
   ● Review status
   ● Service code and name
   ● Description of issue
   ● Bill reflecting resolved disputes
   ● Adjusted quantity and charge for resolved disputes
Agency Responsibilities:
   ●    Agencies track disputes via the Billing Adjustments dashboard on the RtB Website.
   ●    Agencies must use this tool prior to inquiring to OIT about the status of their dispute.

6. Compliance
RtB disputes and change request submissions that do not comply with this procedure and the
associated Real-time Billing Dispute Policy will not be processed.
