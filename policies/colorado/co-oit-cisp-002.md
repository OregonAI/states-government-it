# Incident Response Policy (CISP-002)

  Title                             Incident Response Policy
  Executive Sponsor                 Jill Fraser, Chief Information Security Officer
  Issuing Authority                 Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT)
  Applies to                        All public agencies as defined by C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(26). All
                                    contracted parties must comply with these requirements.
  Instrument Type                   Policy
  Version                           3.0
  Effective Date                    07/01/2026
  Last Updated                      03/09/2026

1. Purpose
This policy supports the State of Colorado Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) in
achieving the requirements of the Colorado Information Security Act (C.R.S. 24-37.5-401, et
seq.). It governs public agency incident response and continuity planning and implementation.

2. Applicability
This policy applies to all public agencies as defined by C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(26). All contracted
parties must comply with these requirements.

3. Defined Terms
All CISP-specific terms are defined in the CISP Glossary unless otherwise defined in this policy.

4. Policy
All state IT resources and state data are the sole property of the State of Colorado, and
applicable statutes, policies, and technical standards govern their use. Public agencies must
implement incident response, continuity management, and contingency planning in
accordance with this policy.

5. Safeguards
Incident Response
The agency, in coordination with the Office of Information Technology, must establish,
maintain and document an agency-level incident response plan. At a minimum, the plan must:
   ●      Designate personnel for incident response and recovery.

   ●   Establish, maintain, and regularly verify contact information of designated incident
       response and recovery personnel, and distribute it to OIT.
    ● Establish, maintain, and document a process for reporting security incidents.
    ● Establish, maintain, and document an incident response process.
    ● Plan, conduct, and document routine incident response exercises.
Consolidated agencies must notify the state CISO if an incident involves a state system,
network, IT resources, or state data. Incidents must be reported to the OIT Service Desk at
303-239-HELP (4357) or through the ServiceHub Customer Portal. OIT will communicate OIT-
discovered incidents to affected agencies.

Logging and Monitoring
The Office of Information Technology must establish, maintain, and document a logging and
monitoring management process that serves to detect security incidents and events, and to
support investigation of security breaches, policy violations, and regulatory compliance,
where possible. OIT, in coordination with the agency, must:
   ●   Develop and maintain processes that increase the likelihood of identifying security-
       relevant events early.
   ●   Define and implement additional logging functions where necessary.
   ●   Regularly review and update logging requirements.
   ●   Provide logs to agencies upon request.

Scope of Logging and Monitoring
Logging and monitoring management documentation must state that all systems, networks,
and applications are subject to logging and monitoring activities.

Types of Data to be Logged
Standard operating procedures or process documentation must identify what events from the
following will be logged:
   ●   Authentication attempts.
   ●   Access to resources.
   ●   System events, including but not limited to operating system, application, database
       and network events.
   ●   Network traffic.
   ●   Security-related audit events.
   ●   Changes to system configurations.
   ●   Sessions.

Log Retention
The technology provider should allocate and retain log storage for 90 days for all logs, and
must retain logs longer where regulatory or legal compliance requires.

Access to Logs
The technology provider must restrict access to logs to individuals with a need-to-know
designation.

Protection of Log Data
Log data must be stored in a protected environment or secured within the technology
solution.

Monitoring for Security Events
The logging and monitoring management process must require daily monitoring for security
events.

Respond to Logging and Monitoring Failures
The information technology security provider must respond to logging and monitoring failures.

Time Clock Synchronization
The technology provider must synchronize internal information system clocks with an
authoritative time source to ensure log integrity and correlation.

Continuity Planning
OIT's Continuity Program adheres to NIST standards and the Colorado Information Security
Act, and follows guidelines from the Division of Homeland Security and Emergency
Management (DHSEM) within the Colorado Department of Public Safety.

Alternate Communications
The technology provider should include alternative communications protocols in case primary
protocols are disrupted or insecure, in support of continuity of operations.

Information System Backups
The technology provider must ensure information system backups by regularly verifying,
documenting, monitoring, and testing backups.

6. Compliance
Failure of users to comply with this policy may result in, as applicable:
   ●   Loss of federal grant funding, fines and penalties.
   ●   Agency-level sanctions regarding state IT resources: restriction, deactivation, or
       suspension of the user's account, project, or system, or termination of employment.
   ● Termination of contract or assignment for temporary workers, interns, volunteers,
       contractors, or vendors who violate this policy.
Non-consolidated agencies must have appropriate policies and/or procedures to ensure
compliance with this policy and all other Colorado Information Security Policies.

7. Exceptions
Any exceptions to this policy must be documented, justified, and approved in advance of
implementation. Requestors must submit a Secure Configuration Exception Request.
