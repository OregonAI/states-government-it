# Information Technology Security Risk Management Policy (CISP-003)

## At a glance

Colorado Information Security Policy, issued under the Colorado Information Security Act (C.R.S. 24-37.5-401 et seq.): Information Technology Security Risk Management Policy. Listed by OIT under Current Statewide Policies, Information Security.

CISP-003: Information Technology Security
Risk Management Policy

  Title                           Information Technology Security Risk Management Policy
  Executive Sponsor               Jill Fraser, Chief Information Security Officer

  Applies to                      All public agencies as defined by C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(26). Not

  Last Updated                    03/09/2026

1. Purpose
This policy supports the State of Colorado Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) in
achieving the requirements of the Colorado Information Security Act (C.R.S. 24-37.5-401, et
seq.). It governs information technology security risk management for State of Colorado IT
resources by public agencies and users.

2. Applicability
This policy applies to all public agencies as defined by C.R.S. 24-37.5-102(26). It is not
intended to govern the use of state IT resources made available for public use (e.g., public
Wi-Fi in state facilities). All contracted parties must comply with these requirements.

3. Defined Terms
All CISP-specific terms are defined in the CISP Glossary unless otherwise defined in this policy.

4. Policy
All state IT resources, information and data are the sole property of the State of Colorado,
and applicable statutes, policies, and technical standards govern their use. Public agencies
must manage and mitigate IT security risk in accordance with this policy.

5. IT Security Planning
Agency Cyber Security Plan (Non-consolidated)
Non-consolidated agencies must work with their information security officer to develop an
annual cyber security plan for their agency, in accordance with Rule 8 CCR 1501-5 and this
policy.

Enterprise Cyber Security Plan (Consolidated)
The Office of Information Technology (OIT) must create a consolidated, integrated enterprise
cyber security plan for all consolidated agencies in compliance with Rule 8 CCR 1501-5 and
this policy.

6. Securing IT Assets and Software
   ●   Secure Configuration of IT Assets and Software: OIT must identify minimum
       configuration standards for IT assets and software solutions.
   ●   Configuration Change Control: OIT and the agency must have a documented procedure
       for system configuration control.
   ●   Access Restrictions for Change: OIT must have a standard to support personnel access
       control to IT systems.
   ●   Configuration Settings: The technology provider must comply with technical standards.
   ●   Software Installation: OIT must establish written procedures for software installation.
   ●   Lifecycle Management: The technology provider must have a lifecycle plan ensuring
       the solution is maintained according to standards throughout its lifetime.
   ●   Maintenance: The technology provider, with the agency, must define monthly
       maintenance windows and allowable downtime, and schedule, perform, document,
       and review maintenance and repairs.
   ●   Maintenance Tools: The technology provider must implement, control, monitor, and
       maintain maintenance tools; prevent removal of equipment containing agency data;
       and restrict tool use to authorized personnel.
   ●   Maintenance Personnel: OIT must establish, and providers must follow, a process for
       authorizing in-person maintenance personnel, including supervision of unescorted
       personnel without required access authorizations.

7. Managing and Mitigating IT Risk
   ●   IT Risk Management: OIT must establish, maintain, and document an IT risk
       management program.
   ●   Data Categorization: OIT must work with the agency to categorize data and maintain
       documentation of the categories; system criticality classification must be performed.
   ●   Vulnerability Scanning: OIT must establish and maintain processes for regularly
       scanning for vulnerabilities, analyzing and remediating, and sharing results with the
       agency and stakeholders.

8. IT Supply Chain Management
OIT must establish, maintain, and document an IT supply chain management process.

   ●   Software Supply Chain: The technology provider must maintain an updated inventory
       of supply chain software and provide it upon request to the information security
       office.
   ●   IT Acquisition Process (Procurement): OIT, with the agency and procurement team,
       must include state requirements in agreements and ensure the supply chain meets the
       Colorado Information Security Policies, technical standards, and agency-specific
       requirements, including review and monitoring.

9. Protecting IT Systems and Communications
OIT must establish, maintain, and document a system and communications protection plan
and process addressing:

Secure Configuration and Infrastructure
   ●   Ensure only supported browsers and email clients are used, and maintain email server
       protections.
   ●   Maintain a secure network infrastructure.
   ●   Ensure the information system defaults to a safe and predictable state in the event of
       failure.

Data Protection
   ●   Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data in transit, in use, and at
       rest.
   ●   Prevent unauthorized and unintended information transfer via shared resources.

Network Security
   ●   Monitor and protect boundary communications, segment internal and external
       networks, and connect to external systems only through managed interfaces.
   ●   Ensure the information system terminates network connections after a session ends or
       after a set period of inactivity.
   ●   Establish usage restrictions and guidance for VoIP technologies, and monitor, control,
       segment, authorize, or deny access based on risk.

Email Verification
   ●   Establish a comprehensive email authentication standard across all domains to verify
       sender identity, ensure message integrity, and defend against spoofing.

Authentication and Encryption
   ●   Establish and manage cryptographic keys according to laws and regulations.
   ●   Issue public key certificates under a defined policy or obtain them from a trusted
       certificate authority.
   ●   Protect the authenticity of communications sessions.
   ●   Define secure mobile code technologies, establish usage restrictions and
       implementation guidance, and manage and monitor their use.

10. Asset Management
OIT and the technology provider must establish, maintain, and document an IT asset
management plan and process that:
   ●   Maintains a current IT asset inventory to record all IT hardware.
   ●   Maintains a current record of licensed and authorized software.
   ●   Ensures only approved software is used on IT assets.

11. Access Control Management and User Security
Account Management
OIT, the technology provider, and the agency must manage credentials for all user accounts
(including privileged and service accounts, IT assets, and software). The technology provider
and agency must:
   ●   Maintain an inventory of all active accounts.
   ●   Authorize user accounts.
   ●   Ensure each account type uses unique credentials.
   ●   Enforce strong password policies.
   ●   Continuously identify, monitor, and manage inactive accounts.
   ●   Manage, verify, revoke, and regularly audit digital identities and credentials.
   ●   Require strict least privilege.
   ●   Prevent account or identifier reuse.

Access Control Management
   ●   Manage IT asset access.
   ●   Require multifactor authentication (MFA) on all privileged accounts and for all remote
       access.
   ●   Ensure data is transmitted securely only to authorized recipients.
   ●   Enforce separation of duties.

Information Sharing
Where required, the technology provider must enable secure data sharing and, with partner
technology providers, ensure they have similar or stronger data protection controls as those
required by the agency and applicable laws and regulations.

12. Security Awareness Training and Education
Every user must receive and complete assigned security awareness training. Records of
compliance and non-compliance must be maintained for each user. Training must be
completed at least annually, and public agencies may need to supplement OIT-created
training based on agency-specific needs.

13. Compliance
Failure of users to comply with this policy may result in, as applicable:
   ●   Loss of federal grant funding, fines and penalties.

   ●   Agency-level sanctions regarding state IT resources: restriction, deactivation, or
       suspension of the user's account, project, or system, or termination of employment.
   ● Termination of contract or assignment for temporary workers, interns, volunteers,
       contractors, or vendors who violate this policy.
Non-consolidated agencies must have appropriate policies and procedures to ensure
compliance with this policy and all other Colorado Information Security Policies.

14. Exceptions
Any exceptions to this policy must be documented, justified, and approved in advance of
implementation. Requestors must submit a Secure Configuration Exception Request.
