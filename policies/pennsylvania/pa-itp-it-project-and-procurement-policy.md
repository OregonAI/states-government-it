# IT Project and Procurement Policy (Commonwealth of Pennsylvania IT Policy: IT Project and Procurement Policy)

## At a glance

Information Technology Policy of the Office of Administration for agencies under the Governor's jurisdiction. Printed purpose: This Information Technology Policy (ITP) establishes the requirements to request and obtain approval of IT Projects and IT Procurements (as well as Pre-Solicitation Reviews). It also establishes the policy for the management of the Enterprise Service Catalog and the use of the Enterprise IT Service Offerings it contains.

1. Authority
Executive Order 2016-06, Enterprise Information Technology Governance

2. Document Control
This document replaces, in its entirety, the IT Projects Policy, dated June 10, 2026.

3. Purpose
This Information Technology Policy (ITP) establishes the requirements to request and obtain
approval of IT Projects and IT Procurements (as well as Pre-Solicitation Reviews). It also
establishes the policy for the management of the Enterprise Service Catalog and the use of the
Enterprise IT Service Offerings it contains.

4. Scope
This ITP applies to all departments, boards, offices, commissions, and councils under the
Governor’s jurisdiction (hereinafter referred to as “agencies”). Agencies not under the
Governor’s jurisdiction are strongly encouraged to follow this ITP.

5. Policy
For definitions of terms found within this document refer to the IT Policy Glossary.

    5.1 IT Project Request
    Designated submitters within an applicable IT Delivery Center shall complete an IT Project
    Request in the enterprise project management system for all new projects requiring IT support
    or resources.

    All IT Project Requests shall contain a cost estimate that includes both internal and external
    costs for IT. These are business planning estimates based on the information known at the time
    of submission and are used to ensure necessary funding is available.

All IT Project Requests shall be routed for approval in the following sequence:

  1. Agency Business Owner
     The request shall first be reviewed and approved by the agency’s designated Business
     Owner. This individual is authorized by the agency to validate the project’s scope and
     optionally approve the project requirements.

  2. Delivery Center or Enterprise Area Leadership
       Next, the request requires approval from the individual responsible for executing
       the project, either the applicable Delivery Center Chief Information Officer (CIO),
       the Enterprise area leader, or their designee, depending on who will perform the
       work.

  3. Office of the Budget
       The Budget Director (or designee) reviews the request and, if necessary, consults with the
       agency to confirm the appropriate project funding source.

  4. Commonwealth CIO
       If the total estimated cost of the project is $5,000,000 or more, approval is required from
       the Commonwealth CIO (or their designee).

  5. Project Sponsor
       The Project Sponsor holds final approval authority and is responsible for ensuring
       alignment with strategic priorities and resource commitments. For delivery center
       projects, the Project Sponsor is the agency secretary, deputy secretary, or their designee.
       For enterprise area projects, the Project Sponsor is the head of the respective enterprise
       area.

Additional details regarding the enterprise project management system and the investment
classifications used for IT Projects are available in the IT Project Standard.

   5.1.1 Project Manager Assignment
   All IT Projects regardless of cost shall have a designated person performing the Project
   Manager role in the enterprise project management system.

   5.1.2 Project Time Reporting
   All Office of Administration, Information Technology (OA/IT) resources shall submit a
   weekly report of time spent on IT Project work that is subject to IT chargeback or other
   external reporting requirements.

   5.1.3 Application Inventory
   IT Projects associated with Commonwealth applications of any type (SaaS, COTS,
   MOTS, custom, etc.) are required to update the record(s) within the enterprise
   Application Inventory.
       •   An IT Project which implements or commissions a new application must include
           and complete a project task to create a corresponding record within the enterprise
           Application Inventory.
       •   An IT Project which modifies an existing application must include and complete
           a project task to modify the corresponding record accordingly within the

           enterprise Application Inventory.
       •   An IT Project which removes or decommissions an existing application must
           include and complete a project task to modify/remove the corresponding record
           within the enterprise Application Inventory.

   A link to the enterprise Application Inventory as well as instructions to update the
   inventory can be found in the Application Inventory Knowledge Article and
   associated APM Guideline Document.

   5.1.4 Exemptions
   The Integrated Enterprise Systems Office (IESO) has a unique project intake process and
   project chargeback method. They are exempt from the requirements of section 5.1 related to
   project intake, chargebacks, and time tracking.

5.2 IT Procurement Intake Process
Agencies shall review the Procurement Handbook and other resources located at DGS
Procurement Resources for guidance with all purchasing and procurement processes and shall
follow the procedures outlined in the IT Procurement Intake Standard.

Depending on what is being procured, an IT Vendor Risk Assessment may also be required as
outlined in the IT Risk Management Policy.

   5.2.1 Purchases in the amount of $250,000 or greater
   The following purchases in the amount of $250,000 or greater require an IT Procurement
   Intake Request to be submitted in accordance with the guidance provided in the IT
   Procurement Intake Standard. Approval for these purchases shall be obtained through
   the enterprise IT Procurement Intake process:
       •   The purchase of cloud-based IT Services.
       •   IT Hardware (i.e., desktops, laptops, network, and telecommunication).
       •   Software or mobile applications in which the product currently exists on a contract
           or there is an existing license agreement already approved by appropriate legal
           entities (Note: Firmware is not considered software for the purposes of this policy).
       •   Purchases from an existing Statewide contract including Participating
           Addendums or any contract not previously approved during solicitation.
       •   All Renewals (unless Renewals were included in and previously approved as part
           of the original IT Procurement Intake Request submission).
       •   All maintenance and support agreements (new procurements and renewals).
       •   All IT consulting Services (new procurements and renewals).
       •   Any other IT Service.
       •   Contract changes, whether they are an Amendment or Change Order, resulting in
           subsequent purchases in the amount of $250,000.00 or greater or an amount
           greater than 20% of the Contract Value. This includes cumulative submissions
           associated with the same contract change when the combined dollar value results
           in the amount of $250,000.00 or greater.

Note: The $250,000 threshold applies to the total dollar value of the purchase rather than the
annual spend. For example: If a single purchase costs $1,000,000, but covers a 5-year period
the annual spend would only be $200,000, however an IT Procurement Intake Request is
required as the total commitment for the Commonwealth is
$1,000,000.

5.2.2 Purchases regardless of dollar value
The following purchases or acquisitions regardless of dollar value require an IT Procurement
Intake Request in accordance with the guidance provided in the IT Procurement Intake
Standard. Approval for these purchases shall be obtained through the appropriate Governing
Bodies via the enterprise IT Procurement Intake process:

   •   New Software or mobile applications where the product has not previously
       been deployed or used within the requesting agency or delivery center or
       where the product does not currently exist on a contract, or there is no
       executed license agreement that has been approved by appropriate legal
       entities (Note: Firmware is not considered software for the purposes of this
       policy).

   •   Open Source Software (OSS) with no executed license agreement or new OSS
       with an executed license agreement.

   •   Digital Document Management.
   •   Production level (high-volume) printing, finishing, or mailing equipment
       (including plotters) or Software solutions that are intended for use in a printshop
       or mail room environment. Excludes stand-alone mail metering systems.
   •   Pilots, proof of concepts, or product demonstrations which involve any of the
       following:
                •   Commonwealth resources such as network, data, or systems
                •   providing vendor information which can be construed as future
                    requirements for a solicitation (unless demo is open publicly via a
                    request for information).
   •   Procurements of Artificial Intelligence (AI) software or services.
   •   IT Staff Augmentation (A new IT investment submission is not required for
       administrative adjustments to previously approved staff augmentation purchase orders
       (POs) when there is no dollar-value impact. However, a new submission is required
       for new purchases or increases to an existing PO when the total cost exceeds the
       original approval by 20% or more.)

5.2.3 Emergency Procurements (EP)
An EP is used by agencies to procure materials or services which are urgently needed.
The requirements for an EP are defined in the Procurement Handbook. EPs shall be
submitted to DGS using the EP Approval Request Form and in accordance with the
guidance provided in the EP Quick Reference Guide.

For Information Technology EP meeting the criteria listed in Sections 5.3.1 and 5.3.2 of
this ITP, an IT Procurement Intake Request shall be submitted in accordance with IT
                                                                                Page 4 of 9

Procurement Intake Standard. Approval for these purchases shall be obtained through the
appropriate Governing Bodies via the enterprise IT Procurement Intake process and are
subject to a 10- business day turnaround. The purchase may proceed concurrently with the
IT Procurement Intake Request process if: 1) DGS approves, and 2) the EP meets the
criteria in the Procurement Handbook (Part II, Chapter 6).

5.2.4 Sole Source Procurements (SS)
SS is a process to allow a contract to be awarded for a supply, service or construction item
without competition if certain criteria is met as described in Title 62 – PROCUREMENT
Chapter 5 - Source Selection and Contract Formation Section 515 - Sole source
procurement. Agencies shall follow DGS guidance as described in the Procurement
Handbook, Pt II Ch 05 Sole Source Procurement of Materials and Part III Chapter 05 Sole
Source Procurement of Services.

For Information Technology SS procurements meeting the criteria listed in Sections 5.3.1 and
5.3.2 of this ITP, an IT Procurement Intake Request shall be submitted in accordance with
the IT Procurement Intake Standard. Approval for these purchases shall be obtained through
the appropriate Governing Bodies via the enterprise IT Procurement Intake process before
the sole source request is submitted to DGS for approval. The IT Procurement Intake process
is subject to a 10-business day turnaround.

5.2.5 Exempt Purchases
PA Compute Services (PACS) change orders for existing service are excluded from the
Enterprise IT Procurement Intake process as these are routed through the Contract
Change Request (CCR) process. New PACS services require an IT Procurement Intake
Request.

5.2.6 Restrictions
2019 National Defense Authorization Act
Restrictions on purchases as outlined in the 2019 National Defense Authorization Act
(NDAA), prohibit Agencies from entering, extending, or renewing a contract with a
vendor that uses any equipment, system, or services that uses telecommunications or
video surveillance equipment or services from certain companies IT Procurement Intake
Standard has the complete listing of the prohibited companies.

Iran Free Procurement
The entities outlined in Iran Free Procurement List are ineligible to enter into a contract
with the Commonwealth entity to provide for goods and services worth at least
$1,000,000 per Sections 3501-3506 of the Commonwealth Procurement Code, 62 Pa.
C.S. §§ 3501-3506.

Russia and Belarus Restrictions
In accordance with the Restrictions for Associations with Russia and Belarus Act, Act 57
of 2023, P.L. 433, No. 57., agencies are prohibited from entering into, renewing, or
extending contracts with persons or entities identified as being associated with the
governments of Russia or Belarus. The Pennsylvania Treasury Department maintains a
list of prohibited persons and entities that are ineligible to contract with Commonwealth
agencies.

   5.2.7 Submissions
   Authorized Users shall initiate a review and follow the guidance outlined in the IT
   Procurement Intake Standard. OA/IT Enterprise reviewers, at their discretion, may add
   conditions of approval for IT Procurement Intake submissions. The agency is responsible
   for ensuring that the conditions of approval have been satisfied in coordination with the
   Submitter to mitigate risks.

5.3 Software Licensing and Contracts

   5.3.1 Software Licensing
   Acquisition or procurement of all software shall be in accordance with the IT Project
   and Procurement Policy, the Commonwealth Procurement Code (62 Pa.C.S. § 101 et
   seq.), and all other applicable procurement policies and processes.
   Prior to the procurement or use of any Third-Party Software or Data Subscriptions,
   Agencies shall:

    •   Ensure there is a negotiated software license or subscription agreement in
        place between the Commonwealth and the publisher.
    •   Ensure all applicable agreements have been identified, reviewed, and
        approved (including but not limited to any clickthrough or OSS agreements)
        as to form and legality if an existing software license or subscription
        agreement is not in place.

   Prior to the procurement or use of any Third-Party Software or Data Subscription,
   Agency or OA legal counsel shall:

    •   Review the terms of the software license or subscription and advise on
        the potential legal risks associated with use.
    •   Review the use case and license terms as identified by the agency
        for potential conflicts with existing license agreements.

   If an agreement cannot be established or the terms of the agreement cannot be
   adequately negotiated, agencies are strongly encouraged to select an alternate
   software or subscription. In the event that another software or subscription cannot
   be utilized either because of mandates or business requirements, agencies shall
   adhere to the process detailed in the Non-Negotiated Software Agreements
   Procedure.

   Agencies shall ensure Authorized Users understand and comply with the
   applicable terms and conditions, such as any software license or subscription
   agreement, and that relevant training regarding the applicable terms and conditions
   is provided as part of education and awareness programs.

   5.3.2 Software Contracts
   For an agency to purchase software, there must first be a contract vehicle in place to
   purchase from. Software may be procured from one of the existing resellers on
   contract or through another contract vehicle. The Software Contracts Procedure
   provides guidance on determining if a software is available on contract and how to
   proceed in instances where it is not.

5.4 Pre-solicitation Review
Solicitations for information technology, such as Request for Proposal (RFP), Invitation to
Qualify (ITQ), Request for Quote (RFQ), Request for Application (RFA), or Invitation for
Bid (IFB) procurements in the amount of $250,000 or greater require a Pre- Solicitation
Review Request to be submitted in accordance with the guidance provided in the IT
Procurement Intake Standard. Approval for these solicitations shall be obtained through the
appropriate Governing Bodies via the enterprise Pre-Solicitation Review process.

Sole Source (SS) and Emergency Procurements (EP) do not require pre-solicitation
review/approval. These will be handled via the IT Procurement Intake Request as described in
sections 5.2.3 and 5.2.4.

Note: The $250,000 threshold applies to the estimated total dollar value of the purchase(s)
resulting from the solicitation rather than the annual spend. For example: If a single
purchase costs $1,000,000, but covers a 5-year period the annual spend would only be
$200,000, however an IT Procurement Intake Request would be required as the total
commitment for the Commonwealth is $1,000,000.
Pre-Solicitation Review Requests and related artifacts shall be submitted once the IT
Solicitation is far enough along in the development process that significant changes to the
scope are not anticipated.

Please allow for a ten (10) business day review cycle for requests for the Pre-Solicitation
Review(s).

5.5 Enterprise Service Catalog
Agencies and delivery centers shall review the Enterprise Service Catalog located at
https://itcentral.pa.gov/Pages/Enterprise-Services.aspx (Commonwealth Access Only).

Agencies and delivery centers shall utilize all Enterprise Standards that are identified in the
Enterprise Service Catalog if that Enterprise Standard is needed to support agency or
delivery center business needs. An agency or delivery center is not required to consume the
Enterprise Standard if the service is not needed, however, if future business needs require a
service offering, agencies and delivery centers shall consume the service offering if it is
categorized as an Enterprise Standard. Independent Agencies may be required to consume
Enterprise Standard services that are identified in the Enterprise Service Catalog.

If agency or delivery center business needs necessitate nonconformity of an Enterprise
Standard, they shall obtain an approved IT Policy exception via the process outlined in the IT
Policy Governance Policy, prior to implementing non-Enterprise Standard services. The IT
Policy exception request shall include justification of why the Enterprise Standard does not
meet their business needs.

Any agency or delivery center may present a candidate for a new Enterprise IT Service
Offering to OA/IT Enterprise by following the OA/IT Service Catalog Management Process,

   located at https://itcentral.pa.gov/Pages/Enterprise-Services.aspx (Commonwealth Access

   All requests to add, change, or retire Enterprise IT Service Offerings shall be submitted to the

6. Contact

In the event an agency chooses to seek an exception from this policy, a request for a policy
