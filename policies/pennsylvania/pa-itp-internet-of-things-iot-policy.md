# Internet of Things (IoT) Policy (Commonwealth of Pennsylvania IT Policy: Internet of Things (IoT) Policy)

## At a glance

Information Technology Policy of the Office of Administration for agencies under the Governor's jurisdiction. Printed purpose: This policy establishes requirements for the secure use, deployment, and management of Internet of Things (IoT) devices within the Commonwealth’s information technology environment, ensuring alignment with state and federal cybersecurity standards.

1. Authority
Executive Order 2016-06, Enterprise Information Technology Governance

2. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for the secure use, deployment, and management of Internet
of Things (IoT) devices within the Commonwealth’s information technology environment,
ensuring alignment with state and federal cybersecurity standards.

3. Scope
This policy applies to all offices, departments, boards, commissions, and councils under the
Governor’s jurisdiction and any other entity connecting to the Commonwealth Network
(hereinafter referred to as "agencies").

4. Definitions
Internet of Things: Network of devices that contain the hardware, software, firmware and
actuators which allow the devices to connect, interact and exchange data and information. (e.g.,
sensors, cameras, smart lighting, HVAC controls). IoT also includes devices such as, but not
limited to, postage meters, kiosks, touchless lockers, game consoles, and barcode scanners.
Devices that require connection to, or operation through, a personal computer (PC) are not
considered IoT devices policy.

5. Policy
For definitions of terms found within this document, refer to the IT Policy Glossary.

  5.1 Procurement and Approval
  All IoT device procurement shall comply with the IT Projects and Procurement Policy and IT
  Vendor Risk Management Policy.

Devices must align with NIST SP 800-213 security guidelines and, where applicable, comply
with the IoT Cybersecurity Improvement Act of 2020.

When multiple suitable IoT devices are available, preference shall be given to devices
carrying the U.S. Cyber Trust Mark, which indicates testing against NIST IR 8425 in
accredited laboratories. This requirement does not obligate agencies to conduct multi-vendor
evaluations where none would otherwise be performed; rather, if alternatives are being
considered, the Cyber Trust Mark should be used as a differentiator in the selection process.

Devices from proscribed suppliers per the IT Procurement Intake Standard or that
communicate with nations blocked by the Geo IP Blocking Services as outlined in the
Enterprise Firewall Rule Set Standard are prohibited.

5.2 Inventory and Asset Management
Agencies shall maintain a comprehensive and up-to-date inventory of all agency or vendor
managed IoT Devices providing services to the commonwealth, including:
•   Device Type
•   Manufacturer & Model
•   Physical Location
•   Assigned Owner
•   Mission-Critical Resource Y/N
•   Support Contact Information (agency contact, vendor details, manufacturer escalation
    contacts)

Agencies must provide inventory records to OA/IT upon request.

Agencies shall be responsible for the installation, operational management, and maintenance
of their IoT Devices.

5.3 Network Requirements
IoT Devices shall only be connected to approved networks as determined by the Enterprise
Network and Architecture Engineering team that are physically and/or logically segmented.
Devices deemed to be critical or high-risk may be required to be further segmented from other
IoT Devices at the discretion of the Enterprise Information Security Office (EISO).

IoT devices that require inbound connections are exempt from the requirement to reside on
the IoT Network.

IoT Devices must only have access to systems and data necessary for their function.

Unauthorized devices discovered on the network may be immediately isolated and
investigated.

5.4 Security Requirements
Default usernames and passwords shall be changed before deployments and strong
authentication shall be enforced.

  All IoT device communications must be encrypted in accordance with the Encryption Policy.

  Remote access must be disabled unless explicitly authorized and secured with multi-factor
  authentication (MFA).

  Agencies shall consult their Agency or Delivery Center Information Security Office (ISO) to
  ensure devices adhere to industry standard best practices.

  Third party vendor connections to Commonwealth resources must be reviewed by the Agency
  or Delivery Center ISO.

  Devices must be regularly assessed and patched in accordance with the IT Resources Patching
  Policy.

  5.5 Incident Response
  Any suspected compromise of an IoT device must be reported immediately to the EISO in
  accordance with the IT Security Incident Reporting Policy.

6. Contact
Questions or comments may be directed via email to OA, IT Policy.

7. Exception from Policy
In the event an agency chooses to seek an exception from this policy, a request for a policy
waiver shall be submitted via the enterprise IT policy waiver process. Refer to IT Policy
Governance for guidance.
