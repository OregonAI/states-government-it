# Generative Artificial Intelligence Acceptable Use Policy (WVOT-PO1035)

## At a glance

Acceptable use of generative large language model and AI systems (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Grammarly are named as examples), based on preliminary findings of the AI Task Force. Also linked from the Office's Artificial Intelligence page.

## Full text

Generative Artificial Intelligence Acceptable Use Policy: WVOT-
PO1035 Issued by the Chief Information Officer on 10/07/2026.
OPR: WVOT Operations Division

                        COMPLIANCE WITH THIS POLICY IS MANDATORY
                                      Publications are available online at
                     https://sites.google.com/wv.gov/othub/policy-governance/ot-policies

     Questions and change recommendations related to this publication shall be directed to this publication's
     Office of Primary Responsibility (OPR). This policy shall be reviewed every year to ensure continued
     alignment with current legal, operational, and strategic requirements. Revisions will be undertaken as
     necessary.

     1.    PURPOSE

             This policy establishes and defines the acceptable use of generative Large Language Model
             (LLM) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems, based on preliminary findings from the WV Task
             Force on AI, examples of such systems include ChatGPT, Copilot, Google Gemini, and
             Grammarly. This policy applies exclusively to generative AI systems.

     2.    SCOPE

             2.1.    This policy applies to all departments, including agencies, boards, authorities, and
                     commissions, within the Executive Branch of West Virginia that utilize or have access to
                     state-provided resources.

             2.2.    An agency may impose additional restrictions on employees' use of AI in the workplace.

             2.3.    An agency cannot allow exceptions to this policy unless it receives approval from WVOT.

             2.4.    This policy does not replace or override more restrictive federal, state, and local policies,
                     rules, statutes, or ordinances that apply to state agencies and employees.

     3.    CAPABILITIES OF GENERATIVE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI)

             3.1.    Generative AI and LLMs offer a diverse array of business capabilities, empowering
                     employees to concentrate on larger objectives and creative strategies while utilizing
                     these tools for writing and research support. Examples of such capabilities include:

                    3.1.1.   Research: Investigating various topics and referencing both web-based and
                             physical sources for reliable information.

                    3.1.2.   Text Generation: Producing well-structured, meaningful content, such as
                             articles, stories, or responses tailored to specific contexts.

                    3.1.3.   Image Creation: Designing artistic or realistic visuals based on descriptive text
                             inputs.

4.   POLICY - GUIDELINES FOR GENERATIVE AI

      4.1.    To ensure security and privacy of state information, WVOT must approve any Generative
              AI software used on the state network or state device.

      4.2.    Employees are only allowed to use West Virginia Office of Technology (WVOT)’s
              Approved Generative AI Software.

             4.2.1.     Link to WVOT’s List of Approved Software

             4.2.2.     A removal from the list takes effect upon written notice to agencies, followed by a
                        transition period of thirty (30) days.

      4.3.    The state retains all rights, ownership, and intellectual property to data used through
              Approved Generative AI Software.

      4.4.    When accessing Approved Generative AI Software, employees must use a state device
              and log in with the state email address and user account associated with their employee
              identification number. Subject to the exceptions in Section 2.4 of this Policy, no
              restrictions apply to the types of data used.

              Aside from the exceptions cited in Section 2.4 of this Policy, there are no restrictions on
              the type of data an employee may use in Approved Generative AI Software as long as
              the employee logged into a state device with a state user account associated with their
              employee identification number.

      4.5.    Employees are strictly prohibited from entering non-public or sensitive information into
              unapproved AI tools, search engines, and LLMs. For purposes of this section, non-public

             or sensitive information means data classified above Level 1 – Public under the WVOT

      4.6.   IMPORTANT: The State only controls and protects information used on Approved
             Generative AI Software, when the employee uses their state login. Therefore, any data
             used or obtained through an alternative unapproved Generative AI Software or by an
             employee who has not logged on with their state login is in violation of this policy.

      4.7.   Generative AI is a valuable resource which, when used correctly, can increase efficiency
             and accuracy within state government. Employees are not discouraged from using
             Approved Generative AI Software or LLMs. Employees are, however, responsible for how
             they use the results of Generative AI or LLMs, and must balance such use with careful,
             thoughtful human review.

      4.8.   Employees are prohibited from using Generative AI or LLMs for the following purposes:

                 4.8.1.       Allowing AI to make consequential decisions without human review.

                 4.8.2.       Providing legal or policy advice without proper data validation or
                              consultation. All governmental attorneys must comply with the Legal
                              Ethics Opinion 24-01 on artificial intelligence, as issued by the West
                              Virginia Office of Disciplinary Counsel.

                 4.8.3.       Creating malicious or intentionally misleading content (including but not
                              limited to deepfakes).

                 4.8.4.       Impersonating a government official.

                 4.8.5.       Adopting AI-generated meeting notes or minutes without thorough
                              human review and manual editing. However, an employee can request
                              AI-generated meeting notes for their own use without adopting or
                              reviewing if the meeting notes are not shared with others.

                 4.8.6.       Finalizing any work product of the agency without human review.

5.   RESPONSIBILITIES

      5.1.   Employees must follow all existing policies, including but not limited to, Information
             Security Policy (WVOT-PO1001).

      5.2.   Employees must follow all state Privacy Policies, located here WV State Privacy Office
             Privacy Policies and report all privacy incidents immediately by following the procedures
             found on the State Privacy Office website at WV State Privacy Office Incident Response
             Page and submitting an incident report using the WVOT’s Online Reporting System form.

      5.3.   Agency-level AI restrictions can be more, but not less restrictive than this policy.

      5.4.   All agency AI initiatives, projects, deployments, and acquisitions of new generative AI or
             LLM tools require approval through CIO Review. An individual employee’s use of an
             already-approved tool does not require separate review.
             https://technology.wv.gov/nonav/policies/cio-19-001

      5.5.   Employees are responsible for the accuracy of the final product. They must check
             responses and final products for errors and bias to ensure there are no hallucinations or
             inaccurate statements

      5.6.   Employees are permitted to use AI only in accordance with the WVOT Artificial
             Intelligence Code of Ethics Policy.

      5.7.   Agencies must ensure all employees complete standard training on this policy and
             related WVOT Acceptable Use and Information Security policies, provided centrally
             through WVOT-administered KnowBe4 and LMS modules. It is the agency's responsibility
             to communicate the information in this policy to all personnel by current employee

      5.9.   Appropriate action will be taken against individuals who violate these terms, up to and
             including termination of employment and notifying law enforcement of potential criminal
             behavior.

6.   COMPLIANCE MONITORING AND AUDIT OVERSIGHT

      6.1.   Verification of Compliance: Use of generative AI tools on state resources is subject to
             monitoring and audit by WVOT and individual agencies to ensure adherence to security
             protocols and this policy.

      6.2.   AI prompts, inputs, and chat histories generated through state networks or on state
             devices remain state property and may be accessed by the state for administrative
             review, internal audit, or legal oversight, subject to the retention and exemption provisions
             in Section 7.

      6.3.   Employees are prohibited from using AI Generative Software owned by the state for
             personal gain.

      6.4.   Agency Oversight: Agencies must conduct periodic reviews of AI-assisted work products
             to verify that human oversight and data protection standards are being met.

7.   PUBLIC RECORDS, DELIBERATIVE PROCESS, AND RECORD RETENTION

      7.1.   AI chat histories, search prompts, notes, working session logs, and session outputs are
             designated as Transitory Records with minimal administrative value, regardless of
             whether the employee saves the results for later use.

      7.2.    Because they are transitory records, AI chat histories, search prompts, notes, working
              session logs and session outputs do not constitute state records or public records under
              the Records Management and Preservation of Essential Records Act (W. Va. Code §
              5A-8-1 et seq.) or the West Virginia Freedom of Information Act (W. Va. Code § 29B-1-1
              et seq.), and do not have to be managed, retained, or produced in accordance with state
              records retention schedules, policies, or procedures.

      7.3.    Until an AI output has been reviewed, adopted, and incorporated into a final document by
              a State employee, AI chat histories, prompts, notes and session outputs are
              pre-decisional internal memoranda within the meaning of West Virginia Code
              29B-1-4(a)(8) and are exempt from disclosure on that basis.

      7.4.    However, if the outcome of a generative AI search is used in another document which
              would normally be considered a public record, then the resulting document may be a
              public record, subject to any exemptions set forth in West Virginia Code § 29B-1-4.

      7.5.    The following examples are illustrative, not exhaustive, of when generative AI becomes a
              public record:

             7.5.1.     An employee uses generative AI to conduct research, compile data, or extract
                        information, which the employee later uses to draft an agency report. The
                        research conducted using generative AI is not a public record, but the resulting
                        report is, even if it incorporates portions of the generative AI research.

             7.5.2.     An employee uses generative AI to take notes of a virtual meeting. The
                        employee reviews the results, makes edits and clarifications, and then sends the
                        updated notes to all the participants of the meeting. The original generative AI
                        notes, as retained solely by the employee, are not public records. The resulting
                        document, as shared with others, may be.

             7.5.3.     An employee uses generative AI to create an illustrative graphic. The illustrative
                        graphic is not significantly altered and is shared with the public. The graphic is a
                        public document, even though it was not significantly edited or modified.

8.   PUBLIC DISCLOSURE AND CONTENT INTEGRITY REQUIREMENTS

      8.1.    Public Disclosure, Media Labeling, and Accessibility: Agencies must clearly disclose to
              the public when public-facing content, including images, audio, and video, is created
              using generative AI. Visual and audio media must include a visible watermark or
              prominent label identifying it as AI-generated to prevent misinformation. For these
              purposes, an “image” does not include an illustrative chart, graphic, cartoon, or other item
              which is clearly not a photograph.

      8.2.    Public AI Text & Documents: Written public materials (e.g., press releases, public notices,
              web copy) should be identified as AI-generated–whether by visible watermark or
              text–when the final public document was created by generative AI, and the material
              constitutes a final product that was not subjected to meaningful human input or review.

10.   PROCUREMENT AND VENDOR RISK MANAGEMENT

      10.1.    Vendor Risk Assessment: Prior to the acquisition of any generative AI tool or service, a
               mandatory Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) must be completed. This assessment
               shall determine the system's Risk Tier (1, 2, or 3) as defined in WVOT-PO1034 and
               ensure the vendor complies with state data classification standards and security
               protocols.

      10.2.    Data Hosting and Privacy: Contracts with AI vendors must explicitly prohibit the use of
               state-provided data for training the vendor’s public models and must ensure data is
               hosted in accordance with state security requirements.

      10.3.    Licensing Compliance: Agencies must ensure that AI software licenses explicitly grant
               the state the right to use generated output for government purposes without additional
               royalty or copyright restrictions.

      10.4.    Intellectual Property and Data Ownership: All outputs, including but not limited to text,
               code, images, and data visualizations, generated by the System during the performance
               of state business are "works made for hire" and shall remain the exclusive property of the
               State of West Virginia. Vendors grant the state a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, and
               non-exclusive license to use, modify, and distribute any AI-generated content. Vendors
               are strictly prohibited from using state-provided data, prompts, or outputs to train, refine,
               or improve their public or proprietary models without the express written consent of the
               WVOT.

11.   DEFINITIONS

       11.1.   Approved Generative AI Software: Generative AI tools and LLM applications listed on
               WVOT’s Approved Software Registry that meet state security standards and have been
               formally evaluated, risk-assessed, and authorized by the West Virginia Office of
               Technology (WVOT) for official state use.

       11.2.   AI prompt: The initial text, document, or data payload submitted by a user to an Artificial
               Intelligence interface to generate a response. Prompts entered by state personnel must
               comply with all state data classification, privacy, and ethics guidelines.

       11.3.   Algorithmic Impact Assessment or AIA: A formal evaluation process used to identify and
               mitigate the potential risks of an automated system.

       11.4.   Artificial Intelligence or AI: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines,
               especially computer systems. These processes include learning, reasoning, and
               self-correction.

       11.5.   AI Council: A formal, cross-functional committee within the state government that is
               delegated the authority to establish, enforce, and oversee the AI governance framework.

       11.6.   Application Programming Interface or API: A set of defined rules that enable different
               applications to communicate with each other.

       11.7.   Consequential Decision: A decision or recommendation made with the assistance of AI
               that significantly impacts a citizen’s access to, or the cost of, state-managed benefits, civil
               liberties, or employment opportunities.

       11.8.   Deepfakes: Synthetic media in which a person in an existing image or video is replaced
               with someone else's likeness. Deepfakes are often created using artificial intelligence
               techniques, particularly deep learning.

       11.9.   Generative AI: An umbrella term for artificial intelligence models—including all related
               software, tools, and solutions—designed to create new content such as text, images,
               audio, video, or code. This encompasses standalone computer programs and specific
               applications that assist users in performing creative tasks, as well as comprehensive
               platforms and frameworks utilized to address specific business needs and manage
               enterprise deployments.

      11.10.   Hallucinations: False information or inaccurate data generated and confidently presented
               by generative AI.

      11.11.   Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act or HIPAA: A federal law that
               establishes national standards for the protection of sensitive patient health information (a
               type of PII that includes health status, provision of health care, or payment for health care
               information) by all Covered Entities and their Business Associates. HIPAA mandates the

   11.12.   Large Language Model or LLM: A type of generative AI that is an artificial intelligence
            tool which can recognize and generate text, among other tasks. LLMs are trained on vast
            amounts of text data.

   11.13.   Model Training: The process of feeding data to a machine learning model to enable it to

   11.14.   Office of Primary Responsibility or OPR: The department or individual accountable for a

   11.15.   Personally Identifiable Information or PII: Any information that can be used to distinguish
            or trace an individual’s identity, such as name, social security number, or biometric

   11.16.   Privileged Information: Information that is legally protected from disclosure, such as
            attorney-client privilege or doctor-patient confidentiality.

   11.17.   Public LLMs: Large Language Models that are accessible to the general public, often
            through web interfaces or APIs, for various text-based tasks.

   11.18.   Risk Tiers: A classification framework used to align the risk level of an application with
            the required level of oversight and the necessity of an AIA. Risk tiers range from Tier 1 to
            Tier 3 where Tier 1 is the lowest risk and Tier 3 the highest.

   11.19.   System: A combination of hardware, software, and procedures necessary to support
            data. A server may have multiple systems, and a system may require multiple servers.
