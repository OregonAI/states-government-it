# AI Acceptable Use Policy (DIR AI Acceptable Use Policy, Version 1.0)

## At a glance

DIR's own rules for its employees, contractors, interns and volunteers when they use AI systems (disclosure, attribution, heightened scrutiny uses, prohibited uses, training). It applies to DIR Users only; DIR's AI templates page shares it as an example for other state and local entities to adapt.

Purpose
DIR’s AI Acceptable Use Policy establishes a comprehensive framework for the responsible,
ethical, and secure use of AI systems by DIR Users. This policy is intended to guide DIR Users as
they incorporate AI systems into their work, while safeguarding DIR’s data, ensuring accurate
work product, and promoting the agency’s goal of helping Texas government better serve
Texans. This policy addresses challenges posed by AI, including the potential for AI output
inaccuracies and hallucinations, privacy violations, and data leakage. It provides minimum
requirements for DIR Users to ensure that the inputs and outputs of AI systems used within DIR
align with agency standards and legal requirements.

Scope
This policy applies to all DIR Users, defined as individuals who access or use DIR systems and
who use or access AI systems in the course of their official duties. This includes employees,
contractors, interns, and agency volunteers.

This policy applies to the use of both DIR Integrated AI systems and Publicly Available AI
systems.

   •   DIR Integrated AI Systems are tools that DIR has formally procured, configured, and
       integrated into its internal technology environment. DIR manages Integrated AI Systems

Goal

Methods
To safely and securely implement AI Systems, DIR Users will:

Review and Follow the AI System Code of Ethics
Section 2054.702 of the Texas Government Code requires state agencies to adopt the AI
Systems Code of Ethics, found in Title 1, Section 219.11, Texas Administrative Code. The AI
Systems Code of Ethics identifies key principles that governmental entities and their employees
must consider in their procurement, development, deployment, and use of AI Systems. Those
principles include:

   •   Human Oversight and Control
   •   Fairness
   •   Accuracy
   •   Redress
   •   Transparency
   •   Data Privacy and Security
   •   Accountability and Liability
   •   Evaluation and Documentation

Consistent with this section, DIR Users must review the AI Systems Code of Ethics and align any
development, deployment, and use of AI systems with the principles found within the AI
Systems Code of Ethics. By signing this Policy below, DIR Users certify that they have reviewed
the AI Systems Code of Ethics.

Limit AI Usage to Approved Tools for DIR Purposes
DIR Users may only utilize approved AI systems in the performance of their official work duties.
In accordance with the DIR Employee Handbook, DIR Users may use Publicly Available AI
Systems for DIR-related purposes only if their supervisor or their supervisor’s designee approves
its use for a specified purpose in writing. Employees desiring to use AI systems not already
embedded within an application or product utilized by DIR must complete the AI Access
Request Form. The employee must submit their written request and receive approval to use a
Publicly Available AI System prior to utilizing it.

AI usage is subject to all other DIR policies and professionalism standards. AI prompts and
outputs are public records and may be subject to public disclosure upon request under the
Public Information Act.

All interactions with AI systems may be logged, monitored, and reviewed by authorized DIR
personnel for security, compliance, and auditing purposes. By using AI systems, DIR Users
acknowledge and consent to such monitoring and are responsible for ensuring compliance with
applicable laws, agency policies, and data classification requirements.

Verify Accuracy of AI-Generated Outputs
While AI is a powerful tool to create efficiencies and improve productivity, it is not infallible. AI
systems are improving in their ability to deliver more accurate results, but inaccurate outputs
remain a significant risk when using AI systems. AI tools create responses based on patterns in
data and language models, which means outputs may contain inaccuracies, outdated
information, or misinterpretations.

To ensure the accuracy of AI-generated content, DIR Users must:

   •   Treat AI outputs as a starting point, written by an unskilled intern. Human judgment
       and expertise remain essential to ensuring a quality final work product.

   •   Review and verify all outputs for correctness and completeness. Fact-check any citations
       to ensure reference materials are correctly attributed and up to date.

   •   If output results are not as expected, review the data quality of the input sources.

   •   Cross-check critical details against authoritative sources.

   •   Consider the tone and relevance of the output and whether it reflects the message
       intended.

   •   Seek review of subject matter experts if outputs pertain to specialized knowledge
       beyond your expertise.

DIR Users are responsible for the content they publish, submit, or take action upon,
regardless of whether the content was created with the assistance of AI.

Protect Sensitive and Personal Identifying Information
DIR has a critical responsibility to protect the PII it collects and processes about individuals and
any sensitive or confidential data held or accessed by the agency. Both legal and ethical
restrictions exist on what PII DIR shares with third parties, including with third-party AI
platforms. Sharing PII with a Publicly Available AI System could result in a data breach and result
in legal liability for DIR. To limit the risk of unauthorized disclosure of PII or other sensitive data,
DIR Users should limit AI usage to DIR Integrated Systems, including Microsoft Copilot,
whenever possible.

Publicly Available AI Systems that DIR has not integrated into its secure environment may
transmit data across the public internet, store user inputs and outputs indefinitely, and use
inputs to train the underlying AI model. DIR Users adoption of these tools creates added risk
and must be treated with caution. When using Publicly Available AI Systems, DIR Users must:

    •   Not share or input any confidential, sensitive, or PII data into the AI tools.
    •   Report any data breaches or incidents involving AI systems to their supervisor and the
        DIR Privacy Officer immediately.

Be Transparent About AI Usage
DIR is committed to ensuring that the public, stakeholders, and internal users understand when,
how, and why AI is used. Transparency is critical to ensuring trust and implementing appropriate
levels of review on AI-created content.

Disclose AI Systems that Interact with the Public
Statutorily-mandated disclosure requirements exist for any AI systems DIR deploys that interact
with the public. DIR Users must notify the Office of General Counsel before making any AI
system available to interact with the public and ensure the appropriate disclosures exist
alongside the AI system before public deployment.

Give Attribution When Work Product Relies Significantly on AI-Generated
Content
DIR Users must notify their supervisor when they create content with the assistance of AI tools.
DIR Users must give attribution to an AI System in instances where the AI System significantly
shapes ideas, arguments, or substantive content of a document or other work product. DIR
Users should consult with their managers and the Office of General Counsel to determine
whether and to what extent attribution to an AI System is appropriate in a given context.

Using an AI System for non-substantive writing assistance, such as grammar or spelling
correction, light editing, or minimal structuring of a document, does not require attribution.

Document AI Integration into Standard Workflows
If a DIR User incorporates an AI System into the integral performance of a job task, the DIR User
must document the process for using the AI System to complete the job task and confer with
the DIR User’s supervisor to ensure that appropriate staff are cross-trained on how to use the
tool to complete the task. If a DIR User develops an AI agent or automated process to help
accomplish a job task, the DIR User must review the Citizen Development Policy and ensure
compliance.

Identify Uses of Heightened Scrutiny AI Systems
Subchapter S, Chapter 2054 of the Texas Government Code places requirements on
governmental entities when they develop, deploy, or use heightened scrutiny AI systems,
including conducting written risk assessments and documentation prior to any deployment of
the AI system.

A heightened scrutiny AI system is any AI system specifically intended to autonomously make, or
be a controlling factor in making, a consequential decision. The term does not include AI
systems intended to: (a) perform a narrow procedural task; (b) improve the result of a previously
completed human activity; (c) perform a preparatory task to an assessment relevant to a
consequential decision; or (d) detect decision-making patterns or deviations from previous
decision-making patterns. A consequential decision is one that has a material legal or similarly
significant effect on the provision, denial, or conditions or a person’s access to a government
service.

Whether a given AI tool qualifies as a heightened scrutiny AI system will depend on both the
capabilities of the tool and how DIR Users will utilize it. DIR Users considering deployment or
use of a heightened scrutiny AI system must notify the Chief AI and Innovation Officer and may
not procure, develop, deploy, or use a heightened scrutiny AI system without their approval.

DIR Users should direct any questions about whether a system qualifies as a heightened scrutiny
AI system to the Office of General Counsel.

Avoid Prohibited Uses of AI Systems
DIR Users must not attempt to manipulate AI systems by prompt injection techniques or
bypassing or disabling AI system safeguards, filters or restrictions through any other means.

In accordance with state and federal law, the following AI uses are prohibited:

   1. Using AI to generate or disseminate misleading or deceptive content.
   2. Using AI for social scoring that could result in unfavorable treatment or infringement of
      an individual’s legal rights.

Complete AI Training and Awareness
Section 2054.5191 of the Government Code requires state employees to complete an annual
artificial intelligence training program certified by DIR. DIR will provide the annual training and
may provide additional periodic training and briefing related to the deployment of specific AI
Systems or other obligations that DIR Users have related to AI Systems usage. DIR Users must
complete the training within the time specified by DIR.

Definitions
Artificial Intelligence System: A machine-based system that for explicit or implicit objectives
infers from provided information a method to generate outputs, such as predictions, content,
recommendations, or decisions, to influence a physical or virtual environment with varying levels
of autonomy and adaptiveness after deployment.

Consequential Decision: A decision that has a material legal or similarly significant effect on
the provision, denial, or conditions or a person’s access to a government service.

Controlling Factor: A factor that is (a) the principal basis for making a consequential decision;
or (b) capable of altering the outcome of a consequential decision.

DIR Integrated AI Systems: AI-enabled systems that DIR has formally procured, configured,
and integrated into its internal technology environment. DIR manages Integrated AI Systems
under its IT security protocols, data remains within DIR-approved infrastructure, and users log-in
with agency credentials. Examples include Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Chat as well as AI
tools incorporated into a larger system formally adopted by DIR and operating within DIR’s
secure environment.

DIR Users: All individuals using or accessing DIR systems who use AI systems in the course of
their official duties. This includes employees, contractors, interns, and agency volunteers.

Heightened Scrutiny Artificial Intelligence System: An AI system specifically intended to
autonomously make, or be a controlling factor in making, a consequential decision. The term
does not include AI systems intended to: (a) perform a narrow procedural task; (b) improve the
result of a previously completed human activity; (c) perform a preparatory task to an assessment
relevant to a consequential decision; or (d) detect decision-making patterns or deviations from
previous decision-making patterns.

Personal Identifying Information: Information that alone or in conjunction with other
information identifies an individual.

Publicly Available AI Systems: Commercial, web-based AI systems that are accessible over the
internet and operate on shared infrastructure not controlled by DIR. Publicly Available AI
Systems operating outside of DIR’s secure environment and pose greater risks for data handling
and privacy.
