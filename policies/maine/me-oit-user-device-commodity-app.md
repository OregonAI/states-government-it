# User Device and Commodity Application Policy (MaineIT User Device and Commodity Application Policy)

## At a glance

Maine State information and communication technology exists exclusively for official State business: the directives on user devices and on the commodity applications allowed on or consumed from them.

## Full text

                               Maine State Government
                 Department of Administrative and Financial Services
                       Office of Information Technology (OIT)

                User Device and Commodity Application Policy

1.0.   Statement
       Maine State information and communication technology exists exclusively for
       official Maine State business. The purpose of this Policy is to ensure that Maine State
       information and communication technology is best positioned to facilitate official
       Maine State business, while complying with relevant Federal and State laws, as well
       as general industry best practices. This policy also contains directives regarding
       allowable applications, both resident on the device, as well as consumed remotely.

2.0.   Definitions
2.1.   Adware: An application that is specifically designed to display advertising content
       without the consent of the user.

2.2.   Commodity Application: Commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) computer program, either
       consumed from the cloud or installed on a client device, that was not created or
       configured for any State of Maine-specific utility.

2.3.   Commodity Cloud Application: An application that is consumed from the Internet,
       exclusively on the basis of an (most likely, non-negotiable) End-User License
       Agreement (EULA) or Terms of Service, i.e., without a dedicated and/or negotiated
       purchasing contract. Could be either free, or against a usage fee.

2.4.   Commodity Platform: A unified collection of applications that provides broad
       functionality and allows significant configuration and customization flexibility.
       Examples include Salesforce, Workday, and Microsoft Dynamics, among others.

2.5.   Extension: An add-on that runs within an existing OIT-approved browser or
       application and enhances its features without requiring a separate operating system
       level installation. Extensions consist of packaged configurations rather than
       standalone executables.

2.6.   Executable: A standalone software file that can be launched or installed by the
       operating system, creating independent functionality outside the scope of an
       existing approved application.

2.7.   Hypervisor: An application, firmware, or hardware that allows multiple virtual
       machines to run on a single physical machine. Also commonly known as a virtual
       machine monitor (VMM) or virtualizer.

2.8.   Malware: An application specifically designed to disrupt, damage, or gain
       unauthorized access to an electronic device.

2.9.   Messaging App: An app resident on a mobile device that provides the user interface
       for sending and receiving messages. At a minimum, such apps support the baseline
       Short Message Service (SMS) features. The two most popular default Messaging
       Apps are Google Messages 1 for Android devices, and Apple Messages 2 for iOS
       devices. These default Messaging Apps do not offer encryption.

2.10. Mobile Devices: Computing and/or communication devices, running a mobile
      operating system (such as Google Android and Apple iOS), as opposed to a
      workstation operating system (such as Microsoft Windows, Mac OS, Ubuntu, etc.).
      Includes, but is not limited to, smartphones and tablets.

2.11. Open-Source Asset: An application which is created using source code that anyone
      can inspect, modify, and enhance.

2.12. Self-Service Platform (SSP): An application platform that allows for rapid, visual
      development of applications and/or workflows with considerably-less code
      compared to traditional coding languages. SSPs are geared toward shared
      development between business users and IT personnel. Examples: Microsoft Power
      Apps, Salesforce, Chatbot, etc.

2.13. Short Message Service (SMS): The technology protocol for exchanging short text
      content through mobile devices. The universally supported baseline by mobile
      carriers and mobile device manufacturers is an upper limit of 160 characters, and no
      encryption, either at-rest, or in-flight.

2.14. Terms & Conditions (End User License Agreement, Terms of Service): General and
      special arrangements, provisions, requirements, rules, specifications, and standards
      that form an integral part of an agreement or contract. For the purposes of this
      Policy, Terms & Conditions, End User License Agreement (EULA), and Terms of
      Service are used interchangeably.

2.15. User Device: Integrated computing and communication device, intended for end-
      users. Includes workstations, laptops, notebooks, tablets, smartphones, personal
      digital assistants, etc. For this Policy, divided into two categories: State-issued, and
      other devices used to conduct State business.

3.0.   Applicability
3.1.   This Policy applies to:
       3.1.1. Maine State Executive Branch personnel, both employees and contractors;
       3.1.2. User devices using the State network, irrespective of whether they are State-
              issued or located within other State government branches, or other devices
              used to conduct State business on the State network; and
       3.1.3. Applications, both resident on the device, as well as consumed remotely.

4.0.   Responsibilities
4.1.   Agency Management:
       4.1.1. Ensure that their personnel are aware of, and compliant with, this Policy.
       4.1.2. The Agency Management responsibility is especially relevant for commodity
              cloud applications (see Definitions) that are chosen by Agency Business
              personnel.
       4.1.3. Collaborate with OIT in owning, interpreting, executing, and enforcing this
              Policy.

4.2.   OIT Architecture and Policy:
       4.2.1. Facilitates the thorough examination of new technology or application
              solutioning requests.

4.3.   OIT Information Security Office:
       4.3.1. Collaborates with Agency Management and OIT Architecture and Policy to
              ensure all IT commodity cloud applications have been thoroughly vetted for
              security posture and State risk.

4.4.   OIT Management:
       4.2.1. Collaborate with Agency Management in owning, interpreting, executing, and
              enforcing this Policy.
       4.2.2. The OIT Account Managers are liaisons with the Agency business partners.

4.3.   DAFS IT Procurement Office:
       4.3.1. Collaborates with Agency Management and OIT Architecture and Policy to
              ensure all IT commodity cloud application Terms & Conditions have been
              thoroughly vetted.

5.0.   Directives
5.1.   Any personal use of a State-issued user device (see Definitions) must be incidental
       in nature and must not interfere with official Maine State business.

5.2.   Access to State of Maine information assets is only allowed from a State-issued or
       State-managed user device, approved multi-factor authentication (MFA) option, and
       approved virtual private network (VPN), if applicable. The use of any personal
       peripheral device(s) (keyboard, mouse, headphones, monitor, etc.) is allowed ONLY
       in support of remote telework agreements for State of Maine personnel. Use of
       personal peripheral device(s) is NOT allowed in State of Maine offices. More

5.3.       All State-issued devices are configured according to industry best practices, based
           upon a combination of standards bodies (such as CIS Benchmarks, IRS Benchmarks,
           etc.), advice of trusted vendors (such as Microsoft, Oracle, etc.), and federal
           compliance requirements.

5.4.       Any State-issued user device may be monitored and audited for many purposes,
           including protecting against unauthorized usage, and ensuring the security and
           optimal functioning of the Maine State network.

5.5.       The operating system of a State-issued workstation is a currently supported
           Microsoft Windows.

5.6.       Certain classes of applications are prohibited from use on State-issued user devices.
           At a minimum, such classes include, but are not limited to, Anonymizers, Peer-to-
           Peer Utilities, Unauthorized Remote-control Agents, Hypervisors, and Unauthorized
           Cloud Transfer and Storage applications.
           5.6.1. Use of Windows Update Delivery Optimization (WUDO) 4 on State-issued user
                  devices is permitted.
           5.6.2. Effective March 31, 2025 Inbound Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) to any
                  State of Maine workstation is prohibited.

5.7.       If net new features of a Commodity Application are integrated into the core
           functionality of an already vetted Commodity Platform at no additional cost, and are
           provided under the State of Maine’s existing contractual agreement with the
           platform vendor, then the application is approved for use without additional vetting.
           5.7.1. Example: When Loop was introduced as part of the Office 365 GCC platform,
                   it was enabled as an extension of the existing platform. By contrast, any
                   Commodity Application that is offered through a Commodity Platform
                   marketplace and is not core or integrated into the platform, regardless of
                   licensing or pricing considerations, must be reviewed and vetted by
                   Enterprise Architecture (See section 5.21) prior to use.

5.8.       Any unauthorized use of a State-issued user device may result in discipline, up to
           and including, dismissal.

5.9.    Unless instructed to do so in the performance of official duties, State-issued user
        devices must not be used to create, record, store, copy, transmit, distribute, image,
        modify, print, download, or display inappropriate or unprofessional materials that
        demean, denigrate, or harass individuals, or groups of individuals, on the basis of
        race, ethnic heritage, religious beliefs, disability, sexual orientation, or gender,
        regardless of whether it was intended to demean, denigrate, or harass any employee
        or group of employees. This prohibition applies regardless of whether the personnel
        is on-duty or off-duty.

5.10. Unless instructed to do so in the performance of official duties, State-issued user
      devices must not be used to create, record, store, copy, transmit, distribute, image,
      modify, print, download, or display materials that involve nudity, are sexually
      explicit, or pornographic in nature. This prohibition applies regardless of whether
      the personnel is on-duty or off-duty.

5.11. State personnel shall not use any State-issued user device for any commercial,
      outside business, or for-profit activity.

5.12. No State business may be conducted through non-Maine.gov account(s). This
      prohibition applies irrespective of whether the device is State-issued, or otherwise.

5.13. Unencrypted SMS is not suitable for Sensitive (TLP: Amber), (TLP: Amber+Strict), or
      Restricted (TLP: Red) content. Unencrypted SMS is only suitable for transactional
      communication, such as setting up, and/or confirming, appointments, etc. Under
      certain circumstances, just name and address may be considered Public (TLP:
      White) data, unless overridden by Federal and/or State laws, statutes, rules, and
      regulations. All SMS content is subject to the Maine State Archives records
      management policy and retention schedules, 5 and the Maine Freedom of Access Act
      (FOAA). 6 Usage of any special Messaging App (i.e., a Messaging App that is not
      natively bundled with the device operating system) for state business is subject to
      the appropriate OIT vetting (Item 5.19 of this policy). Details on data classification
      are defined in the Data Classification Policy. 7

5.14. A State-issued user device is intended for the exclusive use of designated State
      personnel. Any use of the State-issued user device by family or friends of the
      designated State personnel is strictly prohibited.

5.15. A State-issued user device must be returned unconditionally upon the request of the
      issuing Agency. Typical circumstances include, but are not limited to, termination of
      employment, conclusion of telework authorization, etc. Violation of this provision
      may result in referral to law enforcement.

5.16. Only applications that satisfy all of these criteria may be installed on State-issued
      user devices:
      5.16.1. Agency agreement on business-relevance;
      5.16.2. Certified safe by OIT from a cybersecurity standpoint;
      5.16.3. Certified for usage by DAFS IT Procurement (who also verifies with the
              Attorney General’s Office, as needed);
      5.16.4. Proof-of-purchase, or appropriate free-use license;
      5.16.5. Availability of the native operating system installer; and
      5.16.6. Availability of the license key.

5.17. Should there arise any difference of opinion regarding the business-relevance of an
      application, the OIT Management will conduct a review with Agency Management. If
      Agency Management agrees on the business-relevance of that application, then that
      application will be allowed, contingent on fulfillment of the other criteria.

5.18. All applications installed on State-issued user devices must be kept current under
      the support regime of the original vendor.

5.19. All risk associated with using an end-user application is explicitly borne by the user,
      and the sponsoring agency.

5.20. This item is about the use of commodity cloud applications for State business and
      applies irrespective of the user device.
      5.20.1. Commodity cloud applications that purport to be “free” to individual
              consumers may not actually be free to Government consumers. Also, many
              such applications follow the so-called “freemium” model, where a basic
              version is free, but as the user gets used to the application, certain additional
              features are charged a fee. Another variant is to make the application free
              until an arbitrary usage threshold is reached, beyond which a fee is triggered.
              Either way, any application used for State business must always be compliant
              with its licensing terms, including all applicable usage fees.

       5.20.2. Security is foundational to everything else. A commodity cloud application
               may be a carrier of adware (See Definitions) or malware (see Definitions). If
               security controls of the commodity cloud application do not meet the
               minimum requirements of the data being transacted by the application, then
               such an application cannot be used for State business. The OIT Chief
               Information Security Officer (CISO) remains the final arbiter on the
               suitability of a commodity cloud application for State business.

       5.20.3. It is critical to thoroughly review the Terms & Conditions (see Definitions),
               and all IT applications must be vetted through the DAFS IT Procurement
               Office before accepting them.
               5.20.3.1. The boilerplate Limitation of Liability, Indemnification (State held
                           harmless), and Legal Jurisdiction for dispute resolution are more
                           likely to be in favor of the vendor, as opposed to the State. In most

5.22. This item relates to the use of extensions (see Definitions) for State business and
      applies irrespective of the user device.
      5.22.1. Whenever an agency/user would like to add an extension to an OIT-
              approved product, they must either:
              5.22.1.1. Send an email to the Enterprise Architecture inbox:
                        (Enterprise.Architect@Maine.Gov); or
              5.22.1.2. Submit a request to the OIT Enterprise Ticketing System, assigned
                        to MaineIT New Tech Requests.

      5.22.2. The email or Ticketing Request must include the business justification
              describing why the extension is necessary for business purposes. The
              supervisor must be copied on the email to ensure they have approved the
              request.

      5.22.3. OIT reserves the right to immediately deny the request based on obvious
              weaknesses or the determination that the extension is not necessary for
              business use, in which case the rejection will be communicated to the
              requestor within five (5) business days.

      5.22.4. Provided the core product remains fully patched and up to date, any
              extension that does not require a separate operating system installation
              receives provisional approval from the Enterprise Architecture Team.
              However, depending on how the extension interacts with compensating
              controls on OIT-managed devices, the prevailing industry guidance, and the

5.23. For any open-source asset (see Definitions), the quality, reliability, privacy, and
      security of the application is contingent upon the pace and thoroughness with which
      the open-source community responds to issues as they arise, as opposed to a
      corporate service level agreement. These products may also be subject to Terms &
      Conditions, license restrictions, usage limitations, data handling obligations, or
      other legal, financial, operational, privacy, or security risks. This presents an
      intrinsic risk which any user/agency of open-source products must assume.
      5.23.1. Should the use of such unapproved Freeware or Open source software (OSS)
               be discovered by OIT, the agency associated with the user and/or the device
               may be subject to fines and penalties, as a result of the costs incurred to
               mitigate any damage to the network that may be caused by these products.
      5.23.2. Should a vulnerability with respect to an installed, approved open-source
               product come to the attention of OIT, OIT reserves the right to uninstall,
               through the RFC process through emergency or normal changes and/or
               otherwise remediate that product, and/or mandate an alternative product.

5.24. OIT endorses the adoption of a Self-Service Platform (SSP) (See Definitions) by
      agency (i.e., non-OIT) personnel, if both of the below conditions are satisfied:
      5.25. The SSP supports the productivity of an individual, and/or the agency
            workgroup to which this individual belongs; and
      5.26. The transacted data is classified at either TLP: Green or TLP: Clear (See the
            Data Classification Policy 12).
      5.27. Agency personnel are not authorized to adopt SSPs unless both of the above
            conditions are simultaneously satisfied.
