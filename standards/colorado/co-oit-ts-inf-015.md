1. Purpose
The purpose of an Enterprise Two-factor Authentication (2FA) Standard is to enable strong
authentication of users with access to sensitive information and information systems
resources, or who have a privileged level of support access, while ensuring ease of use and
user adoption. Adoption of this standard will reduce unauthorized access and demonstrate
compliance with federal and industry mandates, and will enable solicitation, assessment, and
selection of the 2FA solution(s) that implement these requirements.

2. Scope
These standards apply to and shall be used by employees of OIT, agencies supported by OIT,
and vendors contracted by OIT and its stakeholders, as a means to enforce technology
standardization and assist in making the usage, support, and/or purchase of technologies
more consistent and efficient. This applies to any OIT unit or contracted entity providing
these services to customers.

3. Defined Terms
   ●      MFA: A security process requiring 2+ credentials — such as a password, phone code, or
          fingerprint — to verify identity, protecting against unauthorized access even if one
          factor is compromised.
   ●      Approved Authentication Methods: Methods approved by security to be utilized for
          MFA.
   ●      PING: OIT standard authentication tool.

   ●   SAML: An open-standard, XML-based protocol for exchanging authentication and
       authorization data between an Identity Provider (IdP) and a Service Provider (SP).
   ●   OAuth: An open-standard protocol for token-based authorization that allows users to
       grant third-party applications limited access to their resources without sharing their
       passwords.
   ●   TOTP: A secure, temporary 6-8 digit code generated by an authenticator app that
       changes every 30-60 seconds.
   ●   OpenID Connect (OIDC): A modern, open-standard authentication protocol built on top
       of the OAuth 2.0 framework.
   ●   Privileged Account: An account with elevated permissions beyond a standard user,
       including administrative access to systems, databases, or network configurations.

4. Policy
Authentication must be derived from at least two of the following three categories:
   ●  Knowledge: Something you know, such as a PIN or password.
   ●  Possession: Something you have, such as a FIDO2 security key, smartphone, or TOTP
      app.
   ● Inherence: Something you are, such as fingerprint or facial recognition.
Authentication must meet requirements set by TS-CISO-001 Authentication & Account
Management Standard v. 2026.

5. IT Security Planning
Enterprise PingID 2FA SSP.

6. Managing and Mitigating IT Risk
Business System Owner
The Business System Owner has sufficient knowledge of the system to provide additional
information or points of contact regarding the security plan and the system, as needed, and is
the decision-making authority for the budgetary and operational function of the system or
solution.
System Subject Matter Expert/Administrator
The Subject Matter Expert (SME) is the individual responsible for the overall business
management and administration of the system, involved in all operational discussions for the
system (user access control, documentation, applications, data, design, and disaster recovery
requirements), and is the primary business contact for all security events affecting the
system.
Authorizing Security Official
The Colorado Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), or delegate, after review of each
System Security Plan (SSP), is the individual responsible for sponsoring and approving the
operation, or denying operation, of state computing systems and solutions for the State of
Colorado.

9. Compliance
Failure of users to comply with this standard may result in, as applicable:
   ●   Loss of federal grant funding, fines, and penalties.
   ●   Agency-level sanctions regarding state IT resources: restriction, deactivation, or
       suspension of the user's account, project, or system, or termination of employment.
   ● Termination of contract or assignment for temporary workers, interns, volunteers,
       contractors, or vendors who violate this policy.
Non-consolidated agencies must have appropriate policies and procedures to ensure
compliance with this policy and all other Colorado Information Security Policies.

10. Exceptions
Any exceptions to this policy must be documented, justified, and approved in advance of
implementation. Requestors must submit a Secure Configuration Exception Request.
