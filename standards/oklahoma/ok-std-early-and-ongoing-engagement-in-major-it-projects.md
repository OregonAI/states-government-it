## Full text

                  Early and Ongoing Engagement in Major IT Projects Standard

Introduction
State agencies subject to the Chief Information Officer (CIO) and the Information Services Division
(ISD) authority are required to coordinate with OMES Information Services at the earliest stage of
any major information technology project. Early and ongoing engagement ensures alignment with
statewide standards, enterprise architecture, cybersecurity requirements, accessibility and
procurement practices. It reduces risk, duplication and cost across the enterprise and improves
outcomes for the citizens of Oklahoma.

Purpose
This standard establishes a mandatory early and ongoing engagement and intake process with
OMES Information Services for major IT projects. It defines what constitutes a major IT project, sets
minimum engagement activities and identifies responsibilities and process steps for agencies and
OMES Information Services throughout concept and planning phases.

Definitions
Major IT project – an initiative that introduces or significantly changes statewide or agency
technology capabilities. Examples include enterprise system replacements, statewide platform
implementations, cross-agency integrations, significant cloud migrations, significant cybersecurity
initiatives or projects that materially affect mission-critical services or regulated data.

Early engagement – the required coordination with OMES Information Services beginning at
concept and prior to developing detailed scope, budget, schedule or solicitation materials. This
engagement should continue through the scope of the project unless Information Services
provides an exemption.

Project Initiation Request – a standardized intake package submitted to OMES Information Services
including a concise concept overview, preliminary objectives, stakeholders, anticipated funding
source, rough order of magnitude, technology impacts, data classification and initial risk
considerations.

Agency – any state agency subject to CIO and ISD authority under Oklahoma law.

Standard
Triggers for mandatory early engagement.
Agencies must initiate early engagement with OMES Information Services when any of the following
triggers apply. These triggers help agencies self‑identify “major IT projects” and ensure alignment
and risk management at concept.
     • Total anticipated project value is $500,000 or greater.
     • The project will access, store, host, copy, transmit or otherwise process regulated or sensitive
        data (e.g., HIPAA PHI, CJIS CJI/OLETS, IRS Publication 1075 FTI, FERPA‑protected data, or other
        federally protected data).
     • Introduction of significant new cloud hosting/services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS) or material changes to
        existing cloud platforms, including migration of agency systems or data.

    •   Statewide or cross‑agency impact (shared platforms, enterprise system replacement,
        multi‑agency integrations) or any initiative that materially affects mission‑critical services.
    •   Agency office moves to new locations.

Guidelines.
   • Early engagement is mandatory. Agencies must engage OMES Information Services during
       concept development and before discussions with suppliers. If a solicitation is required,
       engagement is required during concept and before drafting the solicitation.
   • Project intake submission is required. Agencies must submit the intake package through the
       Project Initiation Request as an IT project accessible here: ServiceNow Project Initiation Request.
   • Architecture, security and accessibility reviews will be coordinated in partnership with OMES
       Information Services and documented with guidance and required actions.
   • Cybersecurity and data protection requirements must be addressed early. Projects must align to
       the State of Oklahoma Information Security Policy, Procedures and Guidelines and related
       standards.
   • Compliance with statewide IT standards is required. Projects must follow published
       methodologies, templates and quality practices, including project management standards.
   • Exception requests must be in writing and approved by the CIO or by their delegate(s). Agencies
       seeking exceptions to standards must include justification.
   • Documentation must be maintained. Agencies must retain engagement records including intake
       submission, reviews, decisions and exception approvals in project files.

Process Overview.
   1. Agency identifies the IT concept and contacts OMES Information Services via ServiceNow and
       submits the Project Initiation Request for an IT project.
   2. OMES Information Services acknowledges receipt and schedules a discovery session.
   3. Architecture, security and accessibility reviews are performed; guidance and required actions
       are documented.
   4. Ongoing coordination plan and sourcing strategy are established.
   5. Exception requests, if any, are evaluated and documented.
   6. Agency proceeds to planning and, if applicable, solicitation in alignment with the documented
       guidance. Agency continues to engage with OMES Information Services per the established plan
       and strategy unless an exception is provided.

Note
All IT purchases require CIO approval at a zero-dollar threshold. Engagement with ISD does not
replace ePro or other required approvals; it ensures that architecture, security, accessibility,
procurement, and other evaluations occur before scope, budget or solicitation language is
finalized.

Compliance
This standard shall take effect upon publication and is made pursuant to Title 62 O.S. §§ 34.11.1
and 34.12 and Title 62 O.S. § 35.8. OMES IS may amend and publish the amended standards
policies and standards at any time. Compliance is expected with all published policies and
standards, and any published amendments thereof. Employees found in violation of this standard
may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination..

Rationale
To ensure efficient, secure, and cost-effective delivery of essential public services, all state agency
IT purchases and projects must receive central approval. This allows the Chief Information Officer
to evaluate agency needs and capabilities, strengthen data protection and security, and streamline
and consolidate systems to reduce costs for taxpayers.
