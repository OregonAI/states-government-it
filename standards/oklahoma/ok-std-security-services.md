# Security Services Standard (OMES Security Services Standard)

## At a glance

A CIO standard in the 'Security' group of the OMES statewide IT policy and standards library, binding on state agencies under the Information Technology Consolidation and Coordination Act. Its Purpose or Introduction, as printed: This document defines the authority and services provided by Oklahoma Cyber Command.

## Full text

                                 Security Services Standard

Introduction
Oklahoma Cyber Command is responsible for protecting state users and their devices, networks,
data and applications. Oklahoma Cyber Command’s top priority is safeguarding the state’s data
and computer infrastructure against unauthorized data use, disclosure, modification, damage and
loss. The OMES IS division supports Cyber Command’s vision to provide leadership in the
development, delivery and maintenance of cybersecurity, information security, risk management,
enterprise fraud, physical security systems, compliance and privacy programs.

Purpose
This document defines the authority and services provided by Oklahoma Cyber Command.

Standard
Oklahoma Cyber Command provides security services to all state agencies, boards and
commissions of the State of Oklahoma as defined in Title 62 O.S. §§ 34.11.1. These services
include but are not limited to the following:
    • Defensive services:
           o Endpoint management.
           o Virtual Desktop Infrastructure (VDI).
           o Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR).
           o Endpoint encryption.
           o Security assessment.
           o Secure Mail Gateway (SMG).
           o Secure Web Gateway (SWG).
           o Virtual Private Network (VPN).
           o Intrusion Prevention/Detection Systems (IPS/IDS).
           o Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
           o Privilege Access Management (PAM).
    • Security education (in coordination with OMES Training):
           o Security Education and Awareness Training (SEAT).
           o Simulated phishing campaigns.
    • Offensive services:
           o Access control.
           o Threat intelligence collection, analysis, exploitation and production.
           o Forensics.
           o Investigations.
           o Threat assessments.
           o Threat monitoring and analysis.
           o Security Information and Event Management (SIEM).
           o Incident response.
           o Security assessment.
           o Facility Access Management Systems (FAMS).
           o Surveillance systems (CCTV).
           o Physical Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS).

           o Facility project support.
           o Fraud prevention, detection, and investigation.
           o Third-party risk management.
           o Information sharing and analysis.
   •   Risk and compliance audit services.
           o Fingerprinting.
           o Contract compliance reviews.
           o IS risk assessments.

This list is not comprehensive and is constantly evolving with technology. All requests relating to
physical or cyber security must be reviewed by OMES Cyber Command. State agencies or
employees may submit a service request for Cyber Command assistance through the Service
Portal requesting cyber or physical security solutions not included on this list, as needed.

It is the responsibility of Oklahoma Cyber Command to interpret, design, implement and manage
required regulatory controls. Staff performing similar functions within state agencies must
collaborate and coordinate all activities with Oklahoma Cyber Command and defer interpretation
of regulations and final decisions/solutions to Oklahoma Cyber Command.

Through the Oklahoma Information Sharing and Analysis Center (OK-ISAC), Oklahoma Cyber
Command maintains relationships and facilitates information sharing between regulatory bodies,
including federal partners, industry oversight bodies and state/local law enforcement agencies to
monitor cyber trends and help reduce the risk of cyber threats

Compliance
This standard shall take effect upon publication and is made pursuant to Title 62 O.S. §§ 34.11.1
and 34.12 and Title 62 O.S. § 35.8. OMES IS may amend and publish the amended standards
policies and standards at any time. Compliance is expected with all published policies and
standards, and any published amendments thereof. Employees found in violation of this standard
may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Rationale
To ensure efficient, secure, and cost-effective delivery of essential public services, all state agency
IT purchases and projects must receive central approval. This allows the Chief Information Officer
to evaluate agency needs and capabilities, strengthen data protection and security, and streamline
and consolidate systems to reduce costs for taxpayers.
