1. PURPOSE
This standard establishes the statewide service capability, Data Center, as an enterprise
service under EA-02 – Establishing an Enterprise Service Policy. A Washington state agency
must not adopt, build, expand, or renew a service with similar capabilities unless it has an
approved waiver or exemption (see Section 5).

2. SCOPE
     1. The Data Center must be used to house physical IT infrastructure, including but not
        limited to servers, storage systems, and network equipment.
     2. This standard does not limit state agencies from hosting systems with third-party,
        commercial cloud service providers.

3. APPLICABILITY
     The scope of this standard applies to all state agencies (as defined in RCW 43.105.020).

4. ENTERPRISE SERVICE CAPABILITIES
     The Data Center enterprise service designation encompasses the following service
     capabilities:
     1. Basic data center service: Provides core infrastructure and facility support for hosting,
        operating, and maintaining IT systems, including:
              a. IT equipment enclosures: Houses and protects servers and networking
                 hardware in secure, organized racks or cabinets.
              b. Power and cooling: Supplies reliable electrical power and maintains optimal
                 environmental conditions to ensure continuous IT system operation.
              c. Fire system: Detects and suppresses fires using integrated detection, alarm,
                 and extinguishing technologies to protect equipment and personnel.

  2. Physical security: Ensures controlled access and protection of the facility through
     24/7/365 surveillance, access controls, and monitoring systems.
  3. Shipping and receiving: Manages the secure intake and dispatch of IT equipment and
     materials entering or leaving the data center.
  4. Industry Tier 3 standards: 99.982% uptime and capable of meeting third-party
     regulatory compliance requirements.

5. WAIVERS AND EXEMPTIONS TO THIS STANDARD
  In accordance with POL-01-02-S Technology Policy and Standard Waiver Request
  Standard, agencies may qualify for a waiver or exemption from compliance with this
  standard. Agencies may request an exemption for situations that meet one of the
  following criteria:
     1. Temporary IT equipment that is associated with a natural disaster, emergency
        response, or other unplanned events in cases where the Data Center Service is
        otherwise unavailable for use.
     2. IT equipment required to support Mission-Critical or Business-Essential field
        operations and is one or more of the following types:
            a. Consumer electronics devices, such as personal computers, printers,
               mobile devices, or multifunctional output devices.
            b. Internet of things devices, such as sensors, trackers, smart devices,
               wearables, appliances, IP cameras, lights, TVs, conferencing systems,
               environmental monitoring systems, etc.
            c. Building management systems devices, such as heating, ventilation, and
               air conditioning (HVAC) systems; elevators; parking; video surveillance;
               security systems; etc.
            d. Access control system readers and sensors, such as radio-frequency
               identification (RFID) badges and biometric scanners.
            e. Tactile human interface equipment, such as lab equipment, clinical
               equipment, forensics, etc.
            f. Transportation Systems Management Operations and mobility system
               sensors and devices, such as field deployed sensors, devices, and control
               equipment including roadside traffic operations, public transit vehicles,
               unmanned vehicles, land, water, and aircraft trackers, ferry vessels, mobile
               command centers, mobile disaster recovery platforms when such
               components require local or mobile operation.
            g. Systems required by the Maritime Transportation Security Act (MTSA) and
               or United States Coast Guard (USCG) regulations to remain operational
               during loss of commercial power or wide area network connectivity, to the
               extent that such requirements necessitate local or on-site hosting.
            h. Site-dependent systems, communications equipment such as radio,
               satellite, microwave receivers and transmitters. Server deployments that
               operate locally and aggregate to a primary server in the SDC or QDC.
               Secondary AD Domain Controllers in locations other than the data centers.

            i. Manufacturing and industrial system sensors, trackers, and devices, such
               as smart agriculture, robotics, machine controls systems, and supervisory
               control and data acquisition (SCADA) systems.
            j. Locally operated equipment rooms that only support local office building
               operations, print rooms, file share, on-site client configurations, network
               and telecommunication equipment.
            k. Systems with airgaps (physically not connected to state government
               networks), such as building management systems or building physical
               security systems.
