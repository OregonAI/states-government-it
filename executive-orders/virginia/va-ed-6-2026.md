# Directing a Commonwealth-Wide Preparedness Approach to Cyber Threats Posed by Artificial Intelligence and Recursive Self-Improvement Development (Executive Directive 6 (2026))

## At a glance

Listed on the Governor's Executive Actions page as ED-6, October 23, 2026 (an executive directive, filed here as an executive order). Directs the Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security to establish a Cyber Consortium and a Commonwealth-wide cyber-attack prevention and response plan that addresses AI-related risks, in coordination with the AI Task Force and the CIO at VITA, and directs other executive branch agencies to cooperate.

## Full text

                             EXECUTIVE DIRECTIVE 6 (2026)
    DIRECTING A COMMONWEALTH-WIDE PREPAREDNESS APPROACH TO
   CYBER THREATS POSED BY ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND RECURSIVE
                  SELF-IMPROVEMENT DEVELOPMENT

       In Executive Order No. 19 (2026), I directed the Secretary of Public Safety and
Homeland Security to establish the Virginia Unified Readiness Framework as the
Commonwealth’s enterprise-wide governance model for all-hazards preparedness and resilience.
The hazards covered by Executive Order No. 19 include cybersecurity threats, cyber incidents,
and threats to critical infrastructure.
       In Executive Order No. 22 (2026), I directed the formation of the rapid-response AI Task
Force (“AI Task Force”) to address the unprecedented risks facing the Commonwealth from
unregulated artificial intelligence (“AI”) development. The AI Task Force, in coordination with
the Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security, has identified substantial and growing
cybersecurity risks from AI development, including biorisk and physical infrastructure risks to
the Commonwealth’s critical systems, and determined that immediate action must be taken to
ensure robust processes are in place to protect all Virginians.
        Accordingly, by virtue of the authority vested in me as Governor under Article V of the
Constitution of Virginia and §§ 2.2-222.1 and 44-146.16 to 44-146.18 of the Code of Virginia, I
hereby direct the preparation of a coordinated cyber threat response and infrastructure-hardening
plan for the Commonwealth under the Unified Readiness Framework that accounts for the risks
and mitigates the hazards posed by existing AI models, and the prospect of threat amplification
from recursive self-improvement AI development. As part of the Unified Readiness preparedness
process, the AI Task Force shall seek input and participation from the AI frontier model
developers and other public and private sector participants in order to access and learn from
information that has been or may be shared with other governmental actors about the risks posed
by existing AI models and new models under development, as well as recursive self-
improvement AI development, and to otherwise identify recommended best practices for
guarding against AI-based cyber threats.
Importance of the Issue

        The acceleration of AI development presents significant, unprecedented risks as reflected
by the calls from major AI technology companies to slow down the pace of development.
Virginia’s Unified Readiness Framework exists to address dynamic and complex threats that
demand a modern integrated approach to preparedness. Preparing now for constantly evolving
AI risks is critical to the safety and security of Virginia and its residents. The Commonwealth

expects that leading AI technology companies—who rely on Virginia-based compute, provide
their services to Virginians, and are directly soliciting government engagement—will collaborate
to explain the current and foreseeable issues and risks with today’s publicly available models as
well as internal models, and work in partnership on preparedness to protect critical systems and
infrastructure on which everyone in the Commonwealth relies.
       A leading cause for concern today is recursive self-improvement AI development, which
generally refers to how frontier AI models can improve themselves and build the next version of
the AI model. There are different levels of recursive self-improvement AI development, ranging
from a person guiding an existing AI model on the development of a future, higher capacity AI
model to an AI model working fully autonomously to build and train a future AI model. All
versions of recursive self-improvement AI development, however, result in the faster creation of
newer, more intelligent AI models.
        Although AI development has the potential for societal benefits, those benefits cannot
come at the expense of responsible growth and development that ensures the safety and security
of people. Chief Executive Officers at frontier AI technology companies have made clear that
there are significant safety and security risks associated with models being tested today that they
project will worsen with recursive self-improvement AI development. Virginia must take the
concerns of the businesses leading these development processes seriously.
Directive
       Pursuant to the authority vested in me as the Chief Executive Officer of the
Commonwealth, I hereby direct the Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security and the
AI Task Force take the following immediate steps to address the cybersecurity risks associated
with existing AI models and recursive self-improvement AI development:
       1.      The Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security shall establish a Cyber
               Consortium and integrate AI-related cybersecurity as part of the Unified
               Readiness Framework to ensure the Commonwealth is prepared in the event of an
               AI-related cyber-attack.

                   a. The Cyber Consortium, as part of its integration into the Unified
                      Readiness Framework, shall adopt a Commonwealth-wide cyber-attack
                      prevention and response plan as well as an infrastructure-hardening plan
                      that addresses AI-related risks and other identified cyber risks. The
                      Secretary shall review and incorporate any existing plans, projects, or
                      assessments that bolster preparedness, where appropriate. The Secretary
                      may, in his discretion and in coordination with the AI Task Force, direct
                      the Cyber Consortium to conduct an independent cyber readiness review.

                   b. The Secretary shall directly coordinate with all agencies within the
                      Secretariat, including but not limited to the Virginia State Police and the
                      Virginia Department of Emergency Management, and engage with the AI
                      Task Force and the Chief Information Officer at the Virginia Information
                      Technology Agency. All other executive branch agencies shall cooperate
                      with the Secretary upon request.

        c. The Secretary, in consultation with relevant Secretariats, shall designate
           executive branch agencies who will participate in the Cyber Consortium
           on an active and ongoing basis as part of the Unified Readiness
           Framework.

2.   The AI Task Force shall engage with the leading AI technology companies,
     researchers, other government officials and regulators—including international
     experts and authorities—and private sector leaders to seek their ongoing
     participation and engagement with the Cyber Consortium and in the development
     of the Unified Readiness Framework and response plans as it relates to existing
     AI-related risks and future risks attributable to ongoing AI model development
     including hacks, biorisk, physical infrastructure risk, and recursive self-
     improvement development.

        a. Where applicable, AI technology companies shall be specifically
           requested to provide confidential briefings and reports that are submitted
           to other government actors—including New York’s Department of
           Financial Services under the RAISE Act and the European AI Office
           under the General-Purpose AI Code of Conduct—to the Cyber
           Consortium and other approved participants.

        b. In partnership with the participating AI technology companies and the AI
           Task Force, the Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security shall
           endeavor to hold Unified Readiness briefings on AI-related risks at least
           quarterly due to the rapid advancement of this general-purpose
           technology. Such briefings shall include a tabletop exercise at least
           annually.

        c. The AI Task Force shall ensure deployment of an independent privacy and
           security review team to identify and address any potential data security
           vulnerabilities across Virginia’s interoperable data systems—including
           their associated data architecture and data governance processes—to
           improve system security while enhancing function

3.   The AI Task Force, in coordination with the Secretary of Public Safety and
     Homeland Security, shall engage with the public institutions of higher education
     to leverage their expertise to assess AI cyber-risk preparedness and recursive self-
     improvement AI development.

4.   The AI Task Force and Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security shall
     engage with other states and invite them to participate in the Unified Readiness
     Framework and preparedness briefings on a bipartisan basis. Participating states
     will be required to maintain the confidentiality of any highly sensitive information
     obtained through the Cyber Consortium or under the Unified Readiness
     Framework on the same terms as the Commonwealth.

       5.      The Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security shall assess any and all
               public-private information sharing conduits and portals related to AI risk and
               recursive self-improvement AI development. The Secretary shall, in coordination
               with the AI Task Force, seek to participate in appropriate public-private
               information sharing.

Effective Date of the Executive Directive

        This Executive Directive shall become effective upon its signing and shall remain in full
force and effect unless amended or rescinded by further executive order or directive.
       Given under my hand and under the Seal of the Commonwealth of Virginia this 23rd day
of October 2026.

                                                       Abigail D. Spanberger, Governor

Candi Mundon King, Secretary of the Commonwealth
