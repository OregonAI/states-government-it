# Establishing Utah’s Pro-Human Approach to Artificial Intelligence in State Government (Executive Order 2026-08)

## At a glance

Governor's order of 6 October 2026 on state government use of AI: Pro-Human AI principles that agencies must apply, the Department of Government Operations' lead on AI-driven process improvement and technical safeguards against cyberattack and unauthorized AI agents, the State-Endorsed Digital Identity framework, agency duties (AI literacy training, transparency about AI use in processes that affect the public), and DTS duties (training strategy, uniform AI implementation standards). Effective until modified, amended, rescinded or superseded.

## Full text

                                  EXECUTIVE ORDER
                                       2026-08

    Establishing Utah’s Pro-Human Approach to Artificial Intelligence in State Government

WHEREAS, Utah recognizes that artificial intelligence (AI) represents a generational
opportunity to expand capabilities and opportunities for the residents of Utah;

WHEREAS, Utahns have expressed legitimate concerns regarding the potential impacts of AI
use in government;

WHEREAS, the choice between innovation and safety is a false one, and state government can
use AI in an innovative way while doing so with appropriate safeguards;

WHEREAS, AI should strengthen human judgment, creativity, and responsibility rather than
replace them, ensuring technology remains accountable to the people it serves;
WHEREAS, in December 2025, I established the Pro-Human AI Initiative to ensure
developments in AI technology remain human-guided and human-enhancing, ensuring
individuals stay in control of the AI tools that impact their work and lives;

WHEREAS, Utah is committed to responsible, pro-human AI implementation within state
government, exemplifying how AI can be integrated both ethically and effectively;

WHEREAS, the State values its workforce and is committed to developing its employees as AI
technology is adopted;

WHEREAS, a structured, ongoing approach to workforce AI literacy is essential to equipping
state employees to safely interact with, evaluate, and responsibly deploy these technologies;

WHEREAS, the rapid advancement of AI requires trustworthy, privacy-preserving methods for
verifying the authenticity of individuals, organizations, AI agents, and digital information that
enable Utahns to confidently distinguish genuine interactions from synthetic or fraudulent ones
while ensuring individual control over personal information; and
WHEREAS, Utah has established the State-Endorsed Digital Identity (SEDI) Framework to
provide privacy-preserving, individually controlled, cryptographically verifiable digital identity
and credentials that strengthen trust while preventing identity fraud, impersonation, unauthorized
AI agent activity, and fraudulent digital content;

WHEREAS, Utah is engaged in ongoing statewide efforts to identify and implement AI-driven
process improvements that increase efficiency, improve quality, foster transparency, and enhance
decision making for the benefit of Utahns, while adhering to the foundational principles of
Utah’s Pro-Human AI Initiative; and
WHEREAS, Utah will become the nation's leading example of how AI can expand human
potential while preserving individual liberty, privacy, and democratic governance;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Spencer J. Cox, Governor of the State of Utah, by the authority vested
in me by the Constitution and laws of this state, hereby order the following:

   1) Purpose. The purpose of this executive order is to:
         a) Promote responsible AI use within government by utilizing Utah’s Pro-Human AI
            Initiative principles to evaluate AI tools;
         b) Designate procedures for the Department of Government Operations to lead the
            state in the use of AI-driven process improvements, choosing tools that adhere to
            Pro-Human AI principles,
         c) Invest in the state workforce by providing training and upskilling opportunities
            that equip employees to grow alongside AI, prioritizing the development of state
            employees;
         d) Provide a framework for agencies to improve services through the deployment of
            citizen-facing tools, while establishing guardrails and technical safeguards to
            protect state data; and
         e) Establish agency responsibilities to adhere to Pro-Human AI principles, maintain
            transparency, complete AI literacy training, and evaluate opportunities to
            responsibly optimize processes using AI technology.

   2) Definitions. As used in this order:
         a) “Agency”
                i) includes:
                        (1) A department, division, office, bureau, or other organization within
                            the state executive branch, including the State Tax Commission,
                            the National Guard, and the Board of Pardons and Parole; and
               ii) does not include:
                        (1) an institution of higher education;
                        (2) the Utah Board of Higher Education;
                        (3) the State Board of Education;
                        (4) an independent entity as defined in Utah Code § 63E-1-102;
                        (5) the Attorney General's Office;
                        (6) the State Auditor's Office;
                        (7) the State Treasurer's Office;
                        (8) the Legislative Branch; or
                        (9) the Judicial Branch.

   3) Foundational Principles. The use of AI technologies within the state government is to
      be pro-human. Agencies shall avoid AI technologies that do not adhere strictly to the
      foundational principles of Utah's Pro-Human AI Initiative:

              Human-Guided: AI systems should protect human dignity, preserve human
              agency, and ensure individuals remain in control of the tools that shape their work
              and lives. AI should be understandable in how it functions, accountable in its
              impact, and adaptable to human needs.

           Human-Enhancing: AI systems should amplify human potential by focusing on
           creating a value-based ecosystem that is pioneering in its vision, empowering in
           its outcome, and connective in how it brings people and ideas together.

4) The Department of Government Operations (DGO) Responsibilities. The Department
   of Government Operations (DGO) shall lead statewide efforts in AI-driven process
   improvements that increase efficiency, improve quality, and enhance decision-making for
   the benefit of Utahns in adherence to the foundational principles of Utah’s Pro-Human AI
   Initiative. DGO shall:
       a) Establish guardrails and technical safeguards to protect State information
            technology assets, systems, and data from cyberattacks, malicious actors, and
            unauthorized or rogue AI agents, and provide Agencies with the guidance,
            resources, and technical support necessary to implement equivalent protections.
       b) Assist agencies in:
               i) Identifying areas where AI could be deployed responsibly to reduce wait
                    times, expand public services access, and improve operational outcomes
                    while ensuring human-in-the-loop review for determinations affecting
                    individual rights.
              ii) Evaluating AI tools that can improve operational outcomes for Utahns
                    while adhering to foundational principles of pro-human AI use.
       c) Develop and utilize the SEDI Framework, where applicable, as the State's
            strategic trust infrastructure for AI by enabling cryptographically verifiable
            identities, credentials, and data that protect privacy while preventing identity
            fraud, impersonation, unauthorized AI agent activity, and fraudulent digital
            content.

5) Agency Responsibilities. In coordination with the Division of Technology Services
   (DTS), each Agency, in adherence with Pro-Human AI principles, shall:
      a) Ensure that all applicable Agency employees complete AI literacy training
          prescribed by DTS and the Division of Human Resource Management (DHRM).
      b) Evaluate opportunities for optimizing and transforming state government business
          functions through the use of AI technology in a cost-effective manner.
      c) Actively investigate and propose areas where citizen-facing government processes
          and automated workflows can responsibly be optimized or transformed using AI
          technology.
      d) Maintain transparency regarding its use of AI technology by documenting how AI
          systems, including their inputs, outputs, and roles in decision-making, are
          integrated into Agency processes that impact the public.
      e) Collaborate with other agencies to share resources, best practices, and findings.

6) The Division of Technology Services (DTS) Responsibilities. DTS shall:
      a) In coordination with DHRM, and in adherence with Pro-Human AI principles,
         establish and implement a comprehensive training strategy for AI literacy for
         employees in all Agencies, which must include:
            i) Foundational, general AI concepts;
           ii) Practical, applicable, and beneficial uses of approved state AI tools; and
          iii) Ethical and pro-human principles governing AI use.
      b) Develop specialized training on AI systems analysis for designated Agency leads
         who are accountable for evaluating and executing AI-assisted process
         improvements and ensuring that tools are deployed using Pro-Human principles.
      c) Evaluate scalable training platforms and content providers, prioritizing Utah-
         based entities, to optimize the delivery of AI literacy training.
      d) Coordinate the development and publication of uniform AI technology
         implementation standards for state government.

          e) Evaluate next-generation AI solutions to elevate the State's ability to detect and
             protect against cybersecurity threats at scale.

   7) Applicability and Reporting
        a) The Commissioner of DGO shall provide a progress report to the Governor by
            July 1, 2027, and each July 1st thereafter.
        b) Nothing in this Order shall be construed to limit or alter an Agency's obligations
            under existing state or federal law, including the Government Records Access and
            Management Act (GRAMA), the Government Data Privacy Act (GDPA), or
            applicable federal privacy and civil rights law.

THIS ORDER is effective immediately and shall remain in effect until otherwise modified,
amended, rescinded, or superseded.

                                            IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my
                                            hand and caused to be affixed the Great Seal of the
                                            State of Utah. Done on this, the 6th Day of October,
                                            2026.

                                            Spencer J. Cox
                                            Governor

ATTEST:

                                            Deidre M. Henderson
                                            Lieutenant Governor
